🧊 Why This Matters

Where this shows up

Every time you stack building blocks, pack boxes into a shipping container, or build in Minecraft, you're solving a 3D counting problem: how many unit pieces make up a shape, and what's hidden inside it that you can't directly see? Architects use exactly this thinking to work out how many bricks a wall needs; warehouse packers use it to fit boxes into a truck.

Why it's an ASMOPSS favourite

Competition papers love 3D cube problems because they test something you can't just memorise: can you picture a 3D shape from a flat description, and count it systematically without losing track? This topic has appeared on the real exam multiple years running — it's one of the most reliable "if it's on the paper, you should be ready" topics.

Today's 3 moves: (1) Count cubes in a solid block or stack. (2) Work out how many MORE cubes are needed to complete a shape. (3) Work out how many faces get painted on each cube after the outside of a block is dipped in paint.

🗺️ The Big Picture

1Solid rectangular block? Multiply length × width × height. Done.
2Uneven stack (staircase, footprint of columns)? Add up every column's height. That total IS the cube count.
3"How many MORE to finish it" question? Work out the FULL finished shape's total, then subtract what's already there.
4Painted-cube question? Every unit cube is a CORNER (3 faces), an EDGE (2 faces), a FACE-centre (1 face), or fully INSIDE (0 faces) — depending on how many of its 3 directions touch the outside of the block.

⚠️ All four moves share one habit: never try to "just picture it" and guess a number. Build the count systematically, column by column or layer by layer, the same way you already do for listing and counting problems.

Move 1 — Counting Cubes In A Solid Block

Total cubes = length × width × height

A rectangular block is built from unit cubes: 3 cubes long, 2 cubes wide, and 4 cubes tall. How many unit cubes make up the whole block?

Think of it as 4 identical flat layers stacked up, each layer 3×2:
One layer = 3 × 2 = 6 cubes
There are 4 layers (the height), so multiply by 4:
6 × 4 = 24
The block is made of 24 unit cubes

⚠️ It doesn't matter which two dimensions you call the "layer" — 3×2×4 gives the same answer as 2×4×3. Multiply all three numbers together, in any order.

📋 Build The Block Yourself

A block is 5 cubes long, 4 cubes wide, and 2 cubes tall. Tap each button in order.

One layer = 5 × 4 = 20 cubes
Height = 2, so there are 2 layers
20 × 2 = 40 unit cubes total

Move 2 — Counting Cubes In An Uneven Stack

Total cubes = the sum of every column's height

Every column is its own separate mini-tower. Add every column's height:
1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10
The staircase is made of 10 unit cubes

Move 3 — How Many MORE Cubes To Finish It?

Cubes still needed = FULL finished total − cubes already there

Mia is building a 3×3×3 cube (3 long, 3 wide, 3 tall). So far, the 9 columns (3×3 footprint) have these heights — Row 1: 1, 2, 1. Row 2: 2, 3, 2. Row 3: 1, 2, 1. How many more unit cubes does Mia need to completely fill the 3×3×3 cube?

Step 1 — work out the FULL finished total (Move 1: a solid 3×3×3 block):
3 × 3 × 3 = 27
Step 2 — work out how many cubes are already there (Move 2: add every column):
Row 1: 1+2+1=4. Row 2: 2+3+2=7. Row 3: 1+2+1=4. Total = 4+7+4 = 15
Step 3 — subtract:
27 − 15 = 12
Mia needs 12 more unit cubes

⚠️ This move is really just Move 1 and Move 2 used TOGETHER, then a subtraction — nothing new to learn, just combine the two skills you already have.

📋 Work Out How Many More

A finished block will be 2×3×4 (2 long, 3 wide, 4 tall). So far the 6 columns (2×3 footprint) have heights: Row 1: 2, 3, 1. Row 2: 4, 2, 3. Tap each button in order.

2 × 3 × 4 = 24
Row 1: 2+3+1=6. Row 2: 4+2+3=9. Total = 6+9 = 15
24 − 15 = 9 more unit cubes needed
⭐ Your Turn — Move 3: Filling

Practice — Full Total Minus What's There

P1.  A finished block will be 2×2×5. So far the 4 columns have heights: Row 1: 3, 2. Row 2: 4, 3. How many more unit cubes are needed?
Full total: 2×2×5=20. There now: 3+2+4+3=12.
20 − 12 = 8 more cubes
P2.  A finished block will be 3×2×4. So far the 6 columns have heights: Row 1: 2, 3. Row 2: 4, 1. Row 3: 3, 2. How many more unit cubes are needed?
Full total: 3×2×4=24. There now: 2+3+4+1+3+2=15.
24 − 15 = 9 more cubes

Move 4 — Painted Cube Puzzles

The setup

A solid rectangular block of unit cubes gets its WHOLE outside dipped in paint — every face you can see from outside, including the bottom. Then the block is taken apart into its separate unit cubes. Some cubes will have paint on several faces; some might have none at all!

Every cube's painted-face count depends on how many of its 3 directions (length, width, height) touch the OUTSIDE of the block
3CORNER cube — touches the outside in all 3 directions → 3 faces painted. There are always exactly 8 corners (if every side is 2 or more cubes).
2EDGE cube — touches the outside in 2 directions, sits between two corners → 2 faces painted.
1FACE cube — touches the outside in only 1 direction, sits in the middle of a flat face → 1 face painted.
0INSIDE cube — touches the outside in 0 directions, fully buried inside the block → 0 faces painted (only exists if every side is 3 or more cubes).

Move 4 — Worked Example

A block is built 2 cubes long, 2 cubes wide, and 2 cubes tall. The whole outside (including the bottom) is dipped in paint, then the block is taken apart. How many unit cubes have paint on EXACTLY 3 faces?

In a 2×2×2 block, every single position is at an END in every direction (there's no "middle" position when a side is only 2 long) — so EVERY cube is a corner cube.
Total cubes in the block: 2 × 2 × 2 = 8
All 8 cubes have exactly 3 faces painted

Now a bigger block: 4 cubes long, 3 cubes wide, 2 cubes tall. This time, how many cubes have EXACTLY 1 face painted (a "face" cube)?

A face cube needs to be in the MIDDLE (not at either end) in exactly ONE direction, and at an end in the other two. The height is only 2, so there's no "middle" for height — every cube touches top or bottom. So face cubes can only come from being in the middle of the LENGTH or the middle of the WIDTH:
Middle-of-length positions: 4−2 = 2  |  Middle-of-width positions: 3−2 = 1
Face cubes from the two long flat faces (top & bottom, each 4×3): the middle strip is 2 long × 1 wide = 2 cubes, × 2 faces (top+bottom) = 4
4 unit cubes have exactly 1 face painted

⚠️ Don't panic if this feels tricky — on the test, you'll mostly need CORNER counts (always 8, if every side ≥2) and INSIDE counts (the easiest to calculate — see the next page). Edge and face counts follow the same "middle vs end, in each direction" logic.

Move 4 — The Easiest One: Fully Hidden Cubes

Hidden (0-face) cubes = (length − 2) × (width − 2) × (height − 2)

Why: a cube is fully hidden only if it's in the middle in ALL 3 directions — not touching either end of the length, the width, OR the height. "Shave off" one layer from each end of each direction, and count what's left.

Worked example: a block 5 long, 4 wide, 3 tall — how many cubes have NO paint at all?

(5−2) × (4−2) × (3−2) = 3 × 2 × 1
= 6 unit cubes are fully hidden (0 faces painted)

⚠️ If ANY side is 1 or 2 cubes, there's no room for a fully hidden middle in that direction — the hidden count is 0. That's exactly what happened with the 2×2×2 block on the last page.

📋 Find The Hidden Cubes Yourself

A block is 6 long, 5 wide, 4 tall, fully painted on the outside. Tap each button in order to find the fully-hidden (0-face) cubes.

6 − 2 = 4
5 − 2 = 3
4 − 2 = 2
4 × 3 × 2 = 24 fully hidden unit cubes
⭐ Your Turn — Move 4: Painted Cubes

Practice — Corners & Hidden Cubes

P1.  A block 3 long, 3 wide, 3 tall is fully painted (incl. bottom), then separated. How many unit cubes have exactly 3 faces painted?
Every block has exactly 8 corners (as long as every side ≥ 2) = 8
P2.  A block 6 long, 4 wide, 3 tall is fully painted (incl. bottom), then separated. How many unit cubes have NO paint at all?
(6−2) × (4−2) × (3−2) = 4 × 2 × 1
= 8 fully hidden cubes

🗺️ The Full Checklist

1Solid block: length × width × height.
2Uneven stack / footprint: add up every column's height, row by row.
3"How many more to finish": full finished total (Move 1) minus what's already there (Move 2).
4Painted cubes: corners = 3 faces (always 8, if every side ≥2). Fully hidden = 0 faces = (l−2)×(w−2)×(h−2). Edge = 2 faces, face-centre = 1 face — found by checking "middle vs end" in each direction.

⚠️ Every one of these moves uses the SAME systematic-counting habit you already trust from Art of Counting: never guess a total from a picture — build it up piece by piece, direction by direction.

📖 Today's Big Idea

3D shapes are just 2D counting stacked up. Multiply for solid blocks, add columns for uneven stacks, subtract for "how many more," and check middle-vs-end in each direction for painted cubes.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You multiply length × width × height for a solid block, without needing to draw every cube
  • You add every column's height, row by row, for an uneven stack or footprint
  • You find "how many more cubes needed" by finding the full total first, then subtracting what's there
  • You know corners are always 8 (if every side ≥ 2), and hidden cubes = (l−2)×(w−2)×(h−2)

This topic has shown up on real ASMOPSS papers again and again — you're ready for it now.
