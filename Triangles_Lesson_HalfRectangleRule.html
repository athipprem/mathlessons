🎯 Why Learn This?

Triangles are everywhere

A sailboat's sail, a slice of pizza, a roof truss, a warning road sign, a triangular garden bed — all triangles. If you know how much MATERIAL or SPACE a triangle takes up (its area), you can figure out how much fabric to buy for a sail, how much paint a roof needs, or how much soil fills a garden bed.

Your new superpower

You already know how to find the area of a rectangle (length × width). Today you'll learn a simple trick: every triangle is exactly HALF of a rectangle — once you see that, finding a triangle's area is easy.

A triangle's area is always HALF of a matching rectangle's area.

📐 Base and Height

Two words you need first

Every triangle has a base (any one side, usually drawn flat on the bottom) and a height (the STRAIGHT UP-AND-DOWN distance from the base to the opposite corner — mathematicians call this "perpendicular," which just means it makes a perfect right angle (📐) with the base).

The little square = a right angle. Height meets the base at exactly 90°.

⚠️ The height is NOT the slanted side of the triangle. It's the straight up-and-down (perpendicular) distance — imagine dropping a plumb line from the top corner straight down to the base.

The Half-Rectangle Rule

Take any rectangle. Draw ONE diagonal line corner to corner. You get TWO identical triangles — each one is exactly half the rectangle.

The shaded triangle is exactly HALF of the whole rectangle.
1Rectangle area = base × height
2Triangle = HALF the rectangle, so…
Area of a Triangle = ½ × base × height

Worked Example — Right Triangle

A right triangle has base = 8 cm and height = 6 cm. What is its area?

Step 1 — write the rule:
Area = ½ × base × height
Step 2 — put in the numbers:
Area = ½ × 8 × 6
Step 3 — multiply base × height first:
8 × 6 = 48
Step 4 — take half:
48 ÷ 2 = 24
Area = 24 cm²

⚠️ The unit is always SQUARE units (cm², m²) because area measures flat SPACE, not a length. Never forget the little "²".

Worked Example — Acute Triangle (Height Falls INSIDE)

An acute triangle (all angles less than 90°) has base = 10 cm and height = 7 cm. Here, the height line drops straight down from the top corner and lands INSIDE the base.

The dashed line is the height — it doesn't have to be a SIDE of the triangle, just a straight perpendicular drop to the base.
Area = ½ × base × height
Area = ½ × 10 × 7
10 × 7 = 70
70 ÷ 2 = 35
Area = 35 cm²

Worked Example — Obtuse Triangle (Height Falls OUTSIDE)

This obtuse triangle has base = 6 cm and height = 5 cm (the height line, dashed, lands outside the triangle on the extended base). The RULE doesn't change at all.

Area = ½ × base × height
Area = ½ × 6 × 5
6 × 5 = 30
30 ÷ 2 = 15
Area = 15 cm²

⚠️ Same rule, every single time: Area = ½ × base × height. It doesn't matter if the triangle is right, acute, or obtuse — or which way the height line has to be drawn to make it perpendicular.

½ × 20 × 15 = ½ × 300
= 150 cm²
📖 Today's Big Idea

Area of a Triangle = ½ × base × height. The height is always the PERPENDICULAR distance from the base to the opposite corner — it can fall inside the triangle (acute), be a side (right), or fall outside on the extended base (obtuse). The rule never changes.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You know Area = ½ × base × height, every single time
  • You can correctly spot the height even when it's NOT a side of the triangle
  • You know the height falls outside the triangle for obtuse triangles — and that's still fine
  • You always write your final answer in SQUARE units (cm², m²)
  • You always finish by checking: did I answer the EXACT question asked?
