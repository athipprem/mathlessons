🎯 Why Learn This?

Shorthand for repeated multiplying

A square garden with side 5 m has an area of 5 × 5 m² — mathematicians write that as 5² instead of writing "× 5" over and over. Exponents show up everywhere: squared areas, cubed volumes, doubling patterns, and LOTS of competition questions.

Your new superpower

In Lesson 1 you learned Brackets → Multiply/Divide → Add/Subtract. Today you add TWO new pieces to that same ladder: Exponents (which slot in right after brackets), and brackets inside brackets — for when a question is extra tricky.

An exponent is just a fast way to write repeated multiplying.

🧮 What Is An Exponent?

The one new idea

An exponent has two parts: the base (the number being multiplied) and the small raised number, the exponent (how many times to multiply the base by itself).

3² — base 3, exponent 2  →  3 × 3 = 9
2³ — base 2, exponent 3  →  2 × 2 × 2 = 8
4² — base 4, exponent 2  →  4 × 4 = 16

⚠️ A very common mistake: 3² is NOT 3 × 2 = 6. The exponent tells you how many 3's to multiply together, not what to multiply 3 by. 3² = 3 × 3 = 9.

The Full Order Ladder

Here's the complete ladder from Lesson 1, now with Exponents filled in:

1Brackets ( ) — always done first, no matter what's inside.
2Exponents (powers, like 3²) — done next, BEFORE Multiply/Divide.
3Multiply × and Divide ÷ — left to right.
4Add + and Subtract − — left to right.
Brackets → Exponents → Multiply/Divide (left→right) → Add/Subtract (left→right)

Worked Example — Exponents Before Multiply

5 × 3² = ?

Step 1 — no brackets. Step 2 — Exponents come before Multiply, so do 3² first:
3² = 3 × 3 = 9
Step 3 — the expression is now:
5 × 9
5 × 3² = 45

⚠️ If you multiplied 5 × 3 FIRST and then squared the result — (5×3)² = 15² = 225 — you'd get a totally different, WRONG answer. Only the number touching the small exponent gets squared, not everything before it.

Worked Example — Brackets Inside Brackets

3 × (4 + (6 − 2)) = ? When brackets are nested (one inside another), always solve the INNERMOST pair first — the one with no other brackets inside it.

Step 1a — find the INNERMOST brackets: (6 − 2). Solve that first:
(6 − 2) = 4
Step 1b — the expression is now, with only ONE bracket layer left:
3 × (4 + 4)
Step 1c — solve that remaining bracket:
(4 + 4) = 8
Step 2 — now it's just Multiply, no brackets left:
3 × (4 + (6 − 2)) = 24

⚠️ Work from the INSIDE OUT, like peeling an onion — innermost brackets first, then the next layer out, and so on, until there are no brackets left.

Practice — Exponents & Nested Brackets

P1.  7 + 2³ = ?
2³ = 2×2×2 = 8 (exponent before add)
7 + 8 = 15
P2.  (9 − 4)² = ?
Brackets first: (9−4)=5
5² = 5×5 = 25
P3.  2 × (5 + 3²) = ?
Inside the brackets, exponent before add: 3²=9, so (5+9)=14
2 × 14 = 28
P4 (Word Problem).  A square garden bed has side (2 + 4) m. What is its area in square metres?
Side = (2+4) = 6 m. Area = side²
6² = 36 m²
📖 Today's Big Idea

The full ladder is Brackets → Exponents → Multiply/Divide (left to right) → Add/Subtract (left to right). When brackets are nested, always solve the INNERMOST pair first and work outward, like peeling an onion.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You know an exponent means "multiply the base by itself that many times" — not "multiply by the exponent"
  • You solve exponents BEFORE multiply/divide, but AFTER brackets
  • You find the INNERMOST bracket pair first when brackets are nested, then work outward
  • You can combine ALL FOUR ladder stages in one expression without getting lost
  • You always finish by checking: did I answer the EXACT question asked?
