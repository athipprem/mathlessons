Trigger words: path around, frame, border, carpet leaving a gap.

The ONE Question That Decides Everything

Every frame question has two rectangles, one inside the other. Before touching any numbers, ask: is the border OUTSIDE the shape, or INSIDE it?

➕ Border OUTSIDE (path around a pool)

OUTER = pool + path
POOL
(given size)

The GIVEN shape is the small one.
Outer length = given + 2×width
Outer width  = given + 2×width

➖ Border INSIDE (carpet in a room)

ROOM = given size
CARPET
(find this)

The GIVEN shape is the big one.
Inner length = given − 2×width
Inner width  = given − 2×width

🔑 Why 2× and not 1×?

The border sits on BOTH sides of the shape — left AND right, top AND bottom. A 3 m border changes each dimension by 6 m, never 3.

❌ The M2 mistake: the question said the frame was around the OUTSIDE, but the working ADDED when it should have compared correctly — direction flipped. The nested diagram makes the direction impossible to flip: draw it first, every time.

Worked Examples — All Three Flavours

Example 1  Photo 20×14 cm, frame 2 cm wide around it. Frame area?

Draw: photo INSIDE, frame OUTSIDE → ADD direction← diagram first!
Outer: (20+4) × (14+4) = 24 × 18 = 432← +4 because 2 cm on BOTH sides
Photo: 20 × 14 = 280
Frame = 432 − 280 = 152 cm²← big minus small

Example 2  Room 32×26 m, carpet leaves a 2 m border inside. Carpet area?

Draw: room is the BIG one, carpet INSIDE → SUBTRACT direction
Carpet: (32−4) × (26−4) = 28 × 22 = 616 m²← −4 because 2 m on BOTH sides

Example 3  Garden 25×15 m, path 2 m around outside, ฿120 per m². Cost?

Outer: (25+4) × (15+4) = 29 × 19 = 551
Garden: 25 × 15 = 375
Path = 551 − 375 = 176 m²
Cost = 176 × 120 = ฿21,120← area FIRST, money LAST

Practice

Draw the nested diagram FIRST. Decide: outside (+) or inside (−)? Then big − small.

Q1.   A picture is 16 cm × 10 cm. A frame 2 cm wide goes around the outside. What is the area of the frame only?
Outside → ADD: outer = (16+4)×(10+4) = 20×14 = 280
Picture = 160. Frame = 280 − 160 = 120 cm²
Q2.   A pond is 18 m × 10 m. A path 3 m wide is built all the way around the outside. What is the area of the path?
Outer = (18+6)×(10+6) = 24×16 = 384← 3 m on both sides = +6
Pond = 180. Path = 384 − 180 = 204 m²
Q3.   A hall floor is 30 m × 24 m. A mat covers the middle, leaving a bare border 2 m wide all around inside. What is the area of the mat?
Border INSIDE → SUBTRACT: mat = (30−4)×(24−4)
= 26 × 20 = 520 m²← given shape was the BIG one
Q4.   A vegetable patch is 22 m × 14 m. A gravel path 1 m wide goes around the outside. Gravel costs ฿200 per square metre. What does the path cost?
Outer = (22+2)×(14+2) = 24×16 = 384
Patch = 308. Path = 384 − 308 = 76 m²
Cost = 76 × 200 = ฿15,200
📖 What You Learned Today
Draw first:Two nested rectangles, before any numbers. Always. Outside border:Given shape is SMALL → outer = given + 2×width each way. Inside border:Given shape is BIG → inner = given − 2×width each way. Always 2×:The border is on BOTH sides of every dimension. Frame area:BIG area − small area. Then cost = area × price.

🏆 You are ready when...

  • You draw the nested diagram before calculating — every single time
  • You can say OUT LOUD whether the given shape is the big one or the small one
  • You never add or subtract just 1× the border width
