⚡ The One Rule

You already know how to solve "choose 2 from n people" going forward: n × (n−1) ÷ 2. This is the reverse trick — when you're given the TOTAL number of handshakes/pairs/matches and asked to find n.

Double the total first. Then find two consecutive numbers that multiply to it. The bigger one is n.

That's the whole move. One extra step — doubling — bolted onto a "choose 2" question you can already do backward in your head.

👀 Quick Example

Every player in a chess club played every other player exactly once. There were 45 games in total. How many players were in the club?

Step 1 — double the total: 45 × 2 = 90
Step 2 — find two consecutive numbers that multiply to 90: 9 × 10 = 90 ✓
Step 3 — take the BIGGER number: n = 10
10 players

⚠️ The One Mistake This Fixes

✗ Forgetting to double

Trying to find two consecutive numbers that multiply to 45 directly (there aren't any nice ones) — or guessing n = 45. The total given is HALF of n×(n−1), not the full product.

✓ Double first, then search

45 × 2 = 90, then 9 × 10 = 90, so n = 10. Doubling turns it back into the exact "choose 2" shape you already know.

Before you search for consecutive numbers, always double the total first.
