🔑 Why This Matters

What these puzzles actually are

A symbol or letter takes the place of a digit (0–9). Wherever that same symbol appears, it's always the SAME digit. Your job is to crack the code and work out which digit it's hiding. It's basically a secret code, and you're the code-breaker.

Why it's worth mastering right now

This exact puzzle type shows up 2–3 times on the ASMOPSS paper every single year, always in the harder sections. It rewards one specific habit: working in the RIGHT ORDER, not guessing randomly. That's the whole secret — and it's very learnable.

A symbol is just a digit wearing a disguise. Find the ONE column or fact that pins it down first.

🧩 The Code-Breaker's Method

1Is it ONE symbol in a simple equation (like 4 × ● = 28)? → Solve it directly, just like a missing-number sum.
2Is it TWO different symbols with a sum AND a difference given? → Use the "sum & difference" shortcut.
3Is it a COLUMN ADDITION or SUBTRACTION with a repeated symbol (like B7 + 2B = 60)? → Turn each 2-digit number into (tens × 10 + units), then solve like an equation.
4Whatever type it is: the digit MUST be 0–9. If your answer isn't in that range, you made an arithmetic slip — check your work.

The "sum & difference" shortcut for two symbols ● and ▲: if ●+▲ = S and ●−▲ = D, then ● = (S+D)÷2 and ▲ = (S−D)÷2. You'll see exactly why this works in Skill 2.

Skill 1 of 4

One Symbol — A Missing-Number Sum in Disguise

4 × ● = 28. What digit does ● stand for?

One unknown symbol, one equation → just undo the operation
Step 1 — the equation says "4 times what number gives 28": ● = 28 ÷ 4
Step 2 — calculate: 28 ÷ 4 = 7
● = 7

Check it: 4 × 7 = 28. ✓ And 7 is a valid single digit (0–9), so we're done.

⚠️ Always check your answer is a single digit, 0 through 9. If you get 14 or −3, you've made a mistake somewhere — a symbol can never be two digits or negative.

Two Symbols — The Sum & Difference Shortcut

▲ + ■ = 12 and ▲ − ■ = 4. What digit does ▲ stand for?

Add the two equations together — the ■ cancels out!
CLUE 1▲ + ■ = 12
CLUE 2▲ − ■ = 4
GOALFind ▲
Step 1 — add Clue 1 and Clue 2 together, line by line: (▲+■) + (▲−■) = 12 + 4
Step 2 — the ■ cancels (+■ and −■ add to zero): 2 × ▲ = 16
Step 3 — divide by 2: ▲ = 8
▲ = 8 (and ■ = 12−8 = 4)

Check it: 8+4=12 ✓ and 8−4=4 ✓. Both digits are 0–9. ✓

⚠️ The shortcut in one line: ▲ = (sum + difference) ÷ 2. Once you know ▲, just subtract it from the sum to get ■.

Column Addition — A Repeated Digit

B7 + 2B = 60, where B stands for the SAME digit in both numbers (B7 means a 2-digit number with tens-digit B and units-digit 7; 2B means tens-digit 2 and units-digit B). What digit does B represent?

Turn each 2-digit number into (tens × 10 + units) — this is Expanded Form from Number Theory
CLUE 1B7 means 10×B + 7
CLUE 22B means 20 + B
CLUE 3(10B + 7) + (20 + B) = 60
GOALFind B
Step 1 — combine the B terms and the plain numbers: 11B + 27 = 60
Step 2 — subtract 27 from both sides: 11B = 33
Step 3 — divide by 11: B = 3
B = 3

Check it: B7 = 37, 2B = 23. 37 + 23 = 60. ✓ And 3 is a valid digit. ✓

⚠️ The trick is always the same: rewrite every 2-digit code as (10 × tens-digit) + (units-digit), then solve like a normal balance-scale equation.

Skill 4 of 4 — Hardest

Trickier Codes — Subtraction and Repeated-Digit Multiplication

8C − D3 = 27, where C and D are digits (8C means tens-digit 8, units-digit C; D3 means tens-digit D, units-digit 3). What digit does C represent?

Step 1 — expand both 2-digit codes: 8C = 80 + C,   D3 = 10D + 3
Step 2 — write the subtraction as an equation: (80 + C) − (10D + 3) = 27
Step 3 — simplify: 77 + C − 10D = 27, so C − 10D = −50
Step 4 — C must be 0–9, so try D values until C lands in range: if D=5, then C = 10(5) − 50 = 0 ✓
C = 0 (check: 8C = 80, D3 = 53, and 80 − 53 = 27 ✓)

A second flavor: repeated-digit multiplication. ●● (a 2-digit number where BOTH digits are the same symbol, like 33 or 77) × 5 = 220. What digit does ● represent?

Step 1 — a repeated-digit number like ●● always equals 11 × ●: (because 10×● + ● = 11×●)
Step 2 — so the equation becomes: 11 × ● × 5 = 220, which is 55 × ● = 220
Step 3 — divide both sides by 55: ● = 4
● = 4 (check: 44 × 5 = 220 ✓)

⚠️ Whenever you see a 2-digit number with BOTH digits the same symbol, remember the shortcut: it always equals 11 × that digit. This turns a scary-looking puzzle into a simple division.
