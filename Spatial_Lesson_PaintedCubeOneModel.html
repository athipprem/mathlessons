🎯 Same Puzzle, ONE Idea Instead Of Four

What happened last time

On the last test, you got the two painted-cube questions with a simple one-line answer right — corners (always 8) and fully-hidden cubes. But on the other three, you wrote "forgot how 2 do." You told your dad exactly why: too many solving methods to remember. You were completely right — there WERE too many separate recipes.

Today's fix

There is only ONE idea in this whole lesson: every rectangular block made of unit cubes has exactly 4 kinds of parts — corners, edges, faces, and an inside. Every single unit cube belongs to ONE of these 4 parts. Once you can name which part a cube is in, you already know how many faces of it are painted.

One classification. Applied four times. Not four different tricks.

📦 Every Box Has These 4 Parts

Think of any box — a tissue box, a shoebox, a dice. It always has:

8
CORNERS
Always 8, on any box. Touches 3 outer faces → 3 faces painted.
12
EDGES
The 12 straight lines where 2 faces meet. Cubes here touch 2 outer faces → 2 faces painted.
6
FACES
The 6 flat outer sides. Cubes in the MIDDLE of a face touch 1 outer face → 1 face painted.
1
INSIDE
Fully buried, touches nothing outside → 0 faces painted.

⚠️ These aren't 4 different topics — they're the 4 physical parts of the SAME box. You already know a box has 8 corners, 12 edges, and 6 faces (you've seen dice and tissue boxes your whole life). We're just counting how many unit cubes live in each part.

Quick Recap — Corners & Inside (You Already Know These)

CORNERS: always 8, on any block where every side is 2 or more.   INSIDE: (length−2) × (width−2) × (height−2)

Example: a block 5 long, 4 wide, 3 tall, fully painted on the outside (including the bottom).

Corners — no calculation needed, it's always 8:
8 cubes have exactly 3 faces painted
Inside — shave 1 layer off each end, in each direction:
(5−2) × (4−2) × (3−2) = 3 × 2 × 1
6 cubes have 0 faces painted (fully hidden)

⚠️ These two you already had solid on the last test — this page is just a reminder before we build the two new ones the same way.

(6−2) × (4−2) × (4−2) = 4×2×2 = 16 hidden cubes

📐 Edges — The 12 Lines Where 2 Faces Meet

The one idea

Every box has 12 edges. Group them by which direction they run: 4 edges run along the LENGTH, 4 run along the WIDTH, 4 run along the HEIGHT. A cube sitting ON an edge (but not right at a corner) touches exactly 2 outer faces → 2 faces painted.

Cubes per edge = that direction's size − 2 (the 2 corner cubes at the ends don't count as "just edge")
So for the whole box:
4 × (length−2)  +  4 × (width−2)  +  4 × (height−2)
= total edge cubes (2 faces painted)

⚠️ Notice this is the SAME "shave 2 off, then multiply" idea from the inside formula — just applied to one direction at a time instead of all three at once, then added up across the 3 groups of 4 edges.

Edges — Worked Example

A block is 6 long, 4 wide, 3 tall, fully painted (incl. bottom). How many unit cubes have EXACTLY 2 faces painted?

4 length-edges, each with (6−2)=4 cubes:
4 × 4 = 16
4 width-edges, each with (4−2)=2 cubes:
4 × 2 = 8
4 height-edges, each with (3−2)=1 cube:
4 × 1 = 4
Add all three groups:
16 + 8 + 4 = 28
28 unit cubes have exactly 2 faces painted

▦ Faces — The 6 Flat Outer Sides

The one idea

Every box has 6 flat faces, grouped into 3 opposite PAIRS: top+bottom, front+back, left+right. A cube sitting in the MIDDLE of a face (not on any edge) touches exactly 1 outer face → 1 face painted.

Middle-of-face cubes on ONE face = (the other 2 dimensions, each −2) multiplied together. Each PAIR has 2 identical faces, so ×2.
So for the whole box:
2×(length−2)×(width−2)  +  2×(length−2)×(height−2)  +  2×(width−2)×(height−2)
= total face-centre cubes (1 face painted)

⚠️ Same pattern again: pick 2 of the 3 dimensions, shave 2 off each, multiply — then ×2 because every pair has 2 matching faces. Add up the 3 pairs.

Faces — Worked Example

A block is 6 long, 4 wide, 3 tall, fully painted (incl. bottom). How many unit cubes have EXACTLY 1 face painted?

Top+bottom pair (uses length & width): 2 × (6−2) × (4−2):
2 × 4 × 2 = 16
Front+back pair (uses length & height): 2 × (6−2) × (3−2):
2 × 4 × 1 = 8
Left+right pair (uses width & height): 2 × (4−2) × (3−2):
2 × 2 × 1 = 4
Add all three pairs:
16 + 8 + 4 = 28
28 unit cubes have exactly 1 face painted

Practice — Exactly 1 Face Painted

P1.  A block is 5 long, 3 wide, 2 tall, fully painted (incl. bottom). How many unit cubes have exactly 1 face painted?
Top+bottom: 2×(5−2)×(3−2)=2×3×1=6.
Front+back: 2×(5−2)×(2−2)=2×3×0=0.
Left+right: 2×(3−2)×(2−2)=2×1×0=0.
6+0+0 = 6 cubes
P2.  A block is 6 long, 5 wide, 3 tall, fully painted (incl. bottom). How many unit cubes have exactly 1 face painted?
Top+bottom: 2×(6−2)×(5−2)=2×4×3=24.
Front+back: 2×(6−2)×(3−2)=2×4×1=8.
Left+right: 2×(5−2)×(3−2)=2×3×1=6.
24+8+6 = 38 cubes

All 4 Parts Together — The Full Checklist In Action

A block is 5 long, 4 wide, 3 tall, fully painted (incl. bottom). Find ALL FOUR counts, and check they add up to the total number of cubes.

Corners (always 8):
8
Edges: 4(5−2)+4(4−2)+4(3−2) = 12+8+4:
24
Faces: 2(5−2)(4−2)+2(5−2)(3−2)+2(4−2)(3−2) = 12+6+4:
22
Inside: (5−2)(4−2)(3−2) = 3×2×1:
6
Check — total cubes should be 5×4×3 = 60:
8 + 24 + 22 + 6 = 60 ✓ matches! Every cube accounted for.

⚠️ This add-up-to-the-total check is a great way to catch a mistake in ANY of the 4 counts before you submit an answer.

🗺️ The Full Checklist — One Model, Four Parts

3Corners (3 faces): always 8, if every side is 2 or more.
2Edges (2 faces): 4(l−2) + 4(w−2) + 4(h−2).
1Faces (1 face): 2(l−2)(w−2) + 2(l−2)(h−2) + 2(w−2)(h−2).

✓Always check: the 4 counts should add up to l×w×h.

⚠️ It's the SAME idea every time: "shave 2 off the sides that matter, multiply, and for edges/faces there are always groups of 4 or pairs of 2 to add up." One classification, four parts of the same box.

📖 Today's Big Idea

A box has 4 parts: corners, edges, faces, inside. Every unit cube belongs to exactly one. Name the part, and you already know the painted-face count.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You know corners = always 8 and inside = (l−2)(w−2)(h−2), without hesitating
  • You can find edges by adding 3 groups of 4, and faces by adding 3 pairs of 2
  • You check your 4 counts add up to the total cube count
  • You think "which of the 4 parts is this?" instead of trying to remember 4 separate tricks
