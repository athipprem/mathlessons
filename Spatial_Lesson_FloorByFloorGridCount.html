🎯 A Totally Different Way In

What happened the last two times

Painted-cube boxes have tripped you up twice now — first with 4 separate formulas to remember, then with "one classification, four parts," which was still 4 different calculations underneath. Both times you said the same true thing: too many methods, too easy to mix up.

Today we're not re-explaining the same idea a third time — we're using a skill you ALREADY have

You are already really good at one specific thing: counting corner/edge/middle cells in a flat grid (you've done this with rectangles and squares before, and you're solid at it). Today's whole trick is: a 3D painted box is just a stack of flat grids ("floors") — the exact grid-counting you already know, done floor by floor. No new formulas to memorise. Just your existing grid skill, used one extra time per floor.

3D box = a stack of 2D grids. You already know how to count a 2D grid.

🏢 A Box Is A Stack Of Floors

Picture a box built from little unit cubes — like a block of sugar cubes, or a small Lego tower. If it's 4 long, 3 wide, 3 tall, imagine slicing it into 3 flat floors, stacked on top of each other. Each floor is a flat 4 × 3 grid — 12 little squares, viewed from above.

⚠️ Real life check: a box of 36 sugar cubes, 4×3 on the bottom, stacked 3 high, really does split perfectly into 3 flat layers of 12 cubes each. That's all "4 long, 3 wide, 3 tall" means.

⬆️ Adding The 3rd Dimension — Which Floor Are You On?

The one new idea

A flat grid only tells you about SIDE walls. But a box also has a TOP and a BOTTOM. So every cube also asks one more question: "Am I on the very top floor, the very bottom floor, or a middle floor?"

TOP floor or BOTTOM floor → touches 1 extra painted face (the top or bottom of the box).   MIDDLE floor → touches 0 extra.

⚠️ That's the whole 3rd dimension — just one yes/no question, same as "am I on an outer wall" was for the flat grid. Top and bottom floors work exactly the same as each other.

🧩 Put It Together — The ONE Rule

Painted faces on a cube  =  (wall-touches from its floor's grid: 0, 1, or 2)  +  (1 if top/bottom floor, else 0)
1Look at the cube's FLOOR. Is its grid-cell a corner (2), edge (1), or centre (0)?
2Is the cube's FLOOR the top, the bottom, or a middle floor? (+1 if top/bottom, +0 if middle)
3Add the two numbers. That's the total painted faces for that cube — maximum ever is 3.

⚠️ That's it. One rule, two yes/no-ish questions, always the same two questions for every single cube in any box.

⚡ Optional Shortcut — For When Drawing Every Floor Is Too Slow

You never HAVE to use this — counting from the picture always works. But once the picture-counting feels easy, here's a shortcut for big boxes where drawing every floor would take forever. It's the exact same picture, just described in numbers instead of drawn:

Corner cells per floor (any floor):
always 4
Edge cells per floor (a floor is length × width):
2×(length−2) + 2×(width−2)
Centre cells per floor:
(length−2) × (width−2)
Then: multiply by 2 for the top+bottom floors (they're identical), and by (height−2) for how many middle floors there are — then add top/bottom's own numbers using the +1 rule and middle's using the +0 rule.

⚠️ Notice this is just "count the picture," written as maths instead of drawn as a grid — same 2 questions (which grid-cell type, which floor-type), just faster for huge boxes. If it ever feels confusing, go straight back to drawing the floor and counting — that always works and is never wrong.

🗺️ Full Capstone — 5 Long, 3 Wide, 3 Tall

Fully painted, incl. bottom. Find all 4 counts and check the total.

Each floor is 5×3. Corners per floor (always 4), on 2 top/bottom floors:
4 + 4 = 8
Edge cells per floor: 2(5−2)+2(3−2) = 6+2 = 8. Top+bottom floors (+1 each) give 8+8=16; the 1 middle floor's corner cells (4, but +0 since middle) become "2-face" too:
16 + 4 = 20
Centre cells per floor: (5−2)(3−2)=3. Top+bottom (+1 each): 3+3=6; middle floor's edge cells (8, +0) also land here:
6 + 8 = 14
Middle floor's own centre cells (+0) are the only fully-hidden cubes:
3
Check — total should be 5×3×3 = 45:
8 + 20 + 14 + 3 = 45 ✓ matches!

⚠️ This is the trickiest part: a MIDDLE floor's corner cells become 2-face cubes (not 3), and its edge cells become 1-face cubes (not 2) — because the floor itself gets +0, not +1. Always ask "which floor am I on?" before you decide the final number.

📖 Today's Big Idea

A box is a stack of flat grids ("floors"). Count each floor the way you already count a grid (corner/edge/centre), then add +1 if that floor is the top or bottom, +0 if it's a middle floor. Add the two numbers to get a cube's painted-face count.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You can draw a box as a stack of flat floors without help
  • You can classify a floor's cells as corner/edge/centre by looking at the picture (a skill you already had)
  • You remember: top/bottom floor = +1, middle floor = +0
  • You check your 4 counts add up to the total number of cubes, every time
  • On a middle floor, you remember corner cells become 2-face and edge cells become 1-face (not 3 and 2)

Same underlying maths as before — but now it's a picture you already know how to count, not a new set of formulas.
