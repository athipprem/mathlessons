🍬 What's Left Over?

Why learn this?

You have 17 sweets and want to share them equally among 5 friends. Each friend gets 3, and 2 are left over. That "left over" is the remainder. Remainders are everywhere — sharing snacks, days of the week, knowing if a number splits evenly. And contests love a puzzle like: "I'm thinking of a number. Divide by 5 and 2 are left over… what could it be?" This lesson makes that puzzle easy.

remainder = what is LEFT OVER after sharing into equal groups

"17 ÷ 5 leaves remainder 2" because 5 × 3 = 15, and 17 − 15 = 2 left over.

The Big Idea: Make a List

Say a number leaves remainder 2 when you divide by 5. Which numbers do that? Easy — start at the remainder (2), then keep adding 5:

2712172227…

Check one: 12 ÷ 5 = 2 groups of 5 (that's 10), and 2 left over. ✓ Every number on this list leaves remainder 2.

Numbers with "÷ A remainder r"  =  r,  r+A,  r+A+A,  …
(start at r, keep adding A)

🗺️ Two Clues? Use the Map

Puzzles often give two clues, like: "÷ 5 leaves remainder 2, AND ÷ 4 leaves remainder 1." Here's the plan:

1Write the LIST for the first clue (start at the remainder, keep adding).
2Go along the list and test each one against the second clue.
3The number that passes both clues is your answer. 🏆

Tip: start your list with the clue that has the bigger divider — fewer numbers to write!

Worked Example

A number between 1 and 30 leaves remainder 2 when ÷ 5, and remainder 1 when ÷ 4. Find it.

Step 1 — list for "÷ 5 remainder 2" (start at 2, add 5):
2712172227
Step 2 — test each against "÷ 4 remainder 1":
2 ÷ 4 → remainder 2  ✗
7 ÷ 4 → remainder 3  ✗
12 ÷ 4 → remainder 0  ✗
17 ÷ 4 = 4 groups (16), remainder 1  ✓
Answer = 17

Double-check: 17 ÷ 5 = 3 r2 ✓ and 17 ÷ 4 = 4 r1 ✓. Both clues fit!

📖 Remainders — in one line

List the first clue (start at the remainder, keep adding the divider). Then test each one against the second clue.

The number that passes BOTH clues is the answer. Always double-check it against both.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You can write the list for "÷ 6 remainder 1" → 1, 7, 13, 19, …
  • With two clues, you list one and test the other — no guessing
