🗺️ The Consecutive-Numbers Map

Consecutive means "in a row" — each number is one step from the next. The questions almost always give you a sum and ask you to find the numbers. There are two routes, and you choose based on what the question gives you:

🔍 How many numbers? And what step — +1, or +2?

⭐ MIDDLE TRICK — for an ODD count (3 or 5 numbers)

The middle number is the average. middle = sum ÷ count. Then step outwards. Fastest route — trained today.

🧮 UNIT METHOD — for an EVEN count, or even/odd numbers

Write them with n: n, n+1, n+2, … (or n, n+2, n+4 for even/odd). Add, set equal to the sum, solve. Trained today.

Step size: ordinary numbers step by +1 (7, 8, 9). Consecutive even or odd numbers step by +2 (6, 8, 10  or  5, 7, 9).

The Middle Trick (odd count)

Three numbers in a row are balanced around the middle one. The one below pulls down by the same amount the one above pushes up — so they cancel. That means:

⭐ middle number = sum ÷ how many

Example: three consecutive numbers add to 60.

19
20
21
−1      middle      +1
middle = 60 ÷ 3 = 20← the average
step out: 19, 20, 21← one below, one above
check: 19+20+21 = 60 ✓

Five in a row works the same way: middle = sum ÷ 5, then two steps each side.

The Unit Method (even count & even/odd)

When there is no single middle (an even count like 2 or 4), or the numbers step by 2, the middle trick is awkward. Use n for the smallest and build up.

Four in a row, sum 90

n + (n+1) + (n+2) + (n+3)
= 4n + 6 = 90
4n = 84 → n = 21
21, 22, 23, 24 ✓

Three consecutive EVEN, sum 84

n + (n+2) + (n+4)← step +2
= 3n + 6 = 84
3n = 78 → n = 26
26, 28, 30 ✓

💡 Shortcut check: the even-count answer also obeys the middle idea — for "four in a row, sum 90", 90÷4 = 22.5 sits exactly between 22 and 23, the two middle numbers. Use it to check your n answer.

Two-Step Questions

Harder questions add a second step after you find the numbers. Find the numbers first, then answer what is actually asked — read the last line twice.

Example A  Three consecutive integers add to 81. What is the sum of the smallest and largest?

middle = 81 ÷ 3 = 27 → 26, 27, 28
smallest + largest = 26 + 28 = 54← this is what they asked

Example B  Five consecutive integers add to 90. If the largest is removed, what do the other four add to?

middle = 90 ÷ 5 = 18 → 16, 17, 18, 19, 20
remove largest (20): 90 − 20 = 70← no need to re-add

Example C  The sum of three consecutive integers is 9 more than 60. Find the largest.

first work out the sum: 60 + 9 = 69← hidden sum
middle = 69 ÷ 3 = 23 → 22, 23, 24
largest = 24

Practice

Odd count → middle trick. Even count or even/odd → unit method. Then read the last line again.

Q1.   Three consecutive integers add up to 54. What is the largest one?
middle = 54 ÷ 3 = 18 → 17, 18, 19
largest = 19
Q2.   Five consecutive integers add up to 65. What is the smallest one?
middle = 65 ÷ 5 = 13 → 11, 12, 13, 14, 15
smallest = 11
Q3.   Three consecutive EVEN numbers add up to 90. What is the middle one?
middle = 90 ÷ 3 = 30 → 28, 30, 32
all even ✓   middle = 30
Q4.   Four consecutive integers add up to 74. What is the largest one?
4n + 6 = 74 → 4n = 68 → n = 17
17, 18, 19, 20 → largest = 20
📖 What You Learned Today
Odd count:middle = sum ÷ count, then step outwards. Even count:write n, n+1, n+2, n+3; add; solve for n. Even / odd:step by +2 → n, n+2, n+4. Always check:add your numbers back — do they hit the sum? Last line:smallest? largest? middle? a new total? Answer THAT.

🏆 You are ready when...

  • You spot odd-count and go straight to sum ÷ count
  • You write n, n+1, n+2… without hesitating for even counts
  • You re-read the last line before writing your final answer
