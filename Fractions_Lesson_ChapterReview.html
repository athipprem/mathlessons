Activity 1–3 · Add and take away

➕ Add And Take Away

Adding is the same as taking away, except you add the tops. Tap Next line — each line says which box of your card it uses.

Worked example:

Work out 3 1/2 + 1 5/6.

Answer = 3 1/2 + 1 5/6add: + side of the card
= 7/2 + 1 5/6change the first number to an improper fraction: 3 1/2 = 7/2 (3 wholes = 6 halves, plus 1 half = 7 halves)
= 7/2 + 11/6change the second number to an improper fraction: 1 5/6 = 11/6 (1 whole = 6 sixths, plus 5 sixths = 11 sixths)
= 21/6 + 11/6make the bottoms the same: 7/2 = 21/6 (top and bottom × 3)
= 32/621 sixths and 11 sixths make 32 sixths
= 16/3simplify: divide the top and the bottom by 2
= 5 1/316 thirds = 5 wholes and 1 third over

Activity 5, 7, 8 · Multiply

✖️ Multiply

Tops × tops, bottoms × bottoms. A whole number is a fraction over 1.

Worked example:

Work out 9/14 x 7/12.

Answer = 9/14 × 7/12multiply: × side of the card
= 63/168tops: 9 × 7 = 63; bottoms: 14 × 12 = 168
= 3/8simplify: divide the top and the bottom by 21

Worked example:

Work out 2 1/3 x 6.

Answer = 2 1/3 × 6multiply: × side of the card
= 7/3 × 6change the first number to an improper fraction: 2 1/3 = 7/3 (2 wholes = 6 thirds, plus 1 third = 7 thirds)
= 7/3 × 6/1a whole number is a fraction over 1: 6 = 6/1
= 42/3tops: 7 × 6 = 42; bottoms: 3 × 1 = 3
= 1442 ÷ 3 = 14

Activity 4, 6, 11–12 · Of, remainder, how much more

💰 Of, The Remainder, How Much More

Tap Next line. Before part (b) a ④ chip tells you what “how much more” means.

Worked example:

Nina had $540. She spent 1/3 of it on a bike and 5/9 of the remainder on a helmet. (a) How much money did she have left? (b) How much more did she spend on the helmet than on the bike?

Taken part 1 = 1/3 × 540① "1/3 of the money" → taken part = 1/3 × whole
= 1/3 × 540/1a whole number is a fraction over 1: 540 = 540/1
= 540/3tops: 1 × 540 = 540; bottoms: 3 × 1 = 3
= 180 540 ÷ 3 = 180
Left part 1 = 540 − 180② left part = whole − taken part
= 360 540 − 180 = 360
New whole = 360 ③ "of the money that was LEFT" → the left part is the NEW whole
Taken part 2 = 5/9 × 360① "5/9 of the money that was left" → taken part = 5/9 × whole
= 5/9 × 360/1a whole number is a fraction over 1: 360 = 360/1
= 1800/9tops: 5 × 360 = 1800; bottoms: 9 × 1 = 9
= 200 1800 ÷ 9 = 200
Left part 2 = 360 − 200② left part = whole − taken part
= 160 360 − 200 = 160
How much more = 200 − 180④ "how much MORE?" → bigger part − smaller part
= 20200 − 180 = 20
④how much more = bigger − smaller: helmet $200 − bike $180

Activity 9–10 · Fractions, Division and Decimals

➗ Division And Decimals

A fraction is a division: a ÷ b = a/b. To make a decimal, divide the top by the bottom. Rounding to 2 decimal places: look at the 3rd digit.

Worked example:

Write 7 ÷ 8 as a fraction, then as a decimal.

7 ÷ 8 = 7/8a ÷ b = a/b (÷ box of the card)
7/8 = 875/1000make the bottom 1000: top and bottom × 125
7/8 = 0.875875/1000 = 0.875

Worked example:

Write 2 ÷ 3 as a decimal correct to 2 decimal places.

2 ÷ 3 = 2/3a ÷ b = a/b (÷ box of the card)
2/3 ≈ 0.672 ÷ 3 = 0.666… and never stops; the 3rd decimal digit is 6: 5 or more, so round up

Activity 3 Q6, 11–13 · Working backwards

⏪ Working Backwards

“How much at FIRST?” Use bars, units and the B box of your card.

Worked example:

Raj used 2/7 of his paint. He had 15 L left. How much paint did he have at first?

Left fraction 1 = 1 − 2/7round 1 took 2/7, so what is left is the rest of the whole
= 5/77/7 − 2/7 = 5/7
5 units = 155 of the 7 equal units are left, and that is 15 L
1 unit = 15 ÷ 5share it equally into the units
= 315 ÷ 5 = 3
Whole = 3 × 7the whole is all 7 units
= 21 L3 × 7 = 21

The shaded part is the taken fraction. The unshaded units are the part you know.

Tom spent 1/6 of his money. Then he spent 3/7 of the money he had left. He had $40 left in the end. How much money did he have at first?

= 4/77/7 − 3/7 = 4/7
4 units = 404 of the 7 equal units are left, and that is 40
1 unit = 40 ÷ 4share it equally into the units
= 1040 ÷ 4 = 10
Left after round 1 = 10 × 7the amount before round 2 is all 7 units
= 7010 × 7 = 70
Left fraction 1 = 1 − 1/6round 1 took 1/6, so what is left is the rest of the whole
= 5/66/6 − 1/6 = 5/6
5 units = 705 of the 6 equal units are left, and that is 70

THE BIG PICTURE
① Taken part = fraction × whole “1/4 OF the juice”
Told? “3/4 L” → Taken part = 3/4 L. No × needed!
1/3 OF 12 bricks: the bricks split into 3 equal groups, one group lights up, giving 4.
1/3 × 12 = 12/3 = 4. ‘Of’ means ×.
1/3 kg is just a small lump — that’s TOLD, not ×.
② Left part = whole − taken part
③ “… of what was LEFT”? → the left part is the NEW whole. Back to ①.
④ LAST CHECK: read the question again — does it ask for a TAKEN part, the LEFT part, or HOW MUCH MORE?
“How much more?” = bigger part − smaller part (that’s the − side too)

÷ FRACTIONS ARE DIVISIONS
a ÷ b = a/b   5 ÷ 6 → 5/6 each   13 ÷ 4 = 13/4 = 3 1/4
Improper → mixed: top ÷ bottom = wholes, remainder = the new top.
. FRACTION → DECIMAL
Make the bottom 10, 100 or 1000:   3/4 = 75/100 = 0.75
Can’t? Divide top by bottom:   1/3 = 0.333…
≈ ROUND to 2 decimal places: look at the 3rd digit → 5 or more, round up. 0.333… ≈ 0.33
B WORKING BACKWARDS (“how much at FIRST?”)
1. Left fraction = 1 − taken fraction
2. That many units = the amount you know
3. 1 unit = amount ÷ units
4. Whole = 1 unit × the bottom number
Two rounds? Undo the LAST round first, then the first round.
