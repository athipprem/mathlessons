🎯 Why Learn This?

"Of" shows up everywhere

"I ate ¼ of the pizza." "The sale takes ⅓ off the price." "A recipe for 5 batches needs ⅔ cup of sugar EACH." Every time you see a fraction attached to an amount with the word "of," you're being asked to multiply. Bakers scale recipes, shoppers work out discounts, and builders cut materials to fractional lengths — all using this one skill.

Your new superpower

You already know how to add and subtract mixed numbers (Lesson 1). Now you'll learn to multiply with fractions — a fraction of a set, fraction × fraction, and mixed number × whole number — all using ONE method.

Multiplying by a fraction means taking a PART of something.

🧮 The ONE Rule For Every Case

The one new idea

No matter what you're multiplying — a fraction, a whole number, or a mixed number — the method is always the SAME: write everything as a fraction (a whole number n is really n/1; a mixed number gets converted to an improper fraction, like you learned in Lesson 1), then multiply straight across: numerator × numerator, denominator × denominator. Simplify at the end.

1Write every number as a fraction (whole number n → n/1, mixed number → improper fraction).
2Multiply the TWO numerators together.
3Multiply the TWO denominators together.
4Simplify. If the answer is improper, convert back to a mixed number.
Numerator × numerator, over denominator × denominator. Every single time.

⚠️ Don't mix this up with Lesson 1! If you see a + or − sign, match denominators and combine (Lesson 1's method). Only use THIS multiply-straight-across method when you see a × sign, or the word "of."

Case 1 — Fraction Of A Set

What is ¾ of 20 marbles?

Step 1 — write 20 as a fraction:
20 = 20/1
Step 2 & 3 — multiply straight across:
¾ × 20/1  =  (3×20) / (4×1)  =  60/4
Step 4 — simplify (60 ÷ 4):
¾ of 20 = 15

⚠️ You might already know a shortcut here — divide 20 by the denominator (4) first, THEN multiply by the numerator (3): 20÷4=5, 5×3=15. Same answer! Multiplying straight across always works, even when the shortcut is trickier to spot — so it's the method to trust.

Case 2 — Fraction × Fraction

2/5 × 3/4 = ? The rule (from screen 2) is simple: multiply the two numerators together, and multiply the two denominators together — (numerator × numerator) over (denominator × denominator). That's the shortcut you'll actually use every time.

(numerator × numerator) / (denominator × denominator)  =  (2×3) / (5×4)

Here's WHY that shortcut works, using a grid picture: shade 2 of the 5 rows (for 2/5), and 3 of the 4 columns (for 3/4). The part shaded in BOTH directions is the answer.

The grid has 5 rows × 4 columns = 20 cells in total (that's the new denominator: 5×4).
2 of the 5 rows are shaded, and 3 of the 4 columns are shaded — the overlap is 2×3 = 6 cells (that's the new numerator).
2/5 × 3/4  =  (2×3)/(5×4)  =  6/20
Simplify (both divisible by 2):
2/5 × 3/4 = 6/20 = 3/10

⚠️ Notice this example uses four DIFFERENT digits (2, 5, 3, 4) — nothing repeats. If a question ever reuses the same digit as both a numerator and a denominator, don't let that trick you: just do numerator×numerator and denominator×denominator, exactly like this, every time.

Case 3 — Mixed Number × Whole Number

Reusing what you already know

From Lesson 1, you can already turn a mixed number into an improper fraction. Do that FIRST, then it's just the same "multiply straight across" rule as before.

2⅓ × 4 = ?

Step 1 — convert the mixed number (from Lesson 1's rule: whole×denominator + numerator):
2⅓ → (2×3)+1 = 7  →  7/3
Step 1b — write the whole number 4 as a fraction:
4 = 4/1
Step 2 & 3 — multiply straight across:
7/3 × 4/1 = (7×4)/(3×1) = 28/3
Step 4 — 28/3 is improper, convert back to a mixed number (28÷3 = 9 remainder 1):
2⅓ × 4 = 9⅓

⚠️ A very common mistake is multiplying only the WHOLE part (2×4=8) and forgetting the fraction part entirely. Converting to an improper fraction FIRST avoids that trap completely.

Practice — Multiplying With Fractions

P1.  What is 5/6 of 24?
5/6 × 24/1 = (5×24)/(6×1) = 120/6
5/6 of 24 = 20
P2.  3/4 × 2/5 = ?
3/4 × 2/5 = (3×2)/(4×5) = 6/20
6/20 simplified (÷2) = 3/10
P3.  3 2/5 × 3 = ?
3 2/5 → (3×5)+2 = 17 → 17/5. Then 17/5 × 3/1 = 51/5.
51/5 = 10 remainder 1 = 10 1/5
P4 (Word Problem).  A recipe needs 2/3 cup of sugar per batch. How much sugar is needed for 5 batches?
2/3 × 5/1 = 10/3.
10/3 = 3 remainder 1 = 3⅓ cups
📖 Today's Big Idea

To multiply with fractions, write EVERYTHING as a fraction (whole number n → n/1, mixed number → improper fraction), multiply numerator × numerator and denominator × denominator, then simplify. Same method for a fraction of a set, fraction × fraction, and mixed number × whole number.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You can write a whole number as a fraction over 1
  • You can convert a mixed number to an improper fraction (from Lesson 1) before multiplying
  • You multiply numerators together and denominators together — never add them
  • You always simplify, and convert an improper answer back to a mixed number
  • You recognise "of" in a word problem means multiply
