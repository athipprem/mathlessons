🍬 Splitting a Bag of Identical Things

Why learn this?

You've counted PAIRS (choosing 2 friends from a group) and you've counted ARRANGEMENTS (1st place, 2nd place). Here's a new kind of sharing question: you have a bag of identical things — candies, stickers, raffle tickets — and you want to split them among a few people so everyone gets at least one. How many different ways can you do the split? This shows up everywhere: splitting prize money among winners, sharing snacks among friends, or handing out game tokens to teams — and it's a favourite in maths competitions.

Good news: this uses a NEW picture (stars and gaps) but the SAME "choose 2" move you already own.

The Big Idea — Turn Items Into Stars

Line up all the identical items in a single row, and draw each one as a star (★). To split them into groups, you just need to decide WHERE to cut the row.

★ ★ ★ ★

4 identical stickers, drawn as 4 stars in a row.

Between the stars there are little gaps. If you place a divider in one gap, you cut the row into 2 groups — everything before the divider, and everything after it.

Number of gaps between n stars = n − 1

⚠️ A divider can ONLY go in a gap BETWEEN two stars — never before the first star or after the last one. That is exactly what guarantees every group gets at least 1 item. This whole lesson only covers the "at least 1 each" rule.

Worked Example, Part 1 — Splitting Into 2 Groups

Sam has 4 identical stickers to give to 2 friends. If each friend must get at least 1 sticker, how many different ways can Sam split the stickers?

Step 1 — draw the 4 stickers as 4 stars in a row:
★ ★ ★ ★
Step 2 — count the gaps between the stars (n = 4 stars):
gaps = n − 1 = 4 − 1 = 3
Step 3 — only 2 groups needed, so only ONE divider is needed. Each different gap = one different way to split:
Gap 1 → 1 & 3  ·  Gap 2 → 2 & 2  ·  Gap 3 → 3 & 1
3 ways
For 2 groups: number of ways = number of gaps = n − 1

Worked Example, Part 2 — Splitting Into 3 Groups

7 identical candies are shared among 3 kids. If each kid must get at least 1 candy, how many different ways can the candies be shared?

With 3 groups you need 2 dividers, not 1. So the question becomes: how many ways can you choose 2 of the gaps for your 2 dividers? That's a "choose 2" job — the exact move from Art of Counting!

Step 1 — draw 7 candies as 7 stars, count the gaps:
★ ★ ★ ★ ★ ★ ★  →  gaps = 7 − 1 = 6
Step 2 — 3 groups need 2 dividers, so choose 2 of the 6 gaps: (n × (n−1)) ÷ 2, where n = 6 gaps. Bracket first:
(6 × 5) ÷ 2  =  30 ÷ 2  =  15
15 ways
For 3 groups: gaps = n − 1, then choose 2 of the gaps — (gaps × (gaps−1)) ÷ 2

🗺️ The Plan — Every Time, No Skipping

1Check the rule is "each group gets AT LEAST 1." This lesson's method only works for that rule.
2Count the gaps: gaps = n − 1, where n = the number of identical items.
3Count how many groups you're splitting into: 2 groups → 1 divider needed. 3 groups → 2 dividers needed.
42 groups: the answer is simply the number of gaps (n − 1). 3 groups: choose 2 of the gaps — (gaps × (gaps−1)) ÷ 2, bracket first.
5Sanity-check on small numbers by actually listing the splits if n is small enough — it should match your shortcut answer.

⚠️ This lesson only covers 2 or 3 groups. If a question ever asks for 4 or more groups, that needs a new move — don't guess, ask first.
