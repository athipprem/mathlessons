🧩 Problem Solving

🧩 What Makes a Multi-Step Problem?

ISA and competition problems rarely ask one simple calculation. They chain 2–3 operations together and hide what's being asked.

📋 The 4-Step Attack Plan

1
READ — underline numbers and what's being asked
2
IDENTIFY — what type? (ratio / work-backwards / fraction chain)
3
PLAN — write the chain before calculating
4
CHECK — does the answer make sense?
💡 You already know each piece: ratio, fractions, work-backwards. This lesson chains them together into ISA-level problems.

📊 Type A: Ratio Word Problems

When you're given a ratio and one quantity, find 1 part first, then answer what's asked.

1 part = given quantity ÷ ratio number for that item
Worked Example

In a class, the ratio of boys to girls is 3 : 4. There are 28 girls. How many students are there in total?

1
Girls = 4 parts = 28. So 1 part = 28 ÷ 4 = 7
2
Boys = 3 parts = 3 × 7 = 21
3
Total = 21 + 28 = 49 students
⚠️ Don't multiply ratio directly — always find 1 part first, then scale up.

⏪ Type B: Work Backwards

When you know the end result and need to find the start. Reverse every operation in reverse order.

If forward is: × 3 then + 12 → backward is: − 12 then ÷ 3
Worked Example

After buying 12 more marbles and then tripling what he had, Leo had 60 marbles. How many did he start with?

Start→ +12 →Middle→ ×3 →60
1
Reverse ×3: 60 ÷ 3 = 20 (was middle)
2
Reverse +12: 20 − 12 = 8 (was start)
3
Check: 8 + 12 = 20, × 3 = 60 ✓
💡 Always draw the chain first. Then reverse it step by step from the end.

📖 Type C: Fraction of Remaining

Each step reduces what remains. Track the fraction of the whole that's left after each operation.

Worked Example

Maya read ½ of a book on day 1. On day 2 she read ⅓ of the remaining pages. She has 40 pages left. How many pages does the book have?

1
After day 1: ½ of book remains
2
Day 2 reads ⅓ of remaining = ⅓ × ½ = 1/6 of book
So left after day 2 = ½ − 1/6 = 3/6 − 1/6 = 2/6 = 1/3 of book
3
1/3 of book = 40 pages → Total = 40 × 3 = 120 pages
💡 After each step, write: "fraction remaining = ..." Track it like a shrinking bar.

💪 Practice — Ratio Problem

In a parking lot, the ratio of cars to motorcycles is 3 : 4. There are 28 motorcycles. How many cars are there?

1
Motorcycles = 4 parts = 28. 1 part = 28 ÷ 4 = ___
2
Cars = 3 parts = 3 × ___ = ___
1 part = 28÷4 = 7. Cars = 3×7 = 21 ✓

💪 Practice — Work Backwards

After spending $18 and then receiving double what she had left, Emma had $84. How much did she start with?

1
Draw the chain: Start → −$18 → middle → ×2 → $84
2
Reverse ×2: 84 ÷ 2 = ___
3
Reverse −18: ___ + 18 = ___
84÷2=42. 42+18=60. Check: 60−18=42, ×2=84 ✓

💪 Practice — Fraction of Remaining

A shopkeeper sold ⅓ of his stock in the morning. In the afternoon he sold ¼ of what remained. He had 30 items left at the end of the day. How many items did he start with?

1
After morning: 2/3 of stock remains
2
Afternoon sells ¼ of 2/3 = 2/12 = 1/6. Remaining = 2/3 − 1/6 = 4/6 − 1/6 = 3/6 = ½
3
½ of stock = 30 → Total = 30 × 2 = ___
½ × total = 30 → total = 60 items. Check: sold 20 morn + 10 afternoon = 30 sold, 30 left ✓

🧩 The 4-Step Attack Plan

1. READ — underline what's given and what's asked
2. IDENTIFY — ratio / work-backwards / fraction chain?
3. PLAN — write the chain or the 1-part calculation first
4. CHECK — substitute back and verify

A
Ratio: Find 1 part first → scale up to answer
B
Work-backwards: Draw chain → reverse each step from the end
C
Fraction chain: Track fraction remaining → end fraction × total = leftover
🏆 In the test — write out the chain before calculating. 10 seconds of planning prevents wrong answers.
