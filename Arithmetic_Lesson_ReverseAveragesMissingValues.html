⚖️ One Number That Fairly Represents A Whole Group

Why learn this?

An AVERAGE is one number that stands in for a whole group of numbers — a batting average in cricket, an average test score, the average temperature this week. It's a shortcut that saves you from listing every single value. Today you'll learn how averages are built (so you can compute them), and — the more powerful skill — how to work BACKWARDS from an average to find something that's missing or hidden.

Where this shows up

Competition papers love the "reverse" version: "Four students' average age is 10. The youngest leaves, and the average of the rest rises to 11 — how old was the one who left?" You can't just read that answer off a list — you have to work backwards through the average. That's exactly the superpower this lesson builds.

AVERAGE = TOTAL ÷ COUNT — and everything today is just this ONE rule, used forwards and backwards.

🗺️ The Pocket Map — Four Moves, One Rule

Every question today is built from the SAME rule: Average = Total ÷ Count. The only thing that changes is which piece is missing. Here's your map for the whole lesson:

1You know the numbers, need the AVERAGE: add them all up, divide by how many there are. TODAY
2You know the AVERAGE and COUNT, need the TOTAL: multiply them. This is the rule turned around. TODAY
3You know the average of a list, but ONE number is missing: find the total first (Move 2), then subtract the numbers you DO know. TODAY
4Someone joins or leaves the group and the average CHANGES: find the total before, find the total after, then subtract to find what changed. TODAY

⚠️ Notice Moves 2, 3, and 4 are really the SAME rule read backwards. If you know Average = Total ÷ Count, you already know Total = Average × Count — it's just the same fact, flipped.

Move 1 — What Is An Average?

AVERAGE = TOTAL ÷ COUNT

Ben has 4 bags of marbles. Bag A has 5 marbles, Bag B has 7, Bag C has 9, and Bag D has 11. What is the AVERAGE number of marbles per bag?

Step 1 — add up ALL the marbles (find the TOTAL):
5 + 7 + 9 + 11 = 32
Step 2 — count how many bags there are (the COUNT):
4 bags
Step 3 — divide the TOTAL by the COUNT:
32 ÷ 4 = 8
Average = 8 marbles per bag

⚠️ The average doesn't have to be one of the actual bag counts (it wasn't 5, 7, 9, or 11 here) — it's a NEW number that represents them all fairly.

Practice — Finding An Average

Add up the numbers, then divide by how many there are.

P1.  A gardener counted 6, 10, and 17 flowers in three vases. What's the average number of flowers per vase?
Total: 6 + 10 + 17 = 33
Count: 3 vases
33 ÷ 3 = 11
P2.  A weather station recorded 3, 5, 9, 4, and 14 mm of rainfall over 5 days. What's the average rainfall per day?
Total: 3 + 5 + 9 + 4 + 14 = 35
Count: 5 days
35 ÷ 5 = 7

Move 2 — Reverse It: Find The Total

TOTAL = AVERAGE × COUNT   (the same rule, flipped around)

The average number of customers at a food stall is 12 per hour. If the stall was open for 5 hours, how many customers visited in total?

Step 1 — write down what you know:
average = 12 customers per hour, count = 5 hours
Step 2 — multiply the average by the count:
12 × 5 = 60
Total = 60 customers

⚠️ This is the exact opposite of Move 1. Move 1: total ÷ count = average. Move 2: average × count = total. Same three numbers, just asking for a different one.

Move 3 — Find A Missing Value

1) TOTAL = AVERAGE × COUNT   2) MISSING = TOTAL − (numbers you know, added up)

Four numbers have an average of 15. Three of them are 10, 16, and 20. What is the fourth number?

Step 1 — find the TOTAL of all four numbers:
15 × 4 = 60
Step 2 — add up the three numbers you already know:
10 + 16 + 20 = 46
Step 3 — subtract to find the missing number:
60 − 46 = 14
The fourth number is 14

⚠️ You can't find a missing value straight from the average alone — you always need to find the TOTAL first (Move 2), THEN subtract what you know.

Practice — Finding A Missing Value

Find the total first, then subtract the numbers you know.

P1.  4 kids' heights average 130 cm. Three of them measure 125, 128, and 135 cm. What is the 4th kid's height?
Total: 130 × 4 = 520 cm
Known: 125 + 128 + 135 = 388 cm
520 − 388 = 132 cm
P2.  5 baskets average 18 apples each. Four baskets have 15, 20, 16, and 22 apples. How many apples are in the 5th basket?
Total: 18 × 5 = 90 apples
Known: 15 + 20 + 16 + 22 = 73 apples
90 − 73 = 17 apples

Move 4 — When The Average Changes

BEFORE total = before average × before count  ·  AFTER total = after average × after count

A — Someone LEAVES

5 kids have an average weight of 30 kg. One kid leaves the group. The remaining 4 kids now have an average weight of 32 kg. How heavy was the kid who left?

Step 1 — find the BEFORE total (all 5 kids):
30 × 5 = 150 kg
Step 2 — find the AFTER total (remaining 4 kids):
32 × 4 = 128 kg
Step 3 — subtract to find who left (BEFORE − AFTER):
150 − 128 = 22
The kid who left weighed 22 kg

B — Someone JOINS

3 cyclists have an average speed of 20 km/h. A 4th cyclist joins them. The new average speed of all 4 cyclists is 22 km/h. How fast was the 4th cyclist?

Step 1 — find the BEFORE total (3 cyclists):
20 × 3 = 60 km/h
Step 2 — find the AFTER total (4 cyclists):
22 × 4 = 88 km/h
Step 3 — subtract to find the new cyclist's speed (AFTER − BEFORE):
88 − 60 = 28
The 4th cyclist's speed was 28 km/h

⚠️ Same 3 steps every time — BEFORE total, AFTER total, then subtract. The only thing that changes is WHICH WAY you subtract: someone LEAVING → BEFORE minus AFTER. Someone JOINING → AFTER minus BEFORE. Easy check: the BIGGER total minus the SMALLER total always gives a sensible positive answer.

⭐ Your Turn — Move 4

Practice — When The Average Changes

Find the BEFORE total, find the AFTER total, then subtract the smaller from the bigger.

P1.  7 volunteers picked an average of 40 kg of oranges each at a farm. The one who picked the least left early, and the remaining 6 volunteers' average rose to 42 kg. How many kg did the volunteer who left pick?
BEFORE total: 40 × 7 = 280 kg
AFTER total: 42 × 6 = 252 kg
Left early (BEFORE − AFTER): 280 − 252 = 28 kg
P2.  3 taxi drivers gave an average of 18 rides each in a day. A 4th driver joined the shift, and the new average across all 4 drivers rose to 21 rides. How many rides did the 4th driver give?
BEFORE total: 18 × 3 = 54 rides
AFTER total: 21 × 4 = 84 rides
Joined (AFTER − BEFORE): 84 − 54 = 30 rides

🗺️ The Full Checklist

1Find an average: TOTAL ÷ COUNT.
2Find a total from an average: AVERAGE × COUNT.
3Find a missing value: find the TOTAL (Move 2), then subtract the numbers you already know.
4Find what changed (someone joined/left): BEFORE total, AFTER total, then subtract the smaller from the bigger.

⚠️ Before you calculate anything, ask yourself: "Do I have the average and need the numbers, or do I have the numbers and need the average?" That tells you which direction to use the rule.

📖 Today's Big Idea

Average = Total ÷ Count is ONE rule with three numbers. Whichever number is missing — average, total, or one item in the list — you can always find it, as long as you know the other two.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You can find an average by adding up and dividing by the count
  • You remember Total = Average × Count is the SAME rule, just flipped
  • For a missing value, you find the total FIRST, then subtract what you know
  • When the average changes, you find BOTH totals (before and after) before subtracting

All four moves use the exact same rule. Once you trust Average = Total ÷ Count, you can solve any of them.
