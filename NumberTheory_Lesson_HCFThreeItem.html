🔢 HCF Three-Item Applied

You can find HCF of two numbers perfectly. But in U14, Q9★ gave you three quantities and you gave up. Today we extend HCF to three items — it's the same idea, just one extra step.

Unit 15 · Number Theory · Lesson 3

🎯 What's New Today

HCF of 2 items: find the biggest number that divides into both.
HCF of 3 items: find the biggest number that divides into all three.

The method is identical — just extend your prime factorisation to all three numbers and take the lowest power of each common prime.

Keywords that signal HCF: "equal groups", "identical pieces", "no leftovers", "largest/greatest", "cut into equal", "each bag/box/set gets the same"

🔁 Warm-Up: Two-Item HCF Review

Quick review of the method you already know.

HCF(24, 36): 24 = 2³ × 3 36 = 2² × 3² Common primes: 2 and 3 Take lowest power: 2² × 3 = 4 × 3 = 12 HCF(24, 36) = 12

WU1. Find HCF(45, 75).

45 = 3² × 5    75 = 3 × 5²

HCF =
Answer: 15 — Common primes: 3 and 5. Lowest powers: 3¹ × 5¹ = 15.

🔑 The Three-Item HCF Method

Extending to 3 numbers: factorise all three, then take the lowest power of each prime that appears in all three.

HCF(30, 45, 60): 30 = 2 × 3 × 5 45 = 3² × 5 60 = 2² × 3 × 5 Common primes (in ALL three): 3 and 5 Lowest power of 3: 3¹ (appears as 3¹ in 30 and 60, 3² in 45 → take 3¹) Lowest power of 5: 5¹ HCF = 3 × 5 = 15
Key step: a prime must appear in ALL THREE numbers to be in the HCF. If it's missing from even one, it drops out.
3-Item HCF Steps:
1. Prime factorise all three numbers
2. Circle primes that appear in ALL three
3. Take the lowest power of each circled prime
4. Multiply them together → that's your HCF

✏️ Practice — Three-Item HCF

P1. A florist has 48 red roses, 72 white roses, and 96 pink roses. She wants to make identical bouquets using all flowers, no flowers left over. What is the greatest number of bouquets she can make?

48 = 2⁴ × 3 72 = 2³ × 3² 96 = 2⁵ × 3 Common primes: 2 and 3. Lowest powers: ?
Greatest number of bouquets =
Answer: 24 — Lowest power of 2: 2³=8. Lowest power of 3: 3¹=3. HCF = 8×3 = 24 bouquets. Each has 48/24=2 red, 72/24=3 white, 96/24=4 pink.

Answer: 28 cm — 56=2³×7, 84=2²×3×7, 140=2²×5×7. Common: 2² and 7. HCF = 4×7 = 28.

🎒 Applied: Bags, Sets, Groups

The most common test format: you're told three item quantities and asked to find the maximum number of identical bags/sets/groups with no items left over. The answer is always HCF of the three quantities.

Max identical groups = HCF(quantity 1, quantity 2, quantity 3)

P3. A store has 60 toy cars, 90 building blocks, and 45 puzzles. The store wants to pack them into identical gift bags with no items left over, using as many bags as possible. How many bags can be packed?

Number of bags =
Answer: 15 — 60=2²×3×5, 90=2×3²×5, 45=3²×5. Common: 3 and 5. Lowest: 3¹×5 = 15. Each bag: 60/15=4 cars, 90/15=6 blocks, 45/15=3 puzzles.

Answer: 30 packs

✅ HCF Three-Item — Done!

The Rule:
1. Prime factorise all THREE numbers
2. Only keep primes that appear in ALL three
3. Take the LOWEST power of each
4. Multiply → HCF

Keyword triggers: equal groups · identical bags · no leftovers · largest piece · greatest number of sets
