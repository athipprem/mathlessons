🔄 Two New Twists On Arranging Things

Why learn this?

You know the factorial rule: n different items in a row → n! arrangements. This lesson covers TWO situations where that plain rule needs a twist. First: what if some items are IDENTICAL, like the letters in "DAD"? Second: what if the items aren't in a line at all, but around a CIRCLE, like friends at a round table? Both twists show up constantly in competition problems — and by the end, you'll even combine both twists in one question.

Part 1: arranging with repeats. Part 2: arranging in a circle. Then: both together.
Part 1 of 2

Arranging With Repeats

The factorial rule (n!) assumes every item is DIFFERENT. But if some items are identical twins (same letter, same colour), swapping just those two twins produces an arrangement that LOOKS exactly the same as before — so n! counts some arrangements more than once.

Count all n! orderings, then DIVIDE by the number of ways each repeated group could swap among itself (r!) — because those swaps don't create a new-looking arrangement.

⚠️ Only divide for items that are truly IDENTICAL. Two different letters are NOT the same item, even if they look similar.

Worked Example 1 — "DAD"

How many different-looking ways can the letters of "DAD" be arranged?

Step 1 — count total letters: D, A, D → n = 3
Step 2 — find repeats: D appears 2 times.
Step 3 — start with n!: 3! = 6
Step 4 — divide by r! for the repeated letter: 6 ÷ 2! = 6 ÷ 2 = 3
3 arrangements: ADD, DAD, DDA

Worked Example 2 — "BALL"

How many different-looking ways can the letters of "BALL" be arranged?

Total letters: B, A, L, L → n = 4. L repeats 2 times.
n! = 4! = 24. Divide by 2! → 24 ÷ 2 = 12
12 arrangements
Formula: n! ÷ (r₁! × r₂! × ...) — one r! for EACH letter that repeats.

5! ÷ 3! = 120 ÷ 6 = 20

Part 2 of 2

Arranging In A Circle

Lining people up in a row gives n! orderings. But around a ROUND table, rotating everyone by one seat does NOT create a new arrangement — the neighbours are all still the same. There are exactly n rotations of any circle, so we take n! and divide by n.

n! ÷ n = (n−1)! — or: FIX one person in one seat, then arrange everyone else around them in a line.

⚠️ Flipping the circle over (mirror image) usually counts as DIFFERENT — only ROTATIONS are "the same."

Worked Example 1 — 4 Friends, Round Table

Alice, Ben, Cy, and Dee sit around a round table. How many DIFFERENT circular seatings are possible?

n = 4. Fix Alice's seat — arrange the other 3 around her: (n−1)! = 3!
3! = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6
6 different circular seatings

🗺️ The Full Plan

1Is it in a straight LINE or a CIRCLE? Line → start with n!. Circle → fix one seat, start with (n−1)!.
2Are any items IDENTICAL (repeated)? If yes, divide by r! for EACH repeated group.
3Line + repeats: n! ÷ (r₁! × r₂! × ...)
4Circle + repeats: (n−1)! ÷ (r₁! × r₂! × ...)
5Always check circle-vs-line FIRST, repeats SECOND.

⚠️ Missing either check (circle vs line, or repeats) gives the wrong answer.

⭐ Capstone — Combining Both Ideas

6 Friends, Round Table, 2 Are Identical Twins

6 friends sit around a round table. Two of them are identical twins. How many DIFFERENT-looking circular seatings are there?

Step 1 — it's a circle: n = 6 → (6−1)! = 5! = 120
Step 2 — check for repeats: the 2 twins are identical → divide by 2!
120 ÷ 2! = 120 ÷ 2 = 60
60 different-looking circular seatings
Circle step FIRST, then repeats step: (n−1)! ÷ (r₁! × r₂! × ...)

📖 Two Twists — in one line

Repeats: n! ÷ r!. Circles: (n−1)!. Both together: (n−1)! ÷ (r₁! × r₂! × ...)

Check circle-vs-line first, repeats second.
