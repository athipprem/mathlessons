🔍 What Happened in Milestone Q18?

Milestone Q18: The sum of 5 consecutive whole numbers is 100. What is the largest of the 5 numbers?

What you tried: listing numbers

16, 17, 18, 19, 20 → sum = 90. Not quite 100...

You ran out of time before finding the answer.

The smart way — takes 5 seconds:

Middle number = Total ÷ Count = 100 ÷ 5 = 20

So the 5 numbers are: 18, 19, 20, 21, 22. Largest = 22.

💡 The key insight: In any set of consecutive numbers, the middle number equals the average. Average = sum ÷ count. This lesson teaches you this and more.

Arithmetic Sequences — The Constant Jump

An arithmetic sequence is a list of numbers where each term increases by the same amount — called the common difference (d).

5
+4→
9
+4→
13
+4→
17
+4→
21

Common difference d = 4. Each jump is the same.

nth term = first term + (n − 1) × d
Finding the 10th term
1
First term = 5, d = 4, n = 10
2
10th term = 5 + (10−1) × 4 = 5 + 36 = 41

Quick Check

Sequence: 3, 8, 13, 18, 23, ... What is the 8th term?

d = 5. 8th term = 3 + (8−1)×5 = 3 + 35 = 38

Consecutive Numbers — The Middle Trick

Consecutive numbers are numbers that follow each other: like 7, 8, 9, 10, 11. The common difference is always 1.

For consecutive numbers with an odd count:

n−2
n−1
n
n+1
n+2

Sum = 5n  →  middle = Sum ÷ count

M1 Q18 — Solved in 3 steps
1
5 consecutive numbers, sum = 100
2
Middle = 100 ÷ 5 = 20
3
Numbers: 18, 19, 20, 21, 22. Largest = 22
⚠️ This works when the count is odd (3, 5, 7 numbers). For even counts — see next screen!

Even Count & Consecutive Even Numbers

When count is even — use algebra

4 consecutive numbers: let them be n, n+1, n+2, n+3.

Sum of 4 consecutive = 58. Find the largest.
1
n + (n+1) + (n+2) + (n+3) = 58
2
4n + 6 = 58 → 4n = 52 → n = 13
3
Numbers: 13, 14, 15, 16. Largest = 16

Consecutive Even (or Odd) Numbers

Consecutive even numbers go up by 2: n, n+2, n+4, n+6...

Sum of 4 consecutive even numbers = 60. Find the smallest.
1
n + (n+2) + (n+4) + (n+6) = 60
2
4n + 12 = 60 → 4n = 48 → n = 12
3
Numbers: 12, 14, 16, 18. Smallest = 12

Quick Check

Sum of 5 consecutive even numbers = 80. What is the smallest?

n, n+2, n+4, n+6, n+8. Sum=5n+20=80. 5n=60. n=12. Smallest=12.

Finding Missing Terms — Use the Gap

If you know two terms and their positions, you can find d and work backwards to find any term.

The 4th term is 29. Each term increases by 6. Find the 1st term.
1
4th term = 29, d = 6
2
Go back 3 steps: 29 − 3×6 = 29 − 18 = 11
3
Verify: 11, 17, 23, 29 ✓

⭐ Hard type — finding d first:

3rd term = 15, 7th term = 35. Find d and the 1st term.
1
From 3rd to 7th = 4 steps. Increase = 35 − 15 = 20
2
d = 20 ÷ 4 = 5
3
1st term = 15 − 2×5 = 5
4
Sequence: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35 ✓

Four things to remember:

1
Arithmetic sequence: nth term = first + (n−1) × d
2
Consecutive numbers (odd count): middle = sum ÷ count
3
Consecutive even/odd: let first = n, then n, n+2, n+4... set up equation
4
Find d: d = (later term − earlier term) ÷ (number of steps between them)
