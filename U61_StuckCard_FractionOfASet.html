Before you read anything below — ask yourself:

"Which number is the WHOLE thing? And what does the BOTTOM of the fraction tell me to do to it?"

Say your answer out loud. Then scroll down and check.

(If you already know, you don't need the rest of this page — go answer the question!)

🧮 Go And Get 24 Counters

Or coins, Lego, buttons — anything you can count. Try this with the question "what is ⅔ of 24?"

  1. Count out 24 counters. That's your WHOLE.
  2. The bottom number of ⅔ is 3. Split your 24 counters into 3 equal piles. Each pile should have 8 in it.
  3. The top number of ⅔ is 2. Push 2 of your 3 piles together — leave the third pile alone.
  4. Count what's in your pushed-together piles: 16. That's your answer: ⅔ of 24 = 16.

Two Contrasted Examples, Side By Side

❌ Just "24 ÷ 3" on its own
24 ÷ 3 = 8
This only tells you what ONE pile is worth (one third). It stops too early if the question asked for ⅔.
✅ "⅔ of 24"
Step 1: 24 ÷ 3 = 8  (one third)
Step 2: 8 × 2 = 16  (two thirds)
Dividing by the bottom only gets you ONE piece. You must then multiply by the top to get the number of pieces the question actually asked for.

⚠️ The single most common slip: stopping after the division. If the top number of the fraction isn't 1, you're not done yet.

Now You Try

1.  What is ¾ of 20?
2.  ⅗ of a number is 18. What is the number?

You've got it if you can say WHY you divide first and multiply second (or, for question 2, why you divide by the TOP and multiply by the BOTTOM) — not just get both numbers right.
