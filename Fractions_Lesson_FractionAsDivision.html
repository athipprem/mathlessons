R1.  ⅔ of 21 conkers are shiny. How many are shiny?
21 ÷ 3 = 7.   7 × 2 = 14
R2 (chained).  ¾ of ⅖ of 40 stickers are gold. How many are gold?
⅖ of 40 = 16 first. Then ¾ of 16 = 12
R3 (reverse).  ⅛ of a number is 16. What is the number?
16 ÷ 4 = 4 (one ninth).   4 × 9 = 36

📐 Route B — See It As A Rule

Same idea as the pizza screen, written a different way. If the pizzas made sense, this shows you how to write it down.
a ÷ b = a/b  —  just write the first number on top, the second on the bottom.

Worked example: Write 9 ÷ 4 as a fraction, then as a mixed number.

Step 1 — write it straight across, top and bottom:
9 ÷ 4 = 9/4
Step 2 — 9/4 is an IMPROPER fraction (top bigger than bottom). To turn it into a mixed number, divide and find the remainder:
9 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 1
Step 3 — the whole-number part is 2. The remainder (1) becomes the new top number, over the same bottom number (4):
9/4 = 2¼

💡 Notice this matches the pizza idea: 9 pizza-quarters shared out makes 2 whole pizzas with 1 quarter left over.

🔗 This Is Lesson 1, Flipped

Same idea, written the other way round

Lesson 1 was "a FRACTION of a GROUP" — you started with a fraction and a total, and found a part. Today's idea is "one NUMBER divided by ANOTHER" — you start with two whole numbers, and the answer itself IS the fraction.

Lesson 1 direction:
⅔ of 24 = 16  (fraction × total → a part)
Today's direction:
3 ÷ 4 = ¾  (number ÷ number → a fraction)
Same machinery, opposite job: Lesson 1 turns a fraction INTO a number. Today turns a division INTO a fraction.

✂️ Dividing A FRACTION By A Whole Number

One more shape of question

What if you already have a fraction of something, and THAT gets shared further? Example: half a cake is left, and it gets shared equally between 3 people.

The shaded ½ now gets cut into 3 equal smaller pieces (one per person)

Worked example: ½ of a cake is shared equally between 3 people. What fraction of the WHOLE cake does each person get?

Step 1 — sharing a fraction between more people means the bottom number gets MULTIPLIED by however many people:
(½) ÷ 3 = 1 / (2 × 3)
Step 2 — work out the new bottom number:
2 × 3 = 6
Each person gets 1/6 of the whole cake
Rule: (a/b) ÷ c = a / (b × c)
⭐ Your Turn

Practice — Fraction as Division

P1.  Write 13 ÷ 4 as a fraction, then as a mixed number.
13/4 = 3¼ (13÷4 = 3 remainder 1)
P2.  5 cakes are shared equally among 6 children. What fraction of a cake does each child get?
5 ÷ 6 = 5/6 of a cake each
P3 (fraction ÷ whole number).  ⅓ of a cake is shared equally between 2 people. What fraction of the WHOLE cake does each person get?
(⅓) ÷ 2 = 1/(3×2) = 1/6
P4 (Word Problem).  A rope 7 m long is cut into 3 equal pieces. How long is each piece? Give your answer as a mixed number.
7 ÷ 3 = 7/3 = 2⅓ m (2 remainder 1)
📖 Today's Big Idea

a ÷ b is always the same as a/b — just write the first number on top. If the fraction is improper (top bigger than bottom), divide to find the whole-number part and put the remainder over the same bottom number. To share a fraction further between c people: (a/b) ÷ c = a/(b×c).
