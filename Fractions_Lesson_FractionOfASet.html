🎯 Why Learn This?

You use this every time something gets shared

Three friends split a £24 bill and you have to pay 2/3 of it. A shop sign says "2/3 off" a £30 jacket. A bag has 30 sweets and you're told 5/6 of them are yours. In every one of these, you need to find PART of a GROUP — without lining everything up and counting one by one.

This is a real superpower

Once you know the trick, "2/3 of 24" takes about 3 seconds — no counters, no drawing, no listing every item. It works whether the group has 6 things or 6,000.

Today's superpower: find part of any group, instantly, without counting one by one.

📐 Route B — See It As A Rule

Same idea as the last screen, written a different way. If the counters made sense, this shows you how to write it down. If they didn't quite click, try this route instead — some people find the written rule easier.
DIVIDE by the bottom number, then MULTIPLY by the top number.

Worked example: Find 2/3 of 24.

Step 1 — the bottom number (3) tells you how many EQUAL piles to split into. Divide the total by it:
24 ÷ 3 = 8  (this is ONE third)
Step 2 — the top number (2) tells you how MANY of those piles you want. Multiply:
8 × 2 = 16  (this is TWO thirds)
2/3 of 24 = 16

💡 Notice this matches the counters screen: 24 counters split into 3 piles of 8, then take 2 of those piles → 16. Same answer, two ways of seeing it.

🔗 A Fraction OF A Fraction

What if there are two fractions?

Sometimes a question gives you a fraction of a fraction — like "2/3 of 3/4 of 24 stickers are gold." You do it in exactly two steps: find the FIRST fraction of the total, then find the SECOND fraction of THAT answer.

Step 1 — do the FIRST fraction of the total:
3/4 of 24 → 24 ÷ 4 = 6,   6 × 3 = 18
Step 2 — do the SECOND fraction of your Step 1 answer (not the original 24!):
2/3 of 18 → 18 ÷ 3 = 6,   6 × 2 = 12
2/3 of 3/4 of 24 = 12

⚠️ The most common mistake: doing BOTH fractions of the original 24. Always use your Step 1 answer as the new "total" for Step 2.

🔄 Working Backwards — Find The Whole

The question can flip around

So far you've been given the WHOLE and asked to find a PART. This time it's flipped: you're given the PART and told which fraction it is, and you have to find the WHOLE. It uses the exact same tools, just in reverse order.

Worked example: 2/3 of a number is 16. What is the number?

Step 1 — "2/3 of a number is 16" means 2 EQUAL piles are worth 16 altogether. Find what ONE pile (1/3) is worth by dividing:
16 ÷ 2 = 8  (one third of the number is 8)
Step 2 — the WHOLE number is made of 3 of those piles (not 2!) — because the whole is always 3/3. Multiply by 3:
8 × 3 = 24
The number is 24
Check it: is 2/3 of 24 equal to 16? 24 ÷ 3 = 8, and 8 × 2 = 16. ✓ Yes!

💡 The trick for reverse questions: divide by the TOP number (not the bottom) to find "one piece," then multiply by the BOTTOM number (the total number of pieces in the whole) to rebuild the whole. Notice this is screen 2's fact (2/3 of 24 = 16) run backwards.

Practice — Fraction of a Set

P1.  5/8 of a box of 24 cupcakes have sprinkles. How many have sprinkles?
24 ÷ 8 = 3 (one eighth).   3 × 5 = 15
= 15 cupcakes
P2 (chained).  3/4 of 2/3 of 18 apples are ripe. How many apples are ripe?
2/3 of 18 = 12 first. Then 3/4 of 12: 12÷4=3, 3×3=9
= 9 apples
P3 (reverse).  3/5 of a number is 21. What is the number?
21 ÷ 3 = 7 (one fifth).   7 × 5 = 35
= 35
P4 (Word Problem).  A baker used 2/5 of his flour to bake bread, using 8 kg. (a) How many kg of flour did he have to start with? (b) How many kg does he have left?
8 kg is 2/5 of the total. One fifth = 8÷2=4. Whole = 4×5=20 kg
(a) 20 kg   (b) 20 − 8 = 12 kg left
📖 Today's Big Idea

To find a fraction of a set: divide by the bottom, multiply by the top. For a fraction of a fraction, do it in two steps — using your first answer as the new total. To work BACKWARDS from a part to the whole, divide by the top number, then multiply by the bottom.
