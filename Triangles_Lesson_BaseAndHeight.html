Chapter 4 · Base and height

▲ Base And Height

Why this is worth learning. Triangles are everywhere: a sail, a roof end, a flag, a slice of pizza, a garden corner. To know how much cloth, paint or soil they need, you need their area — and for that you need one special line: the height. Today you learn to find it on any triangle, even a leaning one.

Chapter 4! You already know the big rule from U60: a triangle is half its box. Today’s one new thing: finding the HEIGHT on ANY triangle.

Your 📌 Triangle Card is open. Each worked line says which card line it uses.

Worked example:

Warm-up win (U60): this right-angled triangle has sides 11 cm and 6 cm meeting at a square corner. Find its area.

Base = 11 cm▲1 the bottom side, AB
Height = 6 cm▲2 AC stands straight up from AB — the square corner at A
Rectangle = 11 × 6▲3 base × height = the box the triangle fits in
= 6611 × 6 = 66
Area = 66 ÷ 2▲4 the triangle is half of the box
= 33 cm²66 ÷ 2 = 33; area is in square units

Route B · The lines

📏 The Lines

Slanted sides are not heights. Only the dashed line that meets the base at a square corner is.

Is this line the height?
Does it go straight up from the opposite corner?No → not a height (a slanted side)
Does it meet the base line at a square corner ⊾?No → not a height
Yes to both▲2 It is the height — use it
The top corner hangs past the end of the base?Stretch the base line. The height is outside.

Worked example:

Base AB is 28 cm. The sides 17 cm and 25 cm are slanted. The dashed line is 15 cm. Find the area.

Base = 28 cm▲1 base AB
Height = 15 cm▲2 the dashed line meets AB at a square corner; 17 and 25 are slanted sides, NOT heights
Rectangle = 28 × 15▲3 base × height = the box the triangle fits in
= 42028 × 15 = 420
Area = 420 ÷ 2▲4 the triangle is half of the box
= 210 cm²420 ÷ 2 = 210; area is in square units

Worked example:

A triangular sail has a base of 1 1/2 m and a height of 2 1/3 m. Find its area.

Base = 1 1/2 m▲1 the base
Height = 2 1/3 m▲2 the height
Rectangle = 1 1/2 × 2 1/3▲3 base × height
= 3/2 × 2 1/3improper first: 1 1/2 = 3/2 (1 whole = 2 halves, plus 1 half = 3 halves)
= 3/2 × 7/3improper first: 2 1/3 = 7/3 (2 wholes = 6 thirds, plus 1 third = 7 thirds)
= 21/6tops: 3 × 7 = 21; bottoms: 2 × 3 = 6
= 7/2simplify: divide the top and the bottom by 3
Area = 7/2 ÷ 2▲4 the triangle is half of the box
= 7/4half of 7 halves = 7 quarters: double the bottom
= 1 3/4 m²7 quarters = 1 whole and 3 quarters over; square units
The picture and the lines do the same thing: find the box, then take half.
The leaning triangle

📐 The Leaning Triangle

Here the top corner hangs past the end of the base. Stretch the base line — the height is OUTSIDE.

Worked example:

This triangle leans over. Base AB is 4 cm, the slanted sides are 15 cm and 13 cm, and the height is 12 cm. Find the area — then check it a second way.

Big right-angled triangle A-F-C (shaded) take away the small one B-F-C (white): what is left is triangle ABC.

Base = 4 cm▲1 base AB (only 4 cm)
Height = 12 cm▲2 C hangs past B: stretch the base line; the dashed line meets it at a square corner, outside the triangle
Rectangle = 4 × 12▲3 base × height = the box the triangle fits in
= 484 × 12 = 48
Area = 48 ÷ 2▲4 the triangle is half of the box
= 24 cm²48 ÷ 2 = 24; area is in square units
Big triangle = 9 × 12check: the big right-angled triangle from A to the foot of the dashed line, as a box
= 1089 × 12 = 108
Big half = 108 ÷ 2half the box
= 54108 ÷ 2 = 54
Small triangle = 5 × 12the small right-angled triangle from B to the foot (9 − 4 = 5 long), as a box

Small half = 60 ÷ 2half the box

Triangle ABD has the same base AB (4 cm) and its top corner D is 12 cm above AB too. Find its area. What do you notice?

📌 MY CARDS
THE TRIANGLE CARD
▲1 Pick a BASE — any side can be the base.
▲2 Its HEIGHT = the straight-up line from the opposite corner down to the base line. It meets the base line at a square corner ⊾. A slanted side is NOT the height. If the top corner hangs past the end of the base, stretch the base line — the height is outside.
▲3 Rectangle = base × height (the box the triangle fits in).
▲4 Area = rectangle ÷ 2 (the triangle is half the box). Write square units: cm², m².
Fractions use the × side of the Fraction Card, then halve: half of 7 halves = 7 quarters (double the bottom).
THE BIG PICTURE
① Taken part = fraction × whole “1/4 OF the juice”
Told? “3/4 L” → Taken part = 3/4 L. No × needed!
1/3 OF 12 bricks: the bricks split into 3 equal groups, one group lights up, giving 4.
1/3 × 12 = 12/3 = 4. ‘Of’ means ×.
1/3 kg is just a small lump — that’s TOLD, not ×.
② Left part = whole − taken part
③ “… of what was LEFT”? → the left part is the NEW whole. Back to ①.
④ LAST CHECK: read the question again — does it ask for a TAKEN part, the LEFT part, or HOW MUCH MORE?
“How much more?” = bigger part − smaller part (that’s the − side too)
×0 Mixed number? Make it improper.
2 1/4 = 9/4   Whole number? 12 = 12/1
×1 top × top, bottom × bottom
×2 Simplify → mixed number
Example:
5/6 × 1 4/5
= 5/6 × 9/5 ×0
= 45/30 ×1
= 3/2 ×2
= 1 1/2 ×2
−0 Mixed number? Make it improper.
−1 Make the bottoms the same.
Count up in the BIGGER bottom until the smaller one fits:
thirds & quarters → 4, 8, 12 ✓ → twelfths
−2 top − top (the bottom stays!)
−3 Simplify → mixed number
Example:
3 1/2 − 3/4
= 7/2 − 3/4 −0
= 14/4 − 3/4 −1
= 11/4 −2
= 2 3/4 −3
+0 Mixed? Make it improper.
+1 Same bottoms (count up in the bigger bottom).
+2 top + top (bottom stays!)
+3 Simplify → mixed number
÷ FRACTIONS ARE DIVISIONS
a ÷ b = a/b   5 ÷ 6 → 5/6 each   13 ÷ 4 = 13/4 = 3 1/4
Improper → mixed: top ÷ bottom = wholes, remainder = the new top.
. FRACTION → DECIMAL
Make the bottom 10, 100 or 1000:   3/4 = 75/100 = 0.75
Can’t? Divide top by bottom:   1/3 = 0.333…
≈ ROUND to 2 decimal places: look at the 3rd digit → 5 or more, round up. 0.333… ≈ 0.33
B WORKING BACKWARDS (“how much at FIRST?”)
1. Left fraction = 1 − taken fraction
2. That many units = the amount you know
3. 1 unit = amount ÷ units
4. Whole = 1 unit × the bottom number
Two rounds? Undo the LAST round first, then the first round.
