🎯 Why — Rooms, Rugs and Gardens

Rooms, rugs and gardens are almost never whole numbers of metres. A rug 2 1/2 m by 1 1/3 m — how much floor does it cover? You can't count squares, because the sides aren't whole. This lesson shows you how.

Change every mixed number into an improper fraction first — then multiply exactly like before.

📐 Route B — Convert, Then Multiply

Look at the 5 and the 4 you just counted — they ARE the tops when you change the mixed numbers into improper fractions. That's why we always convert first.

2 1/2 × 1 1/3

2 1/2 × 1 1/3 = 5/2 × 4/3 (2 1/2 = 5 halves; 1 1/3 = 4 thirds)
cancel 4 with 2 → 5/1 × 2/3
= 10/3
= 3 1/3

⚠️ The Trap: ADD or MULTIPLY?

Your strongest mixed-number habit, from adding, is: wholes with wholes, parts with parts. For multiplying, that habit gives a WRONG answer.

2 × 1 = 2
1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6
"answer" 2 1/6  ✗  WRONG

The rectangle splits into FOUR regions: 2 (whole×whole), 2/3, 1/2, 1/6. The wrong way only counted the first and last region.

True total = 2 + 2/3 + 1/2 + 1/6
= 3 1/3
Adding mixed numbers: wholes with wholes, parts with parts — fine. MULTIPLYING mixed numbers: change BOTH to improper fractions first. Always.

📖 Today's Big Idea

Multiplying mixed numbers: convert BOTH to improper fractions first, then multiply top×top, bottom×bottom, cancelling where you can. Never split into wholes and parts the way you do for adding.
