❓ "They drank 1/3 OF it — why can't I just take away 1/3?"

Before you read anything below — ask yourself:

"Is 1/3 OF the milk the same as 1/3 of a LITRE? Which is bigger if there's lots of milk?"

Say your answer out loud. Then scroll down and check.

🧱 Go And Get 12 Lego Bricks

12 bricks, all the same size.

  1. Each brick is 1/4 kg of clay, so all 12 bricks = 3 kg of clay (4 bricks make 1 kg, and 12 ÷ 4 = 3).
  2. "Use 1/3 OF the clay": make 3 equal piles and take one → 4 bricks = 1 kg.
  3. "Use 1/3 kg": that is not even 2 bricks. Same "1/3", very different amounts.

Two Contrasted Examples

✅ 1/3 OF 3 kg of clay
3 kg ÷ 3 = 1 kg (1/3 OF means split the 3 kg into 3 equal parts)
1/3 OF 3 kg = 1 kg ✓
🔶 3 − 1/3
3 − 1/3: break one whole into thirds, so 3 = 2 + 3/3 (then take 1/3 from the 3/3)
= 2 2/3 kg
took away 1/3 of a kg, not 1/3 of the clay.
"1/3 OF it" is a piece of the clay. "1/3 kg" is an amount you take away.

Now You Try

1.  A tub has 1 3/5 kg of butter. 1/4 OF the butter is used. How much is left?
2.  A tub has 1 3/5 kg of butter. 1/4 kg is used. How much is left?

You've got it if you can say which little word made the answers different.
