🌉 The Bridge — A Whole-Number Jug, The Lesson 2 Way

A jug has 33 L of juice. Ana drinks 2/3 of the juice.

Taken = 2/3 × 33"2/3 of the juice" means 2/3 × the whole
= 2/3 × 33/1LINE 2: write the whole as a fraction — here 33 = 33/1
= 66/3tops: 2 × 33 = 66; bottoms: 3 × 1 = 3
= 22 L66 ÷ 3 = 22
Left over = 33 − 22the whole take away the taken piece
= 11 L33 − 22 = 11

See line 2? Here it just writes 33 as 33/1. In Lesson 2, line 2 turns a mixed number into an improper fraction instead. Every other line works exactly like this.

📏 Route A — The Bar

A bottle has 2 4/5 L of juice. Tia drinks 1/4 of the juice.

Count the bottle in tenths (the faint marks), then group the tenths into 4 equal pieces. Shade 1 piece.

🟦 shaded = taken piece    ⬜ white = left-over piece

2 4/5 = 28/10count the bottle in tenths: 2 wholes = 20 tenths, 4/5 = 8 tenths, 20 + 8 = 28 tenths
28 ÷ 4 = 7cut the 28 tenths into 4 equal pieces: each piece is 7 tenths
Taken = 7/10 LTia drinks 1 piece = 7 tenths of a litre
28/10 − 7/10 = 21/10the whole take away the taken piece: 28 tenths − 7 tenths = 21 tenths
21/10 = 2 1/10 L20 tenths = 2 wholes, 1 tenth over
Taken piece = 7/10 L
Left-over piece = 2 1/10 L

🔢 Route B — The Same Bottle, As Numbers

Same as the bridge, just with a fraction bottle.
Taken = 1/4 × 2 4/5"1/4 of the juice" means 1/4 × the whole
= 1/4 × 14/52 4/5 = 14/5 (2 wholes = 10 fifths, plus 4 fifths = 14 fifths)
= 14/20tops: 1 × 14 = 14; bottoms: 4 × 5 = 20
= 7/10 Lsimplify: divide the top and the bottom by 2
Left over = 2 4/5 − 7/10the whole take away the taken piece
= 14/5 − 7/10change the whole to an improper fraction: 2 4/5 = 14/5 (2 wholes = 10 fifths, plus 4 fifths = 14 fifths)
= 28/10 − 7/10make the bottoms the same: 14/5 = 28/10 (top and bottom × 2)
= 21/1028 tenths take away 7 tenths = 21 tenths
= 2 1/10 L21 tenths = 2 wholes and 1 tenth over
Never 2 4/5 − 1/4 here! That takes away a quarter of a LITRE, not a quarter of the JUICE.

🗺 The Map Again

One walk on the bottle story: Tia drinks 1/4 of the 2 4/5 L of juice. Tap Next step.

START Find the WHOLE (what was there at the start). Draw the bar. Write the whole on top.
▼
Q1 Does the story TELL me the taken piece?Look right after the fraction: is there a unit (L, kg, m)?
▼
YES — "drinks 1/3 L" → Taken = 1/3 L. Jump to Q3.
Example: A bottle has 12 L. Ben drinks 1/3 L. The taken piece is 1/3 L.
NO — "drinks 1/3 of the juice" → work it out. Go to Q2.
▼
Q2 Is the whole a whole number, or a fraction bottle?
▼
WHOLE NUMBER (12 L) → One piece = whole ÷ bottom number. Taken = one piece × top number.
Example: 1/3 of 12 L: one piece 12 ÷ 3, taken 4 × 1.
FRACTION BOTTLE (2 4/5 L) → Taken = fraction × whole, then one line each: change the whole to an improper fraction · tops × tops, bottoms × bottoms · simplify · back to a mixed number.
Example: 1/4 of 2 4/5 L: 1/4 × 14/5, then 14/20, then 7/10 L.
▼
Q3 Which box does the question ask for?
▼
"How much does he drink / use / cut off?" → the TAKEN box. Done.
"How much is left?" → Left over = whole − taken, then one line each: improper fractions · same bottoms · take away · simplify · mixed number.
▼
BONUS Does the story keep going: "then … of what was left"? The left-over piece becomes the NEW whole. Go back to START.
