Before We Start — You Already Know This

Why this is worth learning. A shop sells 1/5 of its rice on Monday, then 2/3 of what is left on Tuesday. A baker uses some flour in the morning, then part of what is left in the afternoon. Real stories go on in rounds like this — and each round starts from the piece the last round left behind.

In U66 you got every one-step story right. Today: ONE new idea, in a short lesson. And one habit — read (a) again before you write (a), because two right numbers in the wrong boxes score 0.

Two quick ones, no teaching on this page.

1.  Ben has 35 L of juice. He drinks 2/5 of the juice. (a) How much juice does he drink? (b) How much juice is left?
One piece = 35 ÷ 5cut the 35 L bar into 5 equal pieces
= 735 ÷ 5 = 7, so one piece is 7 L
Taken in round 1 = 7 × 2the taken piece is 2 of those pieces (the top number)
= 14 L7 × 2 = 14
Left after round 1 = 35 − 14the whole take away the taken piece
= 21 L35 − 14 = 21
2.  Eva has 2 2/5 L of juice. She drinks 3/8 of the juice. (a) How much juice does she drink? (b) How much juice is left?
Taken in round 1 = 3/8 × 2 2/5"3/8 of the juice" means 3/8 × the whole
= 3/8 × 12/52 2/5 = 12/5 (2 wholes = 10 fifths, plus 2 fifths = 12 fifths)
= 36/40tops: 3 × 12 = 36; bottoms: 8 × 5 = 40
= 9/10 Lsimplify: divide the top and the bottom by 4
Left after round 1 = 2 2/5 − 9/10the whole take away the taken piece
= 12/5 − 9/10change the whole to an improper fraction: 2 2/5 = 12/5 (2 wholes = 10 fifths, plus 2 fifths = 12 fifths)
= 24/10 − 9/10make the bottoms the same: 12/5 = 24/10 (top and bottom × 2)
= 15/1024 tenths take away 9 tenths = 15 tenths
= 3/2simplify: divide the top and the bottom by 5
= 1 1/2 L3 halves = 1 whole and 1 half over

📏 Two Bars

Amy has 32 L of juice. She drinks 1/4 of the juice. Then she drinks 3/4 of the juice that was left. How much juice is left?

Bar 1 is the 32 L, cut into 4 pieces with 1 shaded. Then the left-over piece slides down and becomes bar 2 — the NEW whole.

⚠️ Spot The Trap

Same story as the last page: 32 L of juice, 1/4 first, then 3/4.

✅ The new whole
3/4 of what was LEFT = 3/4 × 24 = 18 L.
🔶 The old whole
3/4 of ALL the juice = 3/4 × 32 = 24 L → nothing left?! But the story says some is left.

💧 Fraction Bottles — Same Lines

Kim has 2 2/5 L of juice. She drinks 1/6 of the juice. Then she pours out 3/4 of the juice that was left. How much juice is left?

Watch for the glowing yellow line: that is where the NEW whole is written.

🟦 shaded = taken piece    ⬜ dashed green = left-over piece, which slides down and becomes the NEW whole

Taken in round 1 = 1/6 × 2 2/5"1/6 of the juice" means 1/6 × the whole
= 1/6 × 12/52 2/5 = 12/5 (2 wholes = 10 fifths, plus 2 fifths = 12 fifths)
= 12/30tops: 1 × 12 = 12; bottoms: 6 × 5 = 30
= 2/5 Lsimplify: divide the top and the bottom by 6
Left after round 1 = 2 2/5 − 2/5the whole take away the taken piece
= 12/5 − 2/5change the whole to an improper fraction: 2 2/5 = 12/5 (2 wholes = 10 fifths, plus 2 fifths = 12 fifths)
= 10/512 fifths take away 2 fifths = 10 fifths
= 2 L10 ÷ 5 = 2
New whole = 2 Lround 2: "of the juice that was LEFT" — the left-over piece is the NEW whole
One piece = 2 ÷ 4cut the 2 L bar into 4 equal pieces
= 1/22 ÷ 4 = 1/2, so one piece is 1/2 L
Taken in round 2 = 1/2 × 3the taken piece is 3 of those pieces (the top number)
= 1 1/2 L1/2 × 3 = 1 1/2
Left after round 2 = 2 − 1 1/2the whole take away the taken piece
= 2 − 3/2change the taken piece to an improper fraction: 1 1/2 = 3/2 (1 whole = 2 halves, plus 1 half = 3 halves)
= 4/2 − 3/2make the bottoms the same: 2 wholes = 4 halves
= 1/2 L4 halves take away 3 halves = 1 half
Nothing new — the U66 lines, then ‘New whole =’, then the U66 lines again.
Answer: 1/2 L of juice is left.
