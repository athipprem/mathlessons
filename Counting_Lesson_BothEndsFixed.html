The Swap Rule: Any time two named people are placed at "the ends" (even if the question says "one at each end"), they can swap — always ×2.
The Formula

Both-ends problems always follow one pattern.

n people in a row, 2 specific people at the ends:

→ End pair arrangements: 2 (they can swap)
→ Middle people: (n − 2)!
→ Total: 2 × (n − 2)!

For Q13 (5 people, A at either end): 2 × (5−2)! = 2 × 3! = 2 × 6 = 12? Wait —

Actually for Q13: A can be at either end, but only A goes to an end — the other end is free. That's a slightly different setup. Let's check both types:

One person, either end

n people, person A at an end.
A: 2 choices. Rest: (n−1)! ways.

2 × (n−1)!
Q13: 2 × 4! = 48 ✓

Two people, both ends

n people, A and B each at one end.
Pair: 2 ways to swap. Rest: (n−2)! ways.

2 × (n−2)!
Q14: 2 × 4! = 48 ✓

Both give 48 here because n is different (5 vs 6). The formulas are different — read which type the question is.

Both-Ends Fixed — Locked In!

The only thing that was missing was remembering the ×2 when two named people are at the ends.

One person at an end: 2 × (n−1)!
Two people at the ends: 2 × (n−2)!

Both multiply by 2. Always. Because they can always swap.
