🎯 Why Learn This?

You already know how to do everything in this lesson

You already know how to do everything in this lesson. Last test you got the 40-students question, the glasses question and the notebooks question right — all of them, on your own. So this lesson isn't new maths. It's the drawing your school uses, and once you can draw it, the hard questions later this term stop being hard.

The bar is just the whole thing, drawn as a rectangle, cut into equal parts.

📏 Route A — The Bar, Built

Cut it into the bottom number of parts. Shade the top number of them. Change the total or the number of parts, and click any part to shade or unshade it.

📐 Route B — The Same Thing In Numbers

Same job as the last screen, written instead of drawn. If the bar made sense, this shows you how to write it down. If it didn't, this one might click instead — then go back and look at the bar again.

Worked example: 3/8 of 40.

40 ÷ 8 = 5  (that is ONE part)
5 × 3 = 15  (three parts)
3/8 of 40 = 15
DIVIDE by the bottom number, then MULTIPLY by the top number.

🔄 Reverse — The Bar Does The Work

2/3 of a number is 16. What is the number?

💡 When you know a piece and want the whole, find ONE part first. Always one part first.

🔗 NOT, And Chained — On The Bar

30 students. 2/5 walk to school. Of those who do NOT walk, 1/3 come by bus. How many come by bus?

30 ÷ 5 = 6 per part.   2 parts walk = 12.   3 parts do NOT walk = 18.
New bar: 18 ÷ 3 = 6 per part.   1 part by bus = 6.
6 come by bus

💡 The second fraction is a fraction of the NEW bar, not the old one. That's why we draw the new bar.

⭐ Your Turn

Practice — The Bar Model

P1.  3/4 of 36 marbles are blue. How many are blue?
36 ÷ 4 = 9 (one part).   9 × 3 = 27
= 27 marbles
P2.  5/6 of a class of 54 students bring lunch. How many bring lunch?
54 ÷ 6 = 9 (one part).   9 × 5 = 45
= 45 students
P3 (reverse).  2/7 of a number is 12. What is the number?
12 ÷ 2 = 6 (one part).   6 × 7 = 42
= 42
P4.  A box holds 28 pencils. 3/7 are red. How many are NOT red?
28 ÷ 7 = 4 (one part).   Red = 4 × 3 = 12.   NOT red = 28 − 12 = 16
= 16 pencils
📖 Today's Big Idea

Cut the bar into the bottom number of parts. Shade the top number of them. To work backwards from a part to the whole, find ONE part first, then multiply by the bottom number to rebuild the whole.

Finish-the-question check: before you write your final answer — did you answer the EXACT question asked?
