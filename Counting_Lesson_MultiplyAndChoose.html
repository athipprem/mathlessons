🧮 Count Without Listing Them All

Competition questions often ask "how many ways…?" with numbers far too big to draw. The trick is to multiply the choices, and to know when order matters and when it does not. Three moves cover almost everything.

🔍 Are you BUILDING with stages, or CHOOSING a group?

✖️ MULTIPLY — "one from each group / stage after stage"

Number of ways = (choices at stage 1) × (choices at stage 2) × …   2 shirts × 3 shorts = 6 outfits.

🔢 ARRANGE — "in a row / order matters / first & second"

Fill the first slot, then the next from what's left: 5 × 4 × 3… Arrange 4 books = 4×3×2×1 = 24.

🤝 CHOOSE 2 — "pairs / handshakes / games / lines"

Order does NOT matter. Pairs from n people = n × (n−1) ÷ 2. (Divide by 2 because A–B is the same pair as B–A.)

The Multiplication Rule

If a choice happens in stages, multiply the number of options at each stage. One slot per stage.

Example: 2 hats, 3 scarves. How many hat-and-scarf sets?

3

💡 "Make a number / a code / an outfit, one part at a time" → multiply. If digits can repeat, each slot has the full set of choices (e.g. 4 digits, 2-digit number with repeats = 4 × 4 = 16).

Arrange in Order

When you place things in a row (or pick 1st, 2nd, 3rd…), each slot uses one up — so the choices count down.

Example: pick a Captain and a Goalkeeper from 4 players.

4
captain
×
3

=
12

Arrange ALL 4 in a row = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24← this is "4!"

⚠️ Captain-then-keeper, order MATTERS (Ann-captain is different from Ann-keeper) → multiply 4×3, do NOT divide by 2.

Choose 2 — Pairs & Handshakes

Now order does not matter: a handshake between A and B is the same as B and A. So we count the ordered ways and divide by 2.

Example: 4 people each shake hands once. How many handshakes?

ordered: each of 4 people shakes the other 3 → 4 × 3 = 12
but A–B = B–A, so ÷ 2 → 12 ÷ 2 = 6 handshakes← the formula!
🔑 Pairs from n things = n × (n − 1) ÷ 2

Handshakes, games where every pair plays once, lines through dots, choosing 2 from a group — all the same formula.

💡 Diagonals of a shape: count all pairs of corners, then subtract the sides (a side joins corners but isn't a diagonal). Hexagon: 6×5÷2 = 15 pairs, − 6 sides = 9 diagonals.

Practice

Building in stages → multiply. In a row / 1st & 2nd → count down. Pairs / handshakes → n×(n−1)÷2.

Q1.   A set lunch has 5 mains and 2 desserts. How many different main-and-dessert lunches are possible?
stages → multiply: 5 × 2 = 10
Q2.   In how many ways can 3 different trophies be arranged in a row on a shelf?
in a row → 3 × 2 × 1 = 6
Q3.   9 people are at a party and everyone shakes hands once with everyone else. How many handshakes happen?
pairs = 9 × 8 ÷ 2 = 72 ÷ 2 = 36
Q4.   From a club of 10 members, how many different pairs can be chosen to share a prize?
pairs = 10 × 9 ÷ 2 = 90 ÷ 2 = 45
📖 Counting on One Card
Stages / one each:MULTIPLY the choices (2 × 3 = 6). In a row / order:count down: 5 × 4 × 3… (all of them = n!). 1st & 2nd (order):n × (n−1), do NOT divide. Pairs / handshakes:n × (n−1) ÷ 2 (order doesn't matter). Diagonals:all pairs of corners − the sides.

🏆 You are ready when...

  • You ask first: building in stages, or choosing a group?
  • You divide by 2 ONLY when order doesn't matter (pairs, handshakes)
  • You never divide for "1st and 2nd" / captain-and-keeper questions
