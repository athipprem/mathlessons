🧱 How Many Shapes Are Hiding?

Why learn this?

Look at a chocolate bar, a window with panes, a chessboard, or floor tiles. Inside one grid there are way more squares and rectangles than you first think — the big ones hide on top of the small ones! If you just point and count, you will miss some or count some twice. The trick is to count with a system, and contests love asking exactly this.

Never just eyeball it. Count by a SYSTEM — by SIZE for squares, by LINES for rectangles.

Squares — Count by SIZE

In a square grid, squares come in different sizes. Count one size at a time, then add. Here is a 3×3 grid (3 boxes across, 3 down):

1×1 squares: 9 2×2 squares: 4 3×3 squares: 1
9 + 4 + 1 = 14 squares in a 3×3 grid

Quick way to count one size: a k×k square can start in (n−k+1) places across and the same number down. In 3×3: the 2×2 fits in 2×2 = 4 spots. The 3×3 fits in 1×1 = 1 spot.

Rectangles — Choose 2 LINES Each Way

Every rectangle is made by picking 2 up-down lines (its left and right edges) and 2 across lines (its top and bottom). So:

Rectangles = (pick 2 of the up-down lines) × (pick 2 of the across lines)

A 2×2 grid has 3 up-down lines and 3 across lines:

Pick 2 of the 3 up-down lines: that is the pairs move you know → 3×2÷2 = 3 ways. Same across = 3 ways.

3 × 3 = 9 rectangles in a 2×2 grid (this already includes the squares!)

🗺️ The Plan

1Read the question: does it want only SQUARES, or ALL RECTANGLES (rectangles include squares)?
2Squares → count by SIZE: 1×1, then 2×2, then 3×3 … then add them up.
3Rectangles → count the up-down lines and the across lines. Pick 2 of each (the pairs move), then multiply.
4Check the exact ask — "how many squares", "how many rectangles", or "rectangles that are NOT squares" (then subtract the squares).

Lines in a grid: a grid that is m boxes wide has m+1 up-down lines; n boxes tall has n+1 across lines.

Worked Example

How many rectangles (all sizes) are in a 2×2 grid?

It asks for rectangles → use the LINES method.
2 boxes wide → up-down lines = 2 + 1 = 3
2 boxes tall → across lines = 2 + 1 = 3
Pick 2 of the 3 up-down lines (pairs move):
3 × 2 ÷ 2 = 3 ways
Pick 2 of the 3 across lines:
3 × 2 ÷ 2 = 3 ways
Multiply: 3 × 3 = 9
9 rectangles

⚠️ If it had asked only for squares in this 2×2 grid, you would count by size instead: 1×1 = 4, 2×2 = 1, total 5. Different question, different method!

Your Turn

Squares → count by size and add. Rectangles → pick 2 lines each way and multiply. Read the exact ask!

P1.  How many of the smallest (1×1) squares are in a 4×4 grid?
4 across × 4 down of the little boxes.
4 × 4 = 16.
16 small squares
P2.  How many squares of all sizes are in a 2×3 grid (2 boxes tall, 3 boxes wide)?
Count by size. 1×1 squares: 2 rows × 3 cols = 6.
2×2 squares: only 1 row of room tall, 2 spots wide = 2.
No 3×3 fits (only 2 tall).
6 + 2 = 8.
8 squares
P3.  How many rectangles (all sizes) are in a 1×4 strip (one row of 4 boxes)?
4 boxes wide → up-down lines = 5. Across lines = 2 (top & bottom).
Pick 2 of 5 up-down lines: 5 × 4 ÷ 2 = 10.
Pick 2 of 2 across lines: 1 way.
10 × 1 = 10.
10 rectangles
P4.  How many rectangles (all sizes) are in a 1×2 strip (one row of 2 boxes)?
2 boxes wide → 3 up-down lines; 2 across lines.
Pick 2 of 3 up-down lines: 3 × 2 ÷ 2 = 3.
Pick 2 of 2 across: 1.
3 × 1 = 3 (left box, right box, and both together).
3 rectangles
📖 Figure Counting — in one line

Squares: count by SIZE and add. Rectangles: pick 2 lines up-down × 2 lines across, then multiply.

Always read the exact ask — squares only, all rectangles, or "not squares" (subtract the squares).

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You count squares one size at a time instead of guessing
  • You can say how many up-down and across lines a grid has, then pick 2 of each
  • You notice when a question wants rectangles that are NOT squares
