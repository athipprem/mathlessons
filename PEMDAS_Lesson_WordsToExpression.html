🎯 Why Learn This?

On Friday, no one hands you the sum

Every PEMDAS test you've done so far gave you the expression already built — like 9 + 3 × 5. You just had to follow the ladder. On Friday's school test, the questions will be STORIES: "a baker makes 24 cupcakes in the morning and 16 in the afternoon, and packs them into boxes of 8." Nobody writes (24+16)÷8 for you — YOU have to build it, out of the words.

You already own the hard half

Here's the good news: once the expression is built, you already know exactly what to do with it — you've done the order ladder for weeks (U59: 14/15, extra practice: 13/15). This lesson only teaches the OTHER half: reading a story and turning it into one line of maths, and knowing when it needs brackets.

Your job: turn the story into ONE line of maths. Then use the ladder you already know.

🧭 The 3-Step Routine

The one new idea

Every word problem, no matter how it's worded, gets turned into an expression the SAME three-step way. Learn this routine once — use it on every question Friday.

1Find the number facts — underline every number in the story and write next to it what it counts (e.g. "24 = cupcakes, morning").
2Decide what happens FIRST — read the story again and ask: which event happens before the others, in real life?
3Write it as ONE line — put brackets around whatever must happen first, if it's an add or subtract that needs protecting from a × or ÷ nearby.
Step 1: find the numbers → Step 2: what happens first? → Step 3: write ONE line

⚠️ This band (Find → Decide → Write) will appear again on the next few screens — it's the spine of everything in this lesson.

🗝️ Trigger Words

Step 1: find the numbers → Step 2: what happens first? → Step 3: write ONE line

Certain words are a strong clue for which operation a sentence means. Learn these four groups:

Words in the storyUsually means
each, every, per, groups of, rows of× multiply
shared equally, split between, packed into boxes of÷ divide
altogether, in total, combined, also+ add
left, remaining, change, broken, gives away− subtract

Worked Example — Multiply Then Add

"A pack of stickers costs 8 baht. Ploy buys 4 packs, and also buys a pen for 15 baht. How much does she spend altogether?"

Step 1 — the numbers: 4 (packs), 8 (baht per pack — "costs...packs" → multiply trigger), 15 (baht for the pen).
Step 2 — what happens first? She buys the packs (a multiply), THEN adds the pen. "Also" is an add trigger.
Step 3 — write it as one line: the ladder already multiplies before adding, so no brackets are needed.
4 × 8 + 15 = 47 baht

Worked Example — Divide With A Leftover

"Nine friends each bring 6 sweets to a party. They eat 20 sweets. How many sweets are left?"

Step 1 — the numbers: 9 (friends), 6 (sweets each → "each" = multiply trigger), 20 (eaten → "left" = subtract trigger).
Step 2 — what happens first? All the sweets get brought together (multiply), THEN some are eaten (subtract).
Step 3 — write it as one line: multiply happens first anyway on the ladder, so no brackets needed here either.
9 × 6 − 20 = 34 sweets
🔑 Core Idea

The Bracket Test

Does an add or subtract have to happen BEFORE a × or ÷? If yes, it needs brackets.

Not every story needs brackets — most of the time the ladder already does the right thing on its own (like both examples on the last screen). Brackets are only needed when the story's FIRST event would otherwise happen LAST on the ladder.

✅ NEEDS brackets

"A baker makes 24 cupcakes in the morning and 16 in the afternoon. He packs them into boxes of 8."

The two batches must be combined first — you pack the WHOLE pile, not each batch separately.

(24 + 16) ÷ 8 = 5
❌ does NOT need brackets

"4 packs of stickers at 8 baht, plus a pen for 15 baht."

The packs get multiplied first anyway — the ladder already does × before +, so the pen just joins on at the end.

4 × 8 + 15 = 47

⚠️ Both stories above contain an ADD. Only ONE of them needs brackets. That's the actual skill — not "does this story have an add in it," but "does that add have to happen before the multiply/divide next to it?"

📋 See It Move

Same numbers, same story ingredients — tap the button to add or remove the brackets and watch the value change.

24 + 16 ÷ 8
Without brackets: divide happens to the 16 alone first (16÷8=2), then 24+2=26.

🔢 Exponents Hiding In Words

A square arrangement is an exponent in disguise

"5 layers with 5 boxes in each layer" — that's 5 groups of 5, so it's 5×5. But because BOTH numbers are the same, it can be written as a square: 5².

5 rows × 5 columns = 5² = 25 boxes
"5 layers with 5 boxes in each layer" — same number both times, so it's a square:
5 × 5 = 5² = 25

The Discrimination That Matters

"6 rows of 6" — same number both times → IS a square:
6² = 36
"6 rows of 4" — DIFFERENT numbers → is NOT a square, just a plain multiply:
6 × 4 = 24

⚠️ Only write it as an exponent when BOTH numbers in the "rows of / layers of" phrase are the SAME. Different numbers = ordinary multiply, no exponent.

⚠️ Also watch the exponent itself: ³ means THREE of the number multiplied together (n×n×n), not "×3". 2³ = 2×2×2 = 8, NOT 2×3=6.

The Squaring Trap

(5 + 4)² means "add first, THEN square" — it is NOT the same as 5²+4². Work out both and see:

(5 + 4)² = 9² = 81
5² + 4² = 25 + 16 = 41
81 ≠ 41 — brackets around an addition, then squaring, is a completely different journey than squaring two separate numbers and adding.

⚠️ Two Traps To Watch For

Trap 1 — "Subtract FROM" Flips The Order

"Multiply it by 4, then subtract the answer from 50." His number is 9.

"Subtract A from B" always means B − A, NOT A − B. Say it out loud, twice, before writing anything.
A = the answer (4×9=36). B = 50. So it's 50 minus the answer:
50 − 4 × 9 = 50 − 36 = 14

⚠️ A quick self-check: if you wrote 4×9−50 instead, you'd get a NEGATIVE number (36−50=−14). If your answer to a "how much is left / what's the result" question comes out negative, that's a sign you flipped the subtraction the wrong way — go back and re-read "subtract FROM."

Trap 2 — Same-Tier Chains Run Left To Right

Nut saves 35 baht a week for 6 weeks, then spends 90 baht on a book. His sister gives him 40 baht.

35×6 (multiply, happens first on the ladder) = 210. Then −90 and +40 are BOTH Add/Subtract tier — do them in the order they're written, left to right:
210 − 90 = 120
120 + 40 = 160 baht

⚠️ Don't do all the adds before all the subtracts (or the reverse) just because it feels tidier — when + and − are tied on the ladder, left-to-right decides, exactly like Lesson 1's ladder rule.

Practice — Words Into One Expression

P1 (needs brackets).  A shop receives 15 boxes of pens in the morning and 25 in the afternoon. The boxes are shared equally across 8 shelves. How many boxes go on each shelf?
Both batches are combined first, THEN shared: (15+25)÷8 = 40÷8
= 5
P2 (no brackets needed).  Tickets cost 6 baht each. Anda buys 5 tickets and also buys a 12-baht snack. How much does she spend in total?
The tickets multiply first anyway, no brackets needed: 5×6+12 = 30+12
= 42
P3 (exponent in words).  A garden has 4 rows with 4 plants in each row. 3 plants die. How many plants are left?
Same number both times (4 rows, 4 each) → it's a square: 4²=16, then 3 die: 16−3
= 13
P4 (the "subtract FROM" trap).  Multiply a number by 5, then subtract the answer from 60. The number is 8. What is the result?
"Subtract the answer FROM 60" means 60 minus the answer: 60−5×8 = 60−40
= 20
