🔍 Why This Matters

Where you actually see this

Newspapers, sports stats, weather charts, class attendance sheets — almost every real chart or table you see has SOME numbers filled in and others missing. Someone worked out the missing ones using clues, exactly like a detective. That's the skill in this lesson.

Why it's worth mastering right now

On the ASMOPSS competition, a puzzle exactly like this shows up in the hardest section almost every single year — a table or pictogram with some numbers hidden, and 2 or 3 sentences of clues to work them out. It's not hard maths. It's a NEW way of reading clues that you haven't practiced yet.

You are a Data Detective. The chart has holes in it. The clues are your evidence. Your job: fill in the holes, one at a time.

🧭 The Data Detective's Method

Every one of these puzzles is solved the exact same way, no matter what the clues say:

1Draw or picture the table. Put a "?" where a number is missing.
2Turn EVERY clue sentence into a short math fact — one line per clue. Don't skip any.
3Find the ONE clue that lets you solve for a number straightaway (often the TOTAL clue, once you know all the other rows).
4Use that number to unlock the next one, and the next — like a chain.
5Check: do all your filled-in numbers add up to match the total clue?

You already know how to turn a sentence into a math fact — you did this in Word Problem Decoding (Clue Board). The new part here is that the clues are usually about ROWS IN A TABLE, and you almost always finish by using a TOTAL to find the last missing piece.

⚠️ The 3 clue types that show up again and again: "altogether / in total" (a sum), "more than / fewer than" (a difference), and "times as many" (a ratio). Spot which type each clue is before you try to use it.

Skill 1 of 4

Total Minus Known — The Simplest Clue

A pictogram shows the number of toy cars owned by 4 kids. Tom owns 7, Mia owns 10, Jay owns an unknown amount, and Sue owns 9. Altogether, the 4 kids own 40 toy cars. How many toy cars does Jay own?

Tom🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗7
Mia🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗10
Jay??
Sue🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗🚗9
Total − (all the numbers you DO know) = the missing number
CLUE 1Tom = 7, Mia = 10, Sue = 9 (given directly)
CLUE 2Tom + Mia + Jay + Sue = 40
GOALFind Jay
Step 1 — add up everyone you know: 7 + 10 + 9 = 26
Step 2 — subtract that from the total: 40 − 26 = 14
Jay owns 14 toy cars

⚠️ Always add the KNOWN numbers first, then subtract from the total. Don't try to guess-and-check — it's slower and riskier.

⭐ Your Turn

Practice — Total Minus Known

Known: 6+8+5 = 19
Ben = 29 − 19 = 10
10
P2.  ★ A pictogram shows books on 5 shelves: 14, 9, ?, 11, 8. Altogether there are 60 books. How many books are on the missing shelf?
Known: 14+9+11+8 = 42
Missing shelf = 60 − 42 = 18
18
Skill 2 of 4

"More Than / Fewer Than" — The Difference Clue

Plant A is 25 cm tall. Plant B is 8 cm taller than Plant A. How tall is Plant B?

"more than" → ADD   |   "fewer than / less than" → SUBTRACT
CLUE 1Plant A = 25 cm
CLUE 2Plant B = Plant A + 8
GOALFind Plant B
Step 1 — write the difference clue as an equation: Plant B = 25 + 8
Step 2 — calculate: 25 + 8 = 33
Plant B is 33 cm tall

⚠️ The trap: "B is 8 taller than A" means B = A + 8, NOT A = B + 8. Always ask "who is the BIGGER one?" — the word "taller/more" points at them.

⭐ Your Turn

Practice — Difference Clues

P1.  Box A holds 30 marbles. Box B holds 12 more marbles than Box A. How many marbles does Box B hold?
Box B = 30 + 12 = 42
42
P2.  ★ Team X scored 45 points. Team Y scored 17 fewer points than Team X. How many points did Team Y score?
Team Y = 45 − 17 = 28
28
Skill 3 of 4

"Times As Many" — The Ratio Clue

A pictogram shows fish caught by 3 friends. Ben caught an unknown number of fish. Cal caught twice as many fish as Ben. Dee caught 6 fish. Altogether they caught 30 fish. How many fish did Ben catch?

"twice as many as Ben" = 2 × Ben   |   write the UNKNOWN as a box, everything else in terms of it
CLUE 1Ben = unknown (call it B)
CLUE 2Cal = 2 × B
CLUE 3Dee = 6
CLUE 4Ben + Cal + Dee = 30
GOALFind Ben
Step 1 — substitute everything into the total clue: B + 2B + 6 = 30
Step 2 — combine the B terms: 3B + 6 = 30
Step 3 — subtract 6 from both sides: 3B = 24
Step 4 — divide by 3: B = 8
Ben caught 8 fish (Cal caught 16, Dee caught 6, total 30 ✓)

⚠️ This is the same "balance scale" method from Algebraic Thinking — call the unknown a letter, write every OTHER row in terms of that letter, then use the total to solve.

⭐ Your Turn

Practice — Ratio + Total

P1.  3 boxes of buttons: Box P has an unknown amount, Box Q has 3 times as many as Box P, Box R has 5 buttons. Altogether there are 49 buttons. How many does Box P have?
P + 3P + 5 = 49 → 4P = 44 → P = 11
11
P2.  ★ 3 jars of candy: Jar A has an unknown amount, Jar B has twice as many as Jar A, Jar C has 4 more than Jar A. Altogether there are 40 candies. How many are in Jar A?
A + 2A + (A+4) = 40 → 4A + 4 = 40 → 4A = 36 → A = 9
9
Skill 4 of 4 — Hardest

Chained Clues — The Full Detective Puzzle

A pictogram shows laps run by 4 athletes. Runner 1 ran 12 laps. Runner 2 ran 3 more laps than Runner 1. Runner 3 ran 5 fewer laps than Runner 2. Runner 4 ran an unknown number of laps. Altogether the 4 runners ran 60 laps. How many laps did Runner 4 run?

Solve the clues in ORDER — each one unlocks the next, like stepping stones
CLUE 1Runner 1 = 12
CLUE 2Runner 2 = Runner 1 + 3
CLUE 3Runner 3 = Runner 2 − 5
CLUE 4Runner 1 + Runner 2 + Runner 3 + Runner 4 = 60
GOALFind Runner 4
Step 1 — Runner 2, using Clue 2: 12 + 3 = 15
Step 2 — Runner 3, using Clue 3: 15 − 5 = 10
Step 3 — add up the 3 you now know: 12 + 15 + 10 = 37
Step 4 — subtract from the total: 60 − 37 = 23
Runner 4 ran 23 laps

⚠️ Never jump straight to the total. Walk the chain one clue at a time, in the order they unlock — usually the order they're written in.

⭐ Your Turn

Practice — Chained Clues

P1.  4 stalls sold buns: Stall 1 = 10, Stall 2 = Stall 1 + 4, Stall 3 = Stall 2 − 2, Stall 4 = unknown. Altogether they sold 58 buns. How many did Stall 4 sell?
Stall 2 = 10+4 = 14. Stall 3 = 14−2 = 12
Known sum = 10+14+12 = 36. Stall 4 = 58−36 = 22
22
P2.  ★★ 4 workshops: Workshop 1 = unknown (W), Workshop 2 = W + 7, Workshop 3 = 2 × Workshop 2, Workshop 4 = Workshop 3 − 10. Altogether 145 people attended. How many attended Workshop 3?
W + (W+7) + 2(W+7) + (2(W+7)−10) = 145
W + W+7 + 2W+14 + 2W+4 = 145 → 6W + 25 = 145 → 6W = 120 → W = 20
Workshop 3 = 2 × (20+7) = 2 × 27 = 54
54

🗺️ The Data Detective's Full Checklist

1Draw the table. Mark every missing value with a "?".
2Turn every clue into a math fact, one at a time. Don't skip any, don't combine two at once.
3Spot the clue type: TOTAL (sum), MORE/FEWER (difference), or TIMES AS MANY (ratio).
4Solve the clues in the order they unlock — usually top to bottom.
5Use the TOTAL clue last, to find whichever value is still missing.
6Check: do ALL your numbers (given + solved) add up to the stated total?

⚠️ If a puzzle has 3 or 4 clues, that's not harder maths — it's just more stepping stones. Walk them one at a time and you'll always get there.

📖 Today's Big Idea

Every table/pictogram clue-puzzle is solved the same way: turn each clue into a math fact, solve them in order, and save the TOTAL clue to catch the very last missing number. It's detective work, not new maths.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You write EVERY clue down before trying to solve anything
  • You can tell apart a total clue, a difference clue, and a ratio clue at a glance
  • You solve clues in the order they unlock — not randomly guessing
  • You always check your answers add up to the stated total

This exact puzzle type appears in the hardest section of the ASMOPSS paper almost every year. You're ready for it now.
