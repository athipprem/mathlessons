Correct answer: 48  (2 × 4! = 48)

You did 4! correctly — but stopped. The ×2 was missing.

💡 Why ×2? "One end" means Mia can stand at the LEFT end OR the RIGHT end. That's 2 options for Mia's position — so you multiply by 2.

Goal of this lesson: Make ×2 so automatic that you NEVER forget it again.

The Three Both-Ends Types

There are exactly 3 types. Each has a rule. Learn all three — and when to use ×2.

Type 1 — One person, either end

"Ali must be at one end" (can be left OR right)

2 × (n−1)!

×2 because Ali can be at LEFT end or RIGHT end

Type 2 — Two people, fixed ends (either order)

"Ben at one end AND Sara at the other" (either order)

2 × (n−2)!

×2 because Ben–Sara or Sara–Ben at the ends

Type 3 — One person, ONE specific end

"The teacher must be at the left end only"

(n−1)!

NO ×2 — only one valid position for that person

🔑 The ×2 rule: If the person(s) can be at either end → ×2. If locked to ONE specific end → no ×2.

The Automatic ×2 Check

Before writing your answer on any Both-Ends question, run this 2-second check:

Ask yourself: "Can the person(s) be at EITHER end?"

YES → ×2

"must be at one end"
"at one of the two ends"
"either order at the ends"

NO → no ×2

"at the left end"
"at the right end specifically"
"at the front only"

Advanced Practice

These are the ISA-level difficulty questions. Apply the ×2 check every time.

Q4. 8 people stand in a row. The two youngest must each be at one of the two ends. How many arrangements?

Two people at ends (either order) → Type 2: 2 × (8−2)! = 2 × 6! = 2 × 720 = 1440

Q5. 4 friends stand in a row. How many arrangements have Ali at the LEFT end and Ben at the RIGHT end?

Both ends fixed, specific positions → NO ×2: (4−2)! = 2! = 2
