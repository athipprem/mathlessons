🎮 Welcome to the Bag Sorting Challenge

Sometimes there's more than one way to break the rule

In Brick 1, "at least 1 girl" was broken exactly ONE way — zero girls. But what about a rule like “not all the same colour”? If there are 3 colours, there are 3 different ways to be ALL the same colour — all red, all blue, OR all green. That's THREE bad cases, not one.

You are not going to just read about this — you're going to play through it. First you'll sort real handfuls with your own hands (well, taps). Then you'll discover the fast shortcut yourself, because you'll already know it's true. Earn ⭐ stars as you go — watch the counter at the top.

🎯 How to play: tap the buttons on each screen, make predictions before you see answers, and build up your own worked solutions round by round. Every screen has one job — do it before moving on.

🃏 Game 1 — Sort Every Handful

A tiny jar has 5 counters: 3 red (R1, R2, R3) and 2 blue (B1, B2). There are exactly 10 different handfuls of 3 you could pick from this jar. We're going to deal ALL 10, one at a time. For each one, tap the bin it belongs in.

Card 1 of 10
🎯 ALL SAME bin: 0
🎨 MIXED bin: 0

⚡ Same Answers, Faster Way

You just found every number below by sorting real cards. Here's a shortcut that gets the exact same numbers — without listing anything by hand. Watch how each one matches.

ALL handfuls — you sorted 10 cards5×4×3÷6 = 10 ✅
ALL-RED bad case — you found 1 card3×2×1÷6 = 1 ✅
ALL-BLUE bad case — you found 0 cardsonly 2 blues exist, so = 0 ✅
MIXED (GOOD) — you found 9 cards10 − (1+0) = 9 ✅
Why did ALL-BLUE come out as 0?

There are only 2 blue counters, and a handful needs 3. You physically could not deal an all-blue card — and that's exactly why the shortcut also gives 0. The formula isn't magic; it's just counting the same thing you did, faster.

The shortcut formula ALWAYS agrees with sorting by hand. It's just much faster when there are too many handfuls to list one by one.

⚠️ Predict First — The Zero Trap

A jar has 11 counters: 2 red, 5 blue, 4 green (11 in total). A handful of 3 is picked.

PREDICT: there are only 2 red counters. How many different ALL-RED handfuls of 3 can you make?

🔍 Remember Game 1? Blue only had 2 counters and its all-same count was ALSO 0 — same trap, same reason.

Full solution
1
11 × 10 × 9 ÷ 6
11 is the whole jar. 10,9 count down. Divide by 6.
11×10=110, 110×9=990, 990÷6=165 (this is ALL)
a
0  (cannot choose 3 from only 2)
Whenever a group is SMALLER than 3, its bad count is always 0 — write it down, don't skip it.
All-red = 0 handfuls (impossible)
b
5 × 4 × 3 ÷ 6
All-blue: 5 blues total (n=5). 4,3 count down. Divide by 6.
5×4=20, 20×3=60, 60÷6=10 all-blue handfuls
c
4 × 3 × 2 ÷ 6
All-green: 4 greens total (n=4). 3,2 count down. Divide by 6.
4×3=12, 12×2=24, 24÷6=4 all-green handfuls
✓
(0 + 10 + 4) then 165 − 14
0 (red) + 10 (blue) + 4 (green) = 14 total bad. The 0 still counts as a term — leaving it out gives the wrong total.
165 − 14 = 151 handfuls that are NOT all the same colour

⚠️ The rule: if a colour has FEWER than 3 members, its bad case is 0 — you cannot choose 3 from fewer than 3. Write the 0 down and keep adding.

🔍 Spot The Difference — "Not All Same" vs "At Least 1"

Same jar, two different questions. A jar has 20 buttons: 9 red, 6 blue, 5 green. A handful of 3 is picked. Before you see the answers — predict how many SEPARATE bad cases each question has.

Question A: "NOT ALL THE SAME colour" — how many separate bad cases?
Question B: "AT LEAST 1 blue" — how many separate bad cases?
Question A — "NOT ALL THE SAME colour"

Broken by all-red OR all-blue OR all-green — 3 bad cases, add them.

BAD = C(9,3)+C(6,3)+C(5,3) = 84+20+10 = 114
GOOD = 1140 − 114 = 1026

Question B — "AT LEAST 1 blue"

Broken only by ZERO blue — 1 bad case (choose 3 from the 14 non-blue buttons).

BAD = C(14,3) = 364
GOOD = 1140 − 364 = 776

⚠️ The test: "at least 1 [group]" → only ONE bad case (zero of that one group). "Not all the same" → ONE bad case PER group, added together. Read slowly and ask: how many DIFFERENT ways can this rule be broken?

⭐ Final Challenge

4 Levels — Earn Your Badge

🚦 For each question, first count HOW MANY separate bad cases there are, before you calculate anything. Each correct first try earns +15 points!

P1.  A bag has 10 marbles: 4 red, 3 blue, 3 green. A handful of 3 is picked. How many handfuls are NOT ALL THE SAME COLOUR?

ALL: choose 3 from 10 → 10×9×8=720, 720÷6=120.
BAD: all-red C(4,3)=4, all-blue C(3,3)=1, all-green C(3,3)=1 → 4+1+1=6.
GOOD: 120−6=114 handfuls.
A) 120 is ALL, B) 6 is BAD — both half-finished.

P2.  A drawer has 13 keys: 2 gold, 6 silver, 5 bronze. A handful of 3 is picked. How many handfuls are NOT ALL THE SAME COLOUR?

ALL: choose 3 from 13 → 13×12×11=1716, 1716÷6=286.
BAD: all-gold — only 2 gold keys exist, so C(2,3)=0 (write it down!). all-silver C(6,3)=20. all-bronze C(5,3)=10. Sum: 0+20+10=30.
GOOD: 286−30=256 handfuls.

P3.  The SAME drawer as P2 (2 gold, 6 silver, 5 bronze, 13 in total) is picked again, but this time the rule is AT LEAST 1 GOLD. How many handfuls of 3 have at least 1 gold key?

"At least 1 gold" is Brick 1's shape — only ONE bad case (zero gold = all from the 11 non-gold keys).
ALL=286. BAD=C(11,3)=11×10×9÷6=165.
GOOD: 286−165=121.
A) 256 is the answer to a DIFFERENT question (P2's "not all same") — don't mix them up.

P4.  A pencil case has 15 pens: 6 blue, 5 black, 4 red. A handful of 3 is picked. How many handfuls are NOT ALL THE SAME COLOUR?

ALL: choose 3 from 15 → 15×14×13=2730, 2730÷6=455.
BAD: all-blue C(6,3)=20, all-black C(5,3)=10, all-red C(4,3)=4 → 20+10+4=34.
GOOD: 455−34=421 handfuls.

📖 Brick 2's Big Idea

When a rule can be broken in MORE than one way, find EACH bad case separately, ADD them all together (even a 0!), then subtract that ONE combined number from ALL.

GOOD = ALL − (BAD case 1 + BAD case 2 + BAD case 3 + …)

🚦 Finish-Line Check — count how many separate bad cases the question describes BEFORE you calculate. If a group has fewer than 3 members, its bad case is 0 — write it down, don't skip it.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You can spot how many separate bad cases a "not all the same" rule creates
  • You find each bad case with its own Choose-3 calculation, then ADD them
  • You know a group smaller than 3 gives a bad case of 0 — and you still write it down
  • You can tell "not all the same" (many bad cases) apart from "at least 1" (one bad case)
