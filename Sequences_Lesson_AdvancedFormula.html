What if you don't know a or d? 🤔

In Lesson 1 you were always given a (first term) and d (common difference). But many questions give you two terms in the middle instead:

In a sequence, the 3rd term is 25 and the 7th term is 45.
Find d and T₁.

You can't substitute yet — you don't know d or a. So you find them first, then use the formula.

Here's the key insight: from T₃ to T₇ there are exactly 4 jumps of d. The total increase is 45 − 25 = 20. So each jump = 20 ÷ 4 = 5.

4 jumps → 45 − 25 = 20 → d = 20 ÷ 4 = 5

Once you know d = 5, you back-track from T₃ to T₁ (2 jumps backward):

T₁ = T₃ − 2 × d = 25 − 2 × 5 = 25 − 10 = 15

Jump method: d = (later term − earlier term) ÷ number of jumps
Then back-track to T₁: T₁ = T₃ − 2×d (if you start from T₃). Count the jumps carefully.

Three question types — one formula 🎯

Every sequences formula question is one of three types. Know which type you have, then follow the steps.

Question typeWhat it looks likeYour first move
Type A
Direct		 Given a, d, find Tn Go straight to formula: Tn = a + (n−1) × d
Type B
Find d + T₁		 Given two terms (e.g. T₃ and T₇), find d and T₁ Count jumps → d = difference ÷ jumps → back-track to T₁
Type C
Inequality		 "First term exceeding X" or "last term above zero" Set Tn > X, rearrange for n → round UP (exceeding) or DOWN (above zero)

Inequality — how rounding works

For "first term exceeding X": you solve Tn > X and get something like n > 12.3. Since term numbers must be whole numbers, round up to n = 13.

For "last term above zero" (decreasing sequence): you solve Tn > 0 and get n < 14.7. Round down to n = 14.

💡 Always check your answer:

Substitute your n back in to confirm: Tn passes the condition ✓ and Tn±1 fails it ✗. This takes 10 seconds and catches rounding errors.

Exceeding → round UP   |   Last above → round DOWN
When in doubt, substitute both n and n+1 to see which one crosses the boundary.

Example 2 — Type B: Find d, T₁ and T₁₂

T₃ = 25, T₇ = 45. Find d, T₁, and T₁₂.

StepWorking
Count jumps T₃→T₇7 − 3 = 4 jumps
Find dd = (45 − 25) ÷ 4 = 20 ÷ 4 = 5
Back-track to T₁T₁ = 25 − 2 × 5 = 25 − 10 = 15
Find T₁₂T₁₂ = 15 + (12−1) × 5 = 15 + 55 = 70

Example 3 — Type C: First term exceeding 70

Sequence: a = 6, d = 5. Which term is the first one to exceed 70?

StepWorking
Set up inequalityTn > 70 → 6 + (n−1) × 5 > 70
Rearrange5(n−1) > 64 → n−1 > 12.8
Round UPn − 1 ≥ 13 → n = 14
Verify T₁₄T₁₄ = 6 + 13×5 = 6 + 65 = 71 > 70 ✓
Verify T₁₃T₁₃ = 6 + 12×5 = 6 + 60 = 66 < 70 ✓ (not exceeding)
