🆕 A Brand-New Symbol: the "!"

Why learn this?

You already know how to count "ways to line things up in a row" (4 books = 4×3×2×1). Mathematicians gave that a short name — the factorial, written with an exclamation mark. Contests use it all the time, so let's make it easy.

n!  means  n × (n−1) × … × 2 × 1

Read "4!" as "four factorial". It just means: start at the number and multiply your way down to 1.

It's Just Counting Down and Multiplying

You've actually done this already — lining up 4 books in a row was 4! Let's work out a few:

3! = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6
4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24
5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120
6! = 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 720

Tip: each one is just the one before, × the new number. 4! = 3! × 4. 5! = 4! × 5. So they grow fast!

Adding or Subtracting Factorials

If a question says 6! − 5!, just work out each factorial first, then subtract. Take it one step at a time.

Example: 6! − 5!

6! = 720 ← work out each one first
5! = 120
720 − 120 = 600
= 600

Example: 4! + 3!

4! = 24,   3! = 6
24 + 6 = 30
= 30

⚠️ 6! − 5! is NOT 1! — you can't just subtract the small numbers. Always work out each factorial first, then do the + or −.

📖 Factorials — in one line

n! = multiply n all the way down to 1.

3! = 6,  4! = 24,  5! = 120,  6! = 720. For + or −, work out each factorial first.

🏆 You've got it when...

  • You read "5!" as "multiply 5 down to 1" and get 120
  • For 6! − 4! you work out 720 and 24 first, then subtract
