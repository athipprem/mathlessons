Before you read anything below — ask yourself:

"2 × 1 is 2. Could a rug 2 1/2 m by 1 1/3 m really be only a tiny bit more than 2 m², when it's bigger than 2 m by 1 m in BOTH directions?"

Say your answer out loud. Then scroll down and check.

🧮 Go And Get Squared Paper

A sheet of squared paper and two coloured pencils.

  1. Draw a rectangle 5 squares wide and 4 squares tall. Each square stands for 1/2 m across and 1/3 m up.
  2. Draw a thick line after 4 squares across (that's 2 m) and after 3 squares up (that's 1 m).
  3. Colour the 2 m × 1 m part: 12 squares, which is 2 m². That's all the shortcut counts.
  4. Now count the squares you did NOT colour: 8. The shortcut threw those away.
  5. All 20 squares: 6 squares make 1 m², so 20/6 = 3 1/3 m².

Two Contrasted Examples, Side By Side

🔶 The shortcut (WRONG for multiplying)
1 1/2 × 2 1/3
1 × 2 + 1/2 × 1/3 (splitting wholes and parts)
= 2 1/6  ✗
✅ Done right
1 1/2 × 2 1/3
3/2 × 7/3 (both converted first)
cancel 3 with 3 → 1/2 × 7/1 = 7/2
= 3 1/2  ✓
✅ Compare: ADDING mixed numbers — splitting IS right here
1 1/2 + 2 1/3 = 3 + 5/6
= 3 5/6
Adding: split. Multiplying: convert.
Adding mixed numbers: split wholes and parts. Multiplying mixed numbers: convert both to improper fractions first, every time.
