😕 You know the value — but not the position

You already know how to go forward: given a, d, and n → find Tn.

But sometimes the question gives you the value of a term and asks: “Which term number is it?”

Example question:
Sequence: 3, 8, 13, 18, 23, ...
The value 78 is somewhere in this sequence.
Which term number is 78?
❌ Why counting doesn’t work

You could try listing every term: T1=3, T2=8, T3=13 ... but that would take a very long time. What if the answer was T200?

⌛ Counting one by one is too slow — and you’ll make mistakes along the way.

Instead, we use the formula — but we plug in what we know and solve for n.

Tn = a + (n − 1) × d

We know: Tn = 78 a = 3 d = 5 We want: n = ?

So we put those numbers into the formula and work backwards to find n. This is called the Reverse Method.

🔁 The Reverse Method — Every Step Shown

Solve this: Sequence 3, 8, 13, 18, ... — which term equals 78?
We know: a = 3 d = 5 Tn = 78

Step 0 — Write the formula and plug in what you know
Tn = a + (n − 1) × d← the formula
78 = 3 + (n − 1) × 5← plug in: Tn=78, a=3, d=5
Now solve — one operation at a time
78 − 3 = (n − 1) × 5← subtract 3 from both sides
75 = (n − 1) × 5← because 78 − 3 = 75
75 ÷ 5 = n − 1← divide both sides by 5
15 = n − 1← because 75 ÷ 5 = 15
15 + 1 = n← add 1 to both sides
n = 16 ✓← because 15 + 1 = 16
⚠ Why do we add 1 at the end?
The formula uses (n−1), not n. So after dividing, you get n−1, not n itself. You must always add 1 to get the real term number. Missing this step is the most common mistake.
📋 What are we doing at each step?

Here is why each step makes sense:

Step 1
Subtract a from both sides
The +3 (which is a) is on the right side. To get (n−1)×5 alone, we move the +3 to the other side by subtracting it from both sides.
78 − 3 = (n−1) × 5   →   75 = (n−1) × 5
Step 2
Divide both sides by d
The ×5 (which is d) is multiplying (n−1). To get (n−1) alone, we divide both sides by 5. What you do to one side, you do to the other.
75 ÷ 5 = n−1   →   15 = n−1
Step 3
Add 1 to both sides
We now have 15 = n−1. But we want n, not n−1. So we add 1 to both sides: 15 + 1 = n−1 + 1 = n.
15 + 1 = n   →   n = 16
The 3-step rule — always in this order:
1 Subtract a from both sides
2 Divide both sides by d
3 Add 1 to get n

📌 Quick Reference — Finding n
Tn = a + (n − 1) × d
When you know Tn, a, d — find n in 3 steps:
1 Subtract a from both sides  →  Tn − a = (n−1) × d
2 Divide both sides by d  →  (Tn − a) ÷ d = n−1
3 Add 1  →  n = (Tn − a) ÷ d + 1
⚠ Never forget +1 at the end!
After dividing, you have n−1. You need n. So always add 1 to your division result.
