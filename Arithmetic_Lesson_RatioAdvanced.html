The k method — your key tool

In Unit 17 you handled some ratio-change problems correctly (Q13 ✓, Q14 ✓). Q15 was left blank — that was the ages problem. This lesson tackles ages and other harder before/after problems directly.

The k method — always do this
1. Ratio a:b → write quantities as ak and bk
2. Apply the change (+/− some amount)
3. Use the NEW ratio to set up an equation
4. Solve for k → find all original amounts
Before
a : b
Write as ak and bk
After change
new ratio
Set up equation using new ratio

Quick warm-up — adding items

Q: Red and blue counters are in ratio 1:3. After adding 8 red counters, the new ratio is 3:5. How many blue counters are there?
1
Before: red=k, blue=3k
2
After adding 8 red: red=k+8, blue=3k
3
New ratio 3:5 → (k+8):(3k) = 3:5
Cross-multiply: 5(k+8) = 3(3k)
5k+40 = 9k → 40 = 4k → k = 10
4
Blue = 3k = 3×10 = 30
30 blue counters (check: before 10:30=1:3 ✓, after 18:30=3:5 ✓)

Ages problems — the most common ratio-change type

Ages ratio problems are tricky because both people's ages increase by the same number of years. That's what makes them different from adding marbles.

Now
Father
4k
Son
k
Ratio 4:1
In 6 years
Father
4k+6
Son
k+6
Ratio 2:1
Q: Father and son ages are in ratio 4:1. In 6 years the ratio will be 2:1. Find the son's current age. (This was Q15 you left blank!)
1
Now: father=4k, son=k
2
In 6 years: father=4k+6, son=k+6
3
New ratio 2:1: (4k+6):(k+6) = 2:1
4k+6 = 2(k+6) = 2k+12
2k = 6 → k = 3
Son = k = 3 years old. Father = 12. In 6 years: 18:9 = 2:1 ✓
⚠ Key difference from other ratio-change problems

Both ages increase by the same number (6 years here). So you add 6 to BOTH sides. For marbles — only one quantity changes. Always read carefully: who changes?

Another ages example — years ago

Q: Mother and daughter are in ratio 5:2 now. 4 years ago the ratio was 3:1. Find the mother's current age.
1
Now: mother=5k, daughter=2k
2
4 years ago: mother=5k−4, daughter=2k−4
3
Old ratio 3:1: (5k−4):(2k−4) = 3:1
5k−4 = 3(2k−4) = 6k−12
8 = k → k = 8
Mother = 5×8 = 40. Daughter = 16. Check 4 years ago: 36:12 = 3:1 ✓

Remove items + mixed scenarios

Q: A bag has white and black marbles in ratio 3:2. After removing 5 white marbles, the ratio becomes 1:1. How many black marbles are there?
1
Before: white=3k, black=2k
2
After removing 5 white: white=3k−5, black=2k
3
New ratio 1:1 → 3k−5 = 2k → k = 5
Black = 2×5 = 10. Check: before 15:10=3:2 ✓, after 10:10=1:1 ✓
Q: Boys and girls at a club are in ratio 4:3. After 6 more girls join, the ratio becomes 2:2 (equal). How many boys are there?
1
Before: boys=4k, girls=3k
2
After 6 girls join: boys=4k, girls=3k+6
3
Equal → 4k = 3k+6 → k = 6
Boys = 4×6 = 24. Girls originally = 18. After: 24:24 = 1:1 ✓
Which quantity changes?
Adding marbles → that quantity increases
Removing items → that quantity decreases
Years pass → BOTH ages increase by the same amount
Transfer (A gives to B) → A decreases, B increases by same amount

What you've learned ✅

The k method — 4 steps every time
1. Write before quantities as ak and bk
2. Apply the change to the right quantities
3. Set up equation using the new ratio
4. Solve for k → find original amounts
Adding items
Only the added quantity increases. The other stays the same.
Removing items
Only the removed quantity decreases. Subtract from that side only.
Ages problems
BOTH ages change by the same number of years. Add (or subtract) to both.
Transfer problems
One quantity decreases, the other increases by the same amount.
