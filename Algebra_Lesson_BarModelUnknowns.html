Bar Model & Unknowns

Unit 16 · Lesson 2 — Algebraic Thinking

What is a bar model?

A bar model is a picture that shows the relationships between numbers. It turns a word problem into something you can see.

Once you can draw the bar model, writing the equation is easy.

Example: Tom has 3 times as many stickers as Ann. Together they have 48.

The bar model shows us: Ann + Tom = 48, and Tom = 3 × Ann. So there are 4 equal parts totalling 48 → Ann = 12, Tom = 36.

Why is this useful?
Bar models help you see the unknowns before you write any equations. They are especially powerful for ratio problems, difference problems, and multi-person problems.

The Bar Model Method

Three types of problems — and how to handle each one.

Type 1 — Ratio (one is a multiple of the other)

Problem: Tom has 3× as many stickers as Ann. Together = 48. Find Tom’s share.
1Draw the bars. Ann = 1 unit. Tom = 3 units. Total = 4 units.
2Find 1 unit. 4 units = 48 → 1 unit = 48 ÷ 4 = 12
3Answer. Tom = 3 × 12 = 36 stickers
✓ Tom = 36, Ann = 12, total = 48 ✓
Equation approach: Let A = Ann's stickers.
Tom = 3A. Total: A + 3A = 48 → 4A = 48 → A = 12, Tom = 36.

Type 2 — Difference (one is more than the other)

Problem: A rope is cut into 2 pieces. Longer = shorter + 15 cm. Total = 87 cm. Find the shorter piece.
1Let S = shorter. Then longer = S + 15.
2Write the total equation. S + (S + 15) = 87 → 2S + 15 = 87
3Solve. 2S = 72 → S = 36 cm
✓ Shorter = 36 cm, longer = 51 cm, total = 87 cm ✓

Type 3 — Chain (three or more with linked relationships)

Problem: Amy saved $20 more than Ben. Ben saved twice as much as Carl. Together = $90. Find Carl’s savings.
1Let C = Carl. Ben = 2C. Amy = Ben + 20 = 2C + 20.
2Total equation. C + 2C + (2C + 20) = 90 → 5C + 20 = 90
3Solve. 5C = 70 → C = $14
✓ Carl = $14, Ben = $28, Amy = $48. Total = $90 ✓

Lesson Complete! 🎉

You now have a powerful toolkit for solving word problems with unknowns.

📊 Type 1 — Ratio
One is a multiple of the other. Draw equal-sized bar segments. Divide total by number of units.
📏 Type 2 — Difference
One is more/less than the other. Let S = smaller. Larger = S + difference. Write total equation.
🔗 Type 3 — Chain
Three+ people linked. Start from the smallest (let that = C). Build each other in terms of C. Sum and solve.
⏳ Age Problems
Write "current age" and "future age" separately. The future condition gives you the equation to solve.
Key habit: Always define your unknown clearly at the start (e.g. "Let B = Ben's stickers"). Then express everything else in terms of B. This keeps your working clear and avoids errors.
