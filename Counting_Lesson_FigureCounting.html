🏆 Why Learn This?

Where figure-counting shows up:

"How many squares on a chessboard?" "How many triangles in this picture?" "How many ways can you walk to school turning only right or down?" These are classic competition questions — and tiling, design, and map routes use the same idea.

Today's superpower: count shapes without missing any and without counting the same one twice — by being organised, not by eyeballing.

🔍 The rule: count in an ORDER (by size, then position). Never just stare and guess.

Squares in a Grid — Count by Size

Don't just count the little squares! Bigger squares hide inside. Count each size separately, then add.

How many squares on this 3 × 3 grid?

1×1 squares: 3 across × 3 down = 9 ← the little ones
2×2 squares: 2 across × 2 down = 4 ← slide a 2-box around
3×3 squares: 1 across × 1 down = 1 ← the whole thing
add them: 9 + 4 + 1 = 14
= 14 squares

The Pattern for Any Square Grid

On an n × n grid, the count is just the square numbers added up:

n×n grid = n² + (n−1)² + … + 2² + 1²

Example: a 4 × 4 grid

1×1: 4×4 = 16
2×2: 3×3 = 9
3×3: 2×2 = 4
4×4: 1×1 = 1
16 + 9 + 4 + 1 = 30
= 30 squares

💡 Same idea for a chessboard (8×8): 64+49+36+25+16+9+4+1 = 204. The method scales — you never draw them all.

Counting Paths — Right or Down Only

To count shortest routes on a grid (moving only right or down), write at each corner how many ways reach it. Each corner = the corner above it + the corner to its left.

Shortest paths from top-left to bottom-right of a 2 × 2 grid:

start corner = 1
top row & left column = 1 (only one way) ← straight along
each inside corner = above + left: 1+1 = 2, then 2+1 = 3 …
bottom-right = 3 + 3 = 6
= 6 paths

Practice

Squares: add by size (n² + (n−1)² + …). Paths: add above + left at each corner.

Q1.   How many squares of all sizes are on a 3 × 3 grid of small squares?
9 + 4 + 1 = 14
Q2.   How many squares of all sizes are on a 5 × 5 grid of small squares?
25 + 16 + 9 + 4 + 1 = 55
Q3.   On a 1 × 3 grid (a single row of 3 boxes), how many shortest paths go from the top-left corner to the bottom-right corner, moving only right or down?
top corners each = 1; the one down-step can happen after 0, 1, 2 or 3 rights
= 4 paths
Q4.   On a 3 × 3 grid, how many shortest paths go from the top-left corner to the bottom-right corner (right or down only)?
fill corners (above + left): edges all 1
row: 1,2,3,4   next: 1,3,6,10   next: 1,4,10,20
bottom-right = 20
📖 Figure Counting on One Card
Be organised:count in an order, never eyeball. Squares in grid:add by size: n² + (n−1)² + … + 1. Paths (right/down):each corner = above + left; read off the end corner. Triangles in a figure:count smallest first, then 2-piece, then whole. Always check:did I miss a size, or count one twice?

🏆 You are ready when...

  • You count squares by size and add, instead of counting the little ones only
  • You fill a path grid corner-by-corner (above + left)
  • You finish by checking you didn't miss or double-count a shape
