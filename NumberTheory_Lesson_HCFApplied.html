🎯 HCF in the Real World

In U12 you found HCF(16,28) and HCF(30,45) — pure computation. Now we go deeper: word problems where HCF gives the answer.

🔑 Keywords that mean HCF:

largest / longest
— you want the biggest possible equal size
identical groups
— equal groups with same contents
cut equally
— equal pieces from different lengths, no waste
fill exactly
— largest tile / square that fits perfectly
use all of each
— must use every item with nothing left over

🎯 The Pattern

Whenever a problem asks for the LARGEST equal unit that works for two (or more) different quantities — it's always HCF.

⚡ Quick HCF Method Reminder

Two ways to find HCF — use whichever you prefer:

Method 1 — Prime Factorisation (most reliable)

Find HCF(30, 45):

30 = 2 × 3 × 5
45 = 3² × 5
Common prime factors: 3 × 5 = 15

Take only the primes that appear in BOTH, using the SMALLER power.

Method 2 — List Factors (for smaller numbers)

Find HCF(24, 36):

Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36
Largest common factor = 12
Tip: For competition problems, prime factorisation is more reliable for big numbers. Practice both so you can choose fast.

🪢 Type 1: Cutting Into Equal Pieces

Key phrase: "cut into equal lengths, no waste." → Find HCF of the lengths.

📋 Example — Two Ropes

Two pieces of rope are 30 m and 45 m long. They must be cut into equal lengths with no waste. What is the longest possible length of each piece?

1
Keywords: equal lengths, no waste, longest → this is HCF.
2
30 = 2 × 3 × 5  ·  45 = 3² × 5  ·  HCF = 3 × 5 = 15 m.
3
Check: 30 ÷ 15 = 2 pieces ✓  ·  45 ÷ 15 = 3 pieces ✓. Total = 5 pieces.

✏️ Practice

Practice 1
A carpenter has two planks — one 48 cm long and one 60 cm long. He wants to cut them into equal pieces with no waste. What is the longest possible length for each piece?
48 = 2⁴ × 3  ·  60 = 2² × 3 × 5
HCF(48, 60) = cm
HCF = 2² × 3 = 12 cm ✅   (48÷12=4 pieces, 60÷12=5 pieces)

💐 Type 2: Grouping Into Identical Bunches

Key phrase: "identical groups / bouquets / sets using all items." → Find HCF of the quantities.

📋 Example — Flower Bouquets

A florist has 40 yellow and 50 pink flowers. She makes identical bouquets using all flowers. What is the greatest number of bouquets she can make? How many of each colour per bouquet?

1
Greatest number of identical groups → HCF(40, 50).
2
40 = 2³ × 5  ·  50 = 2 × 5²  ·  HCF = 2 × 5 = 10 bouquets.
3
Each bouquet: 40 ÷ 10 = 4 yellow  ·  50 ÷ 10 = 5 pink. ✅

🔲 Type 3: Square Tiles — Largest Tile

Key phrase: "square tiles cover the floor exactly." → Largest tile side = HCF(length, width).

📋 Example — Tiling a Floor

A room is 72 cm long and 54 cm wide. It will be covered with identical square tiles, with no cutting. What is the largest possible tile size?

1
Tile must fit L and W exactly → largest square = HCF(72, 54).
2
72 = 2³ × 3²  ·  54 = 2 × 3³  ·  HCF = 2 × 3² = 18 cm.
3
Check: 72 ÷ 18 = 4 tiles along length ✓  ·  54 ÷ 18 = 3 tiles along width ✓.
Why HCF? The tile side must divide BOTH the length and the width exactly. The largest number that divides both = HCF.

✏️ Practice

Practice 3
A garden patio is 90 cm long and 60 cm wide. It will be paved with identical square stones that fit exactly. What is the largest possible stone size?
90 = 2 × 3² × 5  ·  60 = 2² × 3 × 5
HCF(90, 60) = cm
HCF = 2 × 3 × 5 = 30 cm ✅   (90÷30=3 tiles, 60÷30=2 tiles)

🛍️ Type 4: Equal Bags (Use All of Each)

Key phrase: "pack into identical bags / boxes using all of each type." → HCF gives number of bags.

📋 Example — Fruit Bags

A market stall has 70 lemons and 105 limes. Pack them into identical bags using all fruit. What is the greatest number of bags?

1
Identical bags, use all → HCF(70, 105).
2
70 = 2 × 5 × 7  ·  105 = 3 × 5 × 7  ·  HCF = 5 × 7 = 35 bags.
3
Each bag: 70 ÷ 35 = 2 lemons  ·  105 ÷ 35 = 3 limes. ✅

⭐ Challenge: HCF of Three Numbers

Same idea — find HCF of all three. Use prime factorisation and take the common factors across all three.

📋 Example — Three Strings

Three strings are 60 m, 80 m, and 100 m long. Cut all three into equal lengths, no waste. Longest possible length?

1
60 = 2² × 3 × 5  ·  80 = 2⁴ × 5  ·  100 = 2² × 5²
2
Common to all three: 2² and 5  ·  HCF = 4 × 5 = 20 m.
3
Pieces: 60÷20=3 · 80÷20=4 · 100÷20=5. Total = 12 pieces. ✅
Practice 5 ★
Three ribbons are 36 cm, 48 cm, and 60 cm long. They are cut into equal pieces with no waste. What is the longest possible piece length? How many pieces in total?
36 = 2² × 3²  ·  48 = 2⁴ × 3  ·  60 = 2² × 3 × 5
HCF(36, 48, 60) = cm
Total pieces = 36÷HCF + 48÷HCF + 60÷HCF =
HCF = 2² × 3 = 12 cm ✅   Pieces: 3+4+5 = 12 pieces total ✅

📋 HCF Applied — Summary

The 4 Problem Types:

✂️ Cutting
Longest equal piece from two/three lengths → HCF of lengths
💐 Grouping
Most identical groups using all items → HCF of quantities
🔲 Tiling
Largest square tile for floor → HCF of length and width
🛍️ Packing
Most identical bags, use all → HCF of all quantities

🔑 One Rule for All

Any time you need the LARGEST equal unit that works for two or more quantities — find the HCF.
Then divide each quantity by the HCF to get the count per group.
