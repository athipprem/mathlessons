⚖️ Which Side Wins?

Why learn this?

A see-saw or a kitchen balance tells you something instantly: the heavier side goes DOWN. If two things make it level, they weigh the same. From just a few weighings you can put everything in order — heaviest to lightest — or even trade one thing for another. Contests use balance puzzles all the time, and they're easy once you read the scale right.

Heavier side goes DOWN.  Level = SAME weight.

Chain the Comparisons

Each weighing gives you one fact. Line the facts up like a row of dominoes:

apple
▲
banana

apple is heavier than banana

banana
▲
cherry

banana is heavier than cherry

Put them in a line, heaviest first:

apple>banana>cherry
apple > banana > cherry → heaviest = apple, lightest = cherry

🗺️ The Plan (and a Trading Trick)

1Read each scale: heavier side is DOWN; level means equal.
2Write each fact as A > B (or A = B).
3Chain them into one line: heaviest → lightest.
4Trading: if 1 melon = 3 apples, you can swap "1 melon" for "3 apples" anywhere.

Trading example: if 1 box balances 4 cans, then 2 boxes balance 8 cans (just double both sides).

Worked Example

2 apples balance 1 melon. 1 melon balances 6 grapes. How many grapes balance 2 apples?

Fact 1: 2 apples = 1 melon
Fact 2: 1 melon = 6 grapes
Trade: the melon is the link. Swap "1 melon" for "6 grapes".
2 apples = 1 melon = 6 grapes
So 2 apples balance 6 grapes.

⚠️ Read the question's exact amount. It asked about 2 apples, not 1. If it had asked about 1 apple, you'd halve everything: 1 apple = 3 grapes.

Your Turn

Heavier side goes down; level = equal. Chain the facts, and trade using the matching item.

Q1.  Box A is heavier than Box B. Box B is heavier than Box C. Which box is the HEAVIEST?
A > B > C.
Heaviest = Box A
Q2.  The red ball is lighter than the blue ball. The blue ball is lighter than the green ball. Which ball is the LIGHTEST?
green > blue > red (red is lightest end).
Lightest = red
Q3.  1 brick balances 3 books. How many books balance 2 bricks?
1 brick = 3 books. Double both sides:
2 bricks = 6 books.
6 books
Q4.  3 apples balance 1 melon. 1 melon balances 9 cherries. How many cherries balance 1 apple?
3 apples = 1 melon = 9 cherries.
So 3 apples = 9 cherries → 1 apple = 9 ÷ 3.
3 cherries
📖 Balance Scales — in one line

Heavier side goes down; level = equal. Chain the facts into one order, and trade equal things to swap them.

Always read the exact amount the question asks for (1 apple or 2 apples?).

🏆 You've got it when…

  • From "A > B" and "B > C" you can name the heaviest and the lightest
  • From "1 melon = 6 grapes" you can trade a melon for 6 grapes

You now have both detective tools: logic grids and balance scales. Ready for the test!
