🎯 Why Learn This?

One expression, two different answers?

Try this in your head: 6 + 4 × 2. Did you get 20 (add first) or 14 (multiply first)? Both feel reasonable — but only ONE can be correct, because maths needs everyone in the world to get the SAME answer to the SAME expression. That's exactly why there's a fixed ORDER for doing operations, followed by every calculator, computer, and maths competition on Earth.

Your new superpower

Once you know the order, you'll NEVER be unsure which operation to do first again — in homework, in competitions, or on a calculator. This lesson teaches the core order. The next lesson adds two more pieces: exponents (powers) and parentheses-inside-parentheses.

There is ONE correct order for every expression — and it never changes.

🧮 The Order Ladder

The one new idea

Every expression is solved in FOUR stages, from top to bottom. Today we learn stages 1, 3 and 4 (stage 2, Exponents, comes in the next lesson — for now, treat it as "not yet used").

1Parentheses ( ) — some books call these "Brackets" instead. Same thing, same rule: Parentheses = Brackets = ( ). Anything inside them is done FIRST, before anything outside them.
2Exponents (powers, like 3²) — coming in Lesson 2.
3Multiply × and Divide ÷ — done together, working strictly LEFT TO RIGHT.
4Add + and Subtract − — done together, working strictly LEFT TO RIGHT.
Parentheses → (Exponents) → Multiply/Divide (left→right) → Add/Subtract (left→right)

⚠️ Common mix-up: multiply is NOT always done before divide, and add is NOT always done before subtract. When two operations are on the SAME step of the ladder, you do whichever one comes FIRST when reading left to right.

Worked Example — Multiply Before Add

9 + 3 × 5 = ?

Step 1 — scan for parentheses: there are none, so skip straight to Multiply/Divide.
Step 2 — Multiply/Divide: only one, so do it first.
3 × 5 = 15
Step 3 — the expression is now:
9 + 15
Step 4 — Add/Subtract: only one left, so do it last.
9 + 3 × 5 = 24

⚠️ If you'd added first (9+3=12, then 12×5=60), you'd get a completely different, WRONG answer. The order genuinely changes the result — it's not just a style choice.

Worked Example — Multiply AND Divide, Left To Right

24 ÷ 6 × 2 = ? Multiply and Divide are on the SAME ladder step, so we don't pick a favourite — we just go left to right.

Step 1 — no parentheses. Step 2 — Multiply/Divide are tied, so read LEFT TO RIGHT: the ÷ comes first (it's further left).
24 ÷ 6 = 4
Step 2 continued — the expression is now:
4 × 2
24 ÷ 6 × 2 = 8

⚠️ If you'd done the multiply first out of habit (6×2=12, then 24÷12=2), you'd get the WRONG answer, 2 instead of 8. When it's a tie, left-to-right decides — not "multiply always wins."

Worked Example — Parentheses Jump To The Front Of The Line

15 − 2 × 4 = ? vs a NEW question with parentheses added: 30 ÷ (2 + 3) = ?

15 − 2 × 4: no parentheses, so Multiply/Divide still goes first.
2 × 4 = 8  →  15 − 8
15 − 2 × 4 = 7
30 ÷ (2 + 3): parentheses are stage 1, so they ALWAYS go first, no matter what's outside them.
(2 + 3) = 5  →  30 ÷ 5
30 ÷ (2 + 3) = 6

⚠️ Parentheses don't care what operation is inside them or outside them — Multiply, Divide, Add, Subtract, it doesn't matter. If it's in parentheses, it's done FIRST, every single time.

Practice — The Order Rule

P1.  10 + 4 × 3 = ?
No parentheses → Multiply first: 4×3=12
10 + 12 = 22
P2.  (10 + 4) × 3 = ?
Parentheses first: 10+4=14
14 × 3 = 42
P3.  16 ÷ 4 × 2 = ?
÷ and × tied — left to right: 16÷4=4
4 × 2 = 8
P4 (Word Problem).  Pens cost $2 each. Mia buys 5 pens, plus a $3 eraser. What is the total cost?
5 pens × $2 = 5 × 2 = 10 (multiply first, no parentheses)
$10 + $3 eraser = $13
📖 Today's Big Idea

Every expression follows the SAME fixed order: Parentheses first, then Multiply/Divide (left to right), then Add/Subtract (left to right). Two operations on the same ladder step are never "always this one first" — you read left to right.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You solve anything inside parentheses before anything outside them, no matter what operation is outside
  • You know Multiply and Divide are equal rank — you do whichever is more to the LEFT first
  • You know Add and Subtract are equal rank — same left-to-right rule
  • You never assume "multiply always beats divide" or "add always beats subtract"
  • You're ready for Lesson 2, which adds Exponents and parentheses-inside-parentheses to the ladder
