📐 What is Geometry?

Geometry is the study of shapes, sizes, and spaces. In this lesson you'll learn two key measurements every ISA question on shapes uses:

🔲
Perimeter
The distance around the outside of a shape
🟦
Area
The amount of space inside a shape
💡 Memory trick: Perimeter = "Perimeter walks around the fence." Area = "Area fills the floor inside the fence."
1 of 8

Perimeter — Walking the Outside

Perimeter = sum of all sides. For a rectangle, you can use a shortcut:

Perimeter = 2 × (length + width)
Worked Example
1
Add length + width: 14 + 6 = 20
2
Multiply by 2: 20 × 2 = 40 cm
⚠️ Don't forget the unit! Perimeter is always in cm, m, etc. (not cm² — that's area).

Area of Rectangles & Squares

Area = length × width

The number of unit squares that fit inside.

Square: side × side = side²

A square has 4 equal sides.

💡 Area is always in cm² (or m²) — because you're multiplying cm × cm, you get square centimetres.

Quick Check

A square has perimeter 28 cm. What is its area?

Area of a Triangle

A triangle is exactly half a rectangle with the same base and height.

Area of triangle = ½ × base × height
⚠️ The height must be perpendicular (at 90°) to the base — it's the straight-up measurement, not the slanted side.

Quick Check

A triangle has base 12 cm and height 7 cm. What is its area?

cm²

Composite Shapes — The Split Trick

When a shape is unusual (like an L-shape), split it into rectangles, find each area, then add.

Method: Outer minus Cut-out

OR: imaginary full rectangle minus the missing corner piece.

1
Full rectangle: 10 × 8 = 80 cm²
2
Missing corner: 4 × 4 = 16 cm²
3
L-shape area: 80 − 16 = 64 cm²
💡 Two methods work: split into parts OR full rectangle minus cut-out. Use whichever feels natural.

Practice 1 — Perimeter & Area

Q1. A rectangle is 14 cm long and 6 cm wide.

a) What is its perimeter?

cm

b) What is its area?

cm²

Q2. An L-shaped room is 10 m wide and 8 m tall overall. A rectangular section 4 m wide and 3 m tall is cut from one corner. What is the area of the room?

m²
Full rectangle: 10×8=80. Cut-out: 4×3=12. L-area = 80−12 = 68 m²

Practice 2 — The Path Problem ⭐

A path around the outside of a shape is a favourite ISA question type. The method:

1
Find the outer rectangle (pool + path on all sides)
2
Find the inner shape (just the pool/garden)
3
Subtract: Outer − Inner = Path area
⚠️ If the path is 2 m wide on each side, the outer length = inner + 2+2 = 4 m extra (both sides!).

A rectangular garden is 15 m long and 10 m wide. A path 2 m wide runs along the inside of all four edges. What is the area of the path?

1
Inner lawn: (15−2−2) × (10−2−2) = 11 × 6 = 66 m²
2
Full garden: 15 × 10 = 150 m²
3
Path area: 150 − 66 = 84 m² ✓

Your turn: A pool is 20 m × 12 m. A tiled path 3 m wide surrounds the outside. What is the path area?

m²
Outer: (20+6)×(12+6) = 26×18 = 468. Pool: 240. Path = 468−240 = 228 m²

🎉 Lesson Complete!

Here are the 4 rules to keep in mind for every geometry ISA question:

1
Perimeter = 2 × (length + width). Unit: cm or m.
2
Area of rectangle = length × width. Unit: cm² or m².
3
Area of triangle = ½ × base × height.
4
L-shape / path = full rectangle − cut-out (or outer − inner).
📝 ISA tip: Always draw and label the shape in your working. Label all sides you know. Then pick the right formula.
