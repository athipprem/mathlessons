Two-Variable Totals

Unit 16 · Lesson 1 — Algebraic Thinking

What is this problem type?

In a Two-Variable Totals problem, you are told two things about a group that has two types of items.

Classic example:
A farm has chickens and cows.
There are 10 animals in total.
There are 28 legs in total.
How many cows are there?

You know the count and you know a total property (like legs, cost, or weight). That gives you two conditions:

10
Total Animals
Condition 1 — Count
28
Total Legs
Condition 2 — Property

Two conditions → you can find both unknowns. In this lesson you will learn two methods for solving these problems — and by the end, you will be able to use the equation method on your own.

Method 1 — Assume & Adjust

You already know this method. Let’s review it quickly so Method 2 makes more sense.

The Problem

A farm has chickens and cows. There are 10 animals and 28 legs. How many cows?

Chickens have 2 legs. Cows have 4 legs.

1
Suppose ALL are chickens.
10 × 2 = 20 legs
2
Find the gap.
Real total = 28. We have 20. Gap = 28 − 20 = 8 extra legs needed
3
Each swap adds 2 legs.
Swapping 1 chicken → 1 cow gives: 4 − 2 = +2 legs per swap
4
How many swaps?
8 ÷ 2 = 4 cows ✓
Check: 6 chickens + 4 cows = 10 animals, 12 + 16 = 28 legs ✓
⚠️ Assume & Adjust is great for simple problems. But when the numbers get harder — or when you need to show working in a competition — the equation method is faster and clearer. You will learn it next.

The Bracket Rule

Before you can use the equation method, you need to master expanding brackets. This is the key skill.

What does expanding mean?

When you see 2(10 − C), it means "2 times everything inside the bracket".

2 × (10 − C)
2 × 10 = 20
2 × C = 2C
2(10 − C) = 20 − 2C

The factor outside (2) multiplies every term inside the bracket.

Three more examples

a
3(n + 5) = 3×n + 3×5 = 3n + 15
b
5(20 − k) = 5×20 − 5×k = 100 − 5k
c
4(8 + m) = 4×8 + 4×m = 32 + 4m
The Rule: a(b + c) = ab + ac  |  a(b − c) = ab − ac
Multiply the outside factor by each term inside. The sign (+ or −) stays.

Phase 2 — Two Methods, Same Answer

Let’s solve the same problem both ways so you can see they always match.

The Problem

A farm has chickens and cows. There are 10 animals and 28 legs. How many cows?
⭐ Method 1 — Assume & Adjust
▶ Suppose all are chickens
10 × 2 = 20 legs
▶ Gap = 28 − 20 = 8
▶ Each swap adds 2 legs
Cows = 8 ÷ 2 = 4
Answer: 4 cows ✓
🔬 Method 2 — Equation
▶ Let C = cows
Chickens = 10 − C
▶ Legs equation:
4C + 2(10−C) = 28
▶ Expand: 4C+20−2C = 28
Simplify: 2C = 8
Solve: C = 4
Answer: 4 cows ✓
✅ Same answer — 4 cows — from both methods!
The equation is just Assume & Adjust written in maths symbols.

The Equation Method — 4 Steps

1
Name one unknown. Let C = number of cows.
2
Express the other in terms of it. Chickens = 10 − C
3
Write the legs equation. 4C + 2(10 − C) = 28
4
Expand, simplify, solve. 4C + 20 − 2C = 28 → 2C = 8 → C = 4

Phase 3 — Equation Practice

Remember the 4 steps: (1) Name one unknown  ·  (2) Express the other  ·  (3) Write the equation  ·  (4) Expand, simplify, solve
Practice 1 — Legs
A pond has ducks and frogs. There are 16 animals in total and 44 legs in total. Ducks have 2 legs. Frogs have 4 legs. How many frogs are there?
Let F = frogs. Ducks = 16 − F.
Legs equation: 2(16−F) + 4F = 44
Expand and solve for F.

Working:
Let F = frogs, ducks = 16 − F
2(16−F) + 4F = 44
32 − 2F + 4F = 44
32 + 2F = 44
2F = 12 → F = 6 frogs ✓
Check: 10 ducks + 6 frogs = 16 · 20 + 24 = 44 legs ✓

Let L = lemonade cups. Cola = 14 − L.
Cost equation: 2L + 3(14−L) = 34

What to remember for the test:
1. Name one unknown (e.g. C = cows)
2. Express the second as (total − C)
3. Write the equation using the second condition (legs/cost/weight)
4. Expand the bracket — then simplify and solve
