🔄 When The Line Bends Into A Circle

Why learn this?

Lining people up in a row is one thing — but what about seating friends around a ROUND table, hanging charms on a bracelet, or arranging keys on a keyring? These are all CIRCULAR arrangements, and they behave differently from straight-line arrangements. If everyone at a round table shifts one seat to the right, it LOOKS like a different lineup written down — but it's really the exact same circle of neighbours. Competition problems love this twist, so we need a rule that doesn't over-count rotations.

In a circle, rotating everyone by one seat does NOT create a new arrangement — the neighbours are all still the same.

The Big Idea — Fix One Seat

For n people in a straight LINE, there are n! orderings. But in a CIRCLE, every rotation of the same seating counts as "the same" — and there are exactly n rotations of any circle (shift by 1 seat, 2 seats, 3 seats... all the way around). So we take n! and divide by n to remove those n look-alike rotations.

n! ÷ n = (n−1)! — or think of it as: FIX one person in one seat (they can't rotate away), then arrange everyone else around them in a line.

⚠️ Flipping the circle over (mirror image, clockwise vs counter-clockwise) is usually counted as a DIFFERENT arrangement unless a question says otherwise — only ROTATIONS are treated as "the same."

Worked Example 1 — 4 Friends, Round Table

Alice, Ben, Cy, and Dee sit around a round table. How many DIFFERENT circular seatings are possible?

Step 1 — count people: n = 4
Step 2 — fix ONE person's seat so rotations can't sneak in as "new" arrangements: sit Alice down first — her seat is now fixed.
Step 3 — arrange the REMAINING (n−1) people in the remaining seats, like a line: Ben, Cy, Dee can go in the 3 remaining seats in (n−1)! = 3! ways
3! = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6
6 different circular seatings

Worked Example 2 — 5 Friends, Round Table

5 friends sit around a round table. How many different circular seatings?

n = 5. Fix 1 person's seat, arrange the other (n−1) = 4 around them.
(n−1)! = 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24

Same rule every time: circular arrangements of n items = (n−1)!

🗺️ The Plan

1Is the arrangement in a CIRCLE (round table, ring, bracelet, keyring)? If it's a straight line instead, just use n!.
2Count the people/items → that's n.
3Fix ONE item's position (it can't rotate away) → arrange the rest in a line: (n−1)!
4Are any of the items IDENTICAL (like twins)? If yes, ALSO divide by r! for each repeated item — just like Lesson 1!
5Full combined formula: (n−1)! ÷ (r₁! × r₂! × ...)

⚠️ Always check circle vs line FIRST, then check for repeats SECOND. Missing either check gives the wrong answer.

⭐ Capstone — Combining Both Ideas

6 Friends, Round Table, 2 Are Identical Twins

6 friends sit around a round table. Two of them are identical twins (impossible to tell apart once seated). How many DIFFERENT-looking circular seatings are there?

Step 1 — it's a circle, so start with (n−1)!: n = 6 → (6−1)! = 5! = 120
Step 2 — check for repeats: the 2 twins are identical → r = 2, so also divide by 2!
120 ÷ 2! = 120 ÷ 2 = 60
60 different-looking circular seatings
Circle rule AND repeats rule together: (n−1)! ÷ (r₁! × r₂! × ...)

⚠️ Do the circle step first ((n−1)!), THEN divide for repeats — not the other way around.

📖 Circular Arrangements — in one line

(n−1)! ÷ (r₁! × r₂! × ...) — fix one seat to kill the rotations, then divide out any identical items just like Lesson 1.

Two skills, one formula: circle first, repeats second.
