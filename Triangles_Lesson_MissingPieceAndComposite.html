🎯 Why Learn This?

Sometimes you know the area, not the shape

An architect might say "this roof panel needs to cover 40 m², and I've only got 10 m of base to work with — how TALL does it need to be?" That's working BACKWARDS from the area — exactly what today's lesson teaches.

Real shapes aren't always simple

A garden might be a rectangle with a triangular flowerbed cut out of one corner, or a tent's cross-section might be a rectangle with a triangular roof on top. Today you'll also learn how to find the area of these COMPOSITE (combined) shapes.

Same one rule as Lesson 1 — Area = ½ × base × height — just used in new ways.

🔍 Where Does "bh ÷ 2" Come From?

The one new idea (part 1 of 2)

If you already know the AREA and one of base/height, you can work BACKWARDS to find the missing one. Before we do that, let's rewrite the rule in a slightly different form — it makes the "working backwards" trick much easier to see.

Start with the rule you already know:
Area = ½ × b × h
Every number can be written as a fraction over 1 — it doesn't change its value. So let's write b and h that way too:
Area = ½ × b/1 × h/1
To multiply fractions, multiply straight across — all the TOPS together, all the BOTTOMS together:
Area = (1 × b × h) / (2 × 1 × 1)
1 × b × h is just b × h. And 2 × 1 × 1 is just 2. So it simplifies to:
Area = bh / 2

💡 This is NOT a new rule — it's the exact same rule as Lesson 1, just written as one fraction instead of "½ ×". Now that Area is written as a single fraction, we can use it to work backwards. That's part 2, next screen.

🔄 Solving Backwards For b Or h

The one new idea (part 2 of 2)

We now have Area = bh ÷ 2. To find a missing base or height, we move the pieces of this equation around — one piece at a time. Here's the ONE rule that makes this always work:

Whatever crosses the "=" sign flips its operation.
× becomes ÷, and ÷ becomes ×.
(Same idea as sorting letters in algebra: crossing "=" flips + to −, or − to +.)
Rule we just derived: Area = bh ÷ 2
Step 1 — the "÷ 2" is on the right side. To move it to the LEFT side, divide flips to multiply:
2 × Area = bh
Step 2 — that's the same equation, just written the other way round. Let's use it to solve for b, or for h:
bh = 2 × Area
Step 2a — to find b: right now, h is MULTIPLYING b. To move h across to the right side, multiply flips to divide:
b = (2 × Area) ÷ h
Step 2b — OR to find h instead: b is MULTIPLYING h. To move b across to the right side, multiply flips to divide:
h = (2 × Area) ÷ b

⚠️ Both new formulas start the exact same way: double the area first. Then divide by whichever piece you already have.

Worked Example — Find The Height

A triangle has an area of 40 cm² and a base of 10 cm. What is its height?

Step 1 — write the rearranged rule we just derived (h = (2×Area)÷b):
h = (2 × Area) ÷ b
Step 2 — put in the numbers:
h = (2 × 40) ÷ 10
Step 3 — double the area first:
2 × 40 = 80
Step 4 — divide by the base:
80 ÷ 10 = 8
height = 8 cm
Check it: ½ × 10 × 8 = ½ × 80 = 40 cm² ✓ matches!

Worked Example — Find The Base

A triangle has an area of 54 cm² and a height of 9 cm. What is its base?

Step 1 — write the rearranged rule we just derived (b = (2×Area)÷h):
b = (2 × Area) ÷ h
Step 2 — put in the numbers:
b = (2 × 54) ÷ 9
Step 3 — double the area first:
2 × 54 = 108
Step 4 — divide by the height:
108 ÷ 9 = 12
base = 12 cm
Check it: ½ × 12 × 9 = ½ × 108 = 54 cm² ✓ matches!

Composite Shapes — ADD The Triangle

A tent's cross-section is a rectangle (10 m wide, 6 m tall) with a triangular roof on top (base 10 m, height 4 m). What is the TOTAL area of the cross-section?

Step 1 — rectangle part:
10 × 6 = 60 m²
Step 2 — triangle part:
½ × 10 × 4 = 20 m²
Step 3 — ADD them together (the shapes are stacked, not overlapping):
60 + 20 = 80
Total area = 80 m²

Composite Shapes — SUBTRACT The Triangle

A rectangular sign (12 cm by 8 cm) has a triangular corner (base 6 cm, height 8 cm) cut off. What area is LEFT?

Step 1 — whole rectangle:
12 × 8 = 96 cm²
Step 2 — the cut-off triangle:
½ × 6 × 8 = 24 cm²
Step 3 — SUBTRACT the missing corner (it's been removed, not added):
96 − 24 = 72
Area left = 72 cm²

⚠️ The key question for any composite shape: is the triangle ADDED on (a roof, a bump) or CUT OUT (a corner missing, a hole)? That tells you whether to add or subtract.

Practice — Missing Piece & Composite Figures

P1.  A triangle has an area of 48 cm² and a base of 12 cm. What is its height?
h = (2×48) ÷ 12 = 96 ÷ 12
= 8 cm
P2.  A triangle has an area of 63 cm² and a height of 7 cm. What is its base?
b = (2×63) ÷ 7 = 126 ÷ 7
= 18 cm
P3.  A garden shed's front wall is a rectangle 8 m wide and 5 m tall, topped by a triangular gable (base 8 m, height 3 m). What is the TOTAL area of the front wall?
Rectangle: 8×5=40. Triangle: ½×8×3=12.
ADD: 40+12 = 52 m²
P4 (Word Problem).  A square garden is 10 m by 10 m. A triangular pond (base 8 m, height 5 m) is dug into one corner. What area of the garden is left as lawn?
Square: 10×10=100. Pond triangle: ½×8×5=20.
SUBTRACT (pond is dug out): 100−20 = 80 m²
📖 Today's Big Idea

To find a missing base or height, double the area first, then divide by the piece you already have: h = (2×Area)÷b, or b = (2×Area)÷h. For composite shapes, work out each piece separately, then ADD if the triangle is stuck on, or SUBTRACT if it's cut out.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You can rearrange Area = ½×b×h to solve for either the base or the height
  • You always double the area FIRST, before dividing
  • You can tell whether a composite shape needs ADDING or SUBTRACTING
  • You work out each piece of a composite shape separately before combining
  • You always finish by checking: did I answer the EXACT question asked?
