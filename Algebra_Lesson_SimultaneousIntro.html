🤔 What Are "Two Unknowns"?

You already know how to solve equations with one unknown — like 3x + 5 = 20. Now we'll solve problems with two unknowns — where you need two facts to find both answers.

Two common types you'll see:

📌 Sum & Difference: "Two numbers add to 30 and differ by 6"

📌 Multiple relationship: "Sam has 4× as many cards as Tim, total = 60"

📌 Two-variable totals: "24 animals, 68 legs — how many of each?"

The Golden Rule

Two unknowns → need TWO facts.
Pick one variable, express the other in terms of it, then solve.

➕➖ Method 1 — Sum & Difference

TYPE: Sum & Difference
Bigger = (Sum + Difference) ÷ 2    Smaller = (Sum − Difference) ÷ 2
Worked Example

Two numbers add up to 26. One number is 8 more than the other. Find both numbers.

1
Bigger = (26 + 8) ÷ 2 = 34 ÷ 2 = 17
2
Smaller = (26 − 8) ÷ 2 = 18 ÷ 2 = 9
3
Check: 17 + 9 = 26 ✅   17 − 9 = 8 ✅
💡 Why does it work? If B + S = Sum and B − S = Diff, then adding both equations gives: 2B = Sum + Diff. So B = (Sum+Diff)÷2.

✖️ Method 2 — Multiple Relationship

TYPE: Multiple Relationship

When one quantity is a multiple of another — use substitution.

Worked Example — Sticker collection

Amy has 3 times as many stickers as Ben. Together they have 72 stickers. How many does each have?

1
Let Ben = x. Then Amy = 3x.
2
Total: x + 3x = 72 → 4x = 72 → x = 18
3
Ben = 18, Amy = 3 × 18 = 54. Check: 18 + 54 = 72 ✅
Ben: [ x ] Amy: [ x ][ x ][ x ] Total = 4x = 72 → x = 18

🐓 Method 3 — Two-Variable Totals

TYPE: Two items with different values

These problems give you TWO totals — count of items AND a total value (legs, cost, weight, etc.).

Worked Example — Ducks and Turtles

A pond has ducks (2 legs) and turtles (4 legs). There are 20 animals and 56 legs in total. How many turtles?

1
Let turtles = t. Then ducks = 20 − t.
2
Legs equation: 4t + 2(20 − t) = 56
4t + 40 − 2t = 56 → 2t = 16 → t = 8
3
Turtles = 8, Ducks = 12. Check: 4(8)+2(12) = 32+24 = 56 ✅
💡 Quick trick: if ALL animals had 2 legs → 2×20=40 legs. But we have 56. Extra legs = 56−40=16. Each turtle has 2 extra legs → turtles = 16÷2 = 8. Same answer!

👫👦 Three-Person Problems

When three quantities are related — express all of them in terms of ONE variable.

Worked Example — Three siblings' savings

Three siblings saved a total of $90. The second saved $5 more than the first. The third saved twice as much as the first. How much did each save?

1
Let first = x. Then second = x + 5, third = 2x.
2
x + (x+5) + 2x = 90 → 4x + 5 = 90 → 4x = 85 → x = $21.25
3
First = $21.25, Second = $26.25, Third = $42.50
Check: 21.25+26.25+42.50 = $90 ✅
💡 Always pick the "base" person as x — the one the others are described in terms of.
