🖼️ Frame Trap — Visual Fix

You've solved inner paths and outer paths perfectly. But the frame type — where the outer dimensions are already given — has tripped you up 4 times in a row. Today we fix that permanently.

Unit 15 · Geometry · Lesson 1

🎯 What We're Fixing

In the frame type, the question gives you the outer rectangle dimensions already. There is no garden or pool to add onto — the outer box IS the big number.

❌ The Trap: "The outer is 40 cm × 28 cm, border is 4 cm" → student adds 4 cm outward → WRONG. The outer is already given. You subtract inward.
✅ The Fix: Outer is given → subtract border × 2 from each side to get inner → Frame = Outer − Inner

Today you'll master this with a visual anchor and 6 practice problems. All 6 will give you the outer dimensions. Never add outward.

🖼️ The Visual Anchor

Imagine a picture frame sitting on a table. What you see from the outside is the outer rectangle. The photo inside is the inner rectangle.

┌─────────────────────────┐ ← Outer (given in question) │ ┌───────────────────┐ │ │ │ │ │ ← Border width (given) │ │ Inner (find │ │ │ │ this area) │ │ │ └───────────────────┘ │ └─────────────────────────┘ Frame area = Outer area − Inner area
3-Step Frame Rule:
Step 1: Find outer area = length × width (already given)
Step 2: Find inner dims = each side − (border × 2)
Step 3: Frame = Outer − Inner
🚨 Key check: Does inner length = outer length − 2×border? If you got a BIGGER number, you went the wrong way!

📐 Worked Example

A rectangular mirror has outer dimensions 40 cm × 28 cm. The frame border is 4 cm wide. Find the area of the frame.

Outer: 40 × 28 cm (THIS IS GIVEN — don't touch it) Border: 4 cm wide all around Inner length = 40 − (4 × 2) = 40 − 8 = 32 cm Inner width = 28 − (4 × 2) = 28 − 8 = 20 cm Outer area = 40 × 28 = 1120 cm² Inner area = 32 × 20 = 640 cm² Frame area = 1120 − 640 = 480 cm²
Answer: Frame area = 480 cm²

⚠️ This was U14 Q3 — you added 4 cm outward and got ~608 cm². Notice: inner = outer − 8, NOT outer + 8.

✏️ Practice 1 & 2

P1. A rectangular wooden board has outer dimensions 25 cm × 18 cm. The frame border is 3 cm wide. What is the area of the frame?

Step 1: Inner dims = (25 − ?) × (18 − ?)

Frame area = cm²
Working: Outer = 25×18 = 450 cm². Inner = (25−6)×(18−6) = 19×12 = 228 cm². Frame = 450 − 228 = 222 cm²

P2. A rectangular tray has outer dimensions 32 cm × 24 cm. The raised border is 4 cm wide. Find the area of the base (inner area).

Note: this time find the INNER area, not the frame.

Inner area = cm²
Working: Inner = (32−8)×(24−8) = 24×16 = 384 cm²

⬛ Square Frames

When the outer shape is a square, both sides shrink by the same amount. The rule is the same — subtract border × 2 from the given outer side.

Square frame: Outer side = S, border = b
Inner side = S − 2b
Frame = S² − (S−2b)²

P3. A square photo frame has an outer side of 20 cm. The frame border is 2 cm wide. What is the area of the frame?

Frame area = cm²
Working: Outer = 20² = 400. Inner side = 20−4 = 16. Inner = 16² = 256. Frame = 400 − 256 = 144 cm²

💰 Frame + Cost

Some questions ask for the cost of making the frame. You first find the frame area, then multiply by the cost per unit area.

Frame area → × cost per cm² → Total cost

P4. A rectangular decorative border has outer dimensions 48 cm × 36 cm and a border width of 6 cm. The border material costs $4 per cm². What is the total cost?

Frame area = cm²    Total cost = $
Working: Outer = 48×36 = 1728 cm². Inner = (48−12)×(36−12) = 36×24 = 864 cm². Frame = 1728 − 864 = 864 cm². Cost = 864 × $4 = $3,456

🧱 Frame + Tiles

Another twist: the frame area is covered with tiles. Find the number of tiles needed.

Frame area ÷ tile area = number of tiles (round up if needed)

P5. A rectangular wall has outer dimensions 50 cm × 35 cm. A 5 cm border is tiled with square tiles, each 1 cm². How many tiles are needed?

Frame area = cm²    Tiles needed =
Working: Outer = 50×35 = 1750. Inner = (50−10)×(35−10) = 40×25 = 1000. Frame = 1750 − 1000 = 750 cm². Tiles = 750 ÷ 1 = 750 tiles

🏆 Mixed Challenge

Now try two problems back-to-back — no hints. Remember: outer is given, subtract inward.

C1. A rectangular signboard has outer dimensions 60 cm × 40 cm. The border is 5 cm wide and painted at a cost of $1.50 per cm². What is the total painting cost?

Frame area = cm²
Total cost = $
Working: Outer = 60×40 = 2400. Inner = (60−10)×(40−10) = 50×30 = 1500. Frame = 2400−1500 = 900 cm². Cost = 900 × $1.50 = $1,350

C2. A square tile design has an outer side of 30 cm with a 3 cm border. The border is made of gold tiles costing $5 per cm². What is the total cost?

Total cost = $
Working: Outer = 30² = 900. Inner = (30−6)² = 24² = 576. Frame = 900−576 = 324 cm². Cost = 324 × $5 = $1,620

🎉 Frame Trap — Conquered!

The Frame Rule (never forget):
1️⃣ Outer dims are GIVEN — they are already the big numbers
2️⃣ Inner = each outer dim − (border × 2)
3️⃣ Frame = Outer area − Inner area
4️⃣ If they ask for cost: Frame area × cost per unit
4️⃣ If they ask for tiles: Frame area ÷ tile area
🔑 Quick check: Is my inner SMALLER than my outer? If no → you went the wrong way!

You have 6 practice problems under your belt. The test will give you 5 frame questions — all with outer dims given. Apply the 3-step rule and you'll get them all.
