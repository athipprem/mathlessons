Chapter 4 · The missing height or base

▲ Run The Triangle Rule Backwards

Why this is worth learning. A sail maker knows how much cloth the sail needs (its area) and how wide it is. How tall must it be? A gardener knows the bed must hold a certain area and how long one side is. Real jobs often start with the AREA and ask for the missing side. Today you learn to find it — and to catch your own slips before the answer line.

U70: you NEVER used a slanted side — not once. That’s the big idea, and you’ve got it. Today we run it backwards.

Route A · The box

📦 Two Triangles Make The Box

The height is how tall the box is. Watch the second triangle fill the box.

Worked example:

Route A — the box. This triangle fits in a box 15 cm long; its top corner touches the lid. Its area is 30 cm². How tall is the box (the triangle's height)?

A second copy of the triangle (orange) flips in and fills the box. Two triangles make the box.

Area = 30 cm²told: the area of the triangle
Box = 30 × 2▲5 undo the half: the box (base × height) is twice the triangle
= 6030 × 2 = 60
Height = 60 ÷ 15▲6 the box is base × height, so the missing side = box ÷ the length of the box (15)
= 4 cm60 ÷ 15 = 4
15 × 4 = 60✔3 back-check: length of the box × my answer gives the box again
60 ÷ 2 = 30✔3 and half the box is the area in the question ✓
Two triangles make the box. So the box is area × 2.

Same triangle, new base

🔄 One Triangle, Two Bases

Find the area from the square corner. Then turn the triangle so the long side is the base, and run ▲5 and ▲6.

Worked example:

One triangle, two bases. A right-angled triangle has sides 45 cm, 60 cm and 75 cm. Find the height from the square corner to the 75 cm side.

The triangle as drawn
Turn it: the 75 cm side on the floor
Base = 60 cm▲1 use the base you can: the 60 cm side
Height = 45 cm▲2 its height is the 45 cm side (square corner)
Rectangle = 60 × 45▲3 base × height = the box the triangle fits in
= 270060 × 45 = 2700
Area = 2700 ÷ 2▲4 the triangle is half of the box
= 1350 cm²2700 ÷ 2 = 1350; area is in square units

📌 MY CARDS
THE TRIANGLE CARD
▲1 Pick a BASE — any side can be the base.
▲2 Its HEIGHT = the straight-up line from the opposite corner down to the base line. It meets the base line at a square corner ⊾. A slanted side is NOT the height. If the top corner hangs past the end of the base, stretch the base line — the height is outside.
▲3 Rectangle = base × height (the box the triangle fits in).
▲4 Area = rectangle ÷ 2 (the triangle is half the box). Write square units: cm², m².
▲5 Missing height or base? UNDO THE HALF first: box = area × 2.
▲6 The box is base × height, so the missing side = box ÷ the side you know.
ANSWER CHECK — on every question
✔1 What does it ask for? Read the question again — ALL of it (each triangle? the total? how much more?).
✔2 Copy check: is every number in my working the same as in the question?
✔3 Back-check: undo my last step (× ↔ ÷). Do I get the number I started with? (27 × 2 = 54 ✓ · 28 × 2 = 56 ✗)
FREE ITEMS
G “For every 3 you buy, 1 is free”: one GROUP = bought + free (3 + 1 = 4). Full groups = people ÷ group size. The extra people pay. Paid = groups × bought + extras.
THE BIG PICTURE
① Taken part = fraction × whole “1/4 OF the juice”
Told? “3/4 L” → Taken part = 3/4 L. No × needed!
1/3 OF 12 bricks: the bricks split into 3 equal groups, one group lights up, giving 4.
1/3 × 12 = 12/3 = 4. ‘Of’ means ×.
1/3 kg is just a small lump — that’s TOLD, not ×.
② Left part = whole − taken part
③ “… of what was LEFT”? → the left part is the NEW whole. Back to ①.
④ LAST CHECK: read the question again — does it ask for a TAKEN part, the LEFT part, or HOW MUCH MORE?
“How much more?” = bigger part − smaller part (that’s the − side too)

+1 Same bottoms (count up in the bigger bottom).

÷ FRACTIONS ARE DIVISIONS
a ÷ b = a/b   5 ÷ 6 → 5/6 each   13 ÷ 4 = 13/4 = 3 1/4
Improper → mixed: top ÷ bottom = wholes, remainder = the new top.
. FRACTION → DECIMAL
Make the bottom 10, 100 or 1000:   3/4 = 75/100 = 0.75
Can’t? Divide top by bottom:   1/3 = 0.333…
≈ ROUND to 2 decimal places: look at the 3rd digit → 5 or more, round up. 0.333… ≈ 0.33
B WORKING BACKWARDS (“how much at FIRST?”)
1. Left fraction = 1 − taken fraction
2. That many units = the amount you know
3. 1 unit = amount ÷ units
4. Whole = 1 unit × the bottom number
Two rounds? Undo the LAST round first, then the first round.
