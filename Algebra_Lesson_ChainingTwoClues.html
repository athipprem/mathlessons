⚖️ When ONE Clue Isn't Enough

Why learn this?

You already know how to solve an equation with letters on BOTH sides (Unit 21) — sort the letters to one side, the numbers to the other, then divide. Now we'll use that EXACT same skill on a new kind of puzzle: a balance scale with two DIFFERENT items mixed on both pans, PLUS a second clue that tells you a total weight. You'll sort the first clue into a ratio, then chain that ratio into the second clue to find real weights. This "use one answer inside the next step" move is one of the most common competition tricks.

Same equation-sorting skill from Unit 21. New job: use its answer to unlock a SECOND clue.

The Big Idea — Sort, Then Chain

In Unit 21, an equation like 6n+3 = 2n+35 had ONE letter (n). Here, a balance scale gives you an equation with TWO letters, like 2p+1e = 1p+4e (p and e for two different items). The method is identical:

Move all of ONE letter to the LEFT (subtract the smaller group from both sides). Move all of the OTHER letter to the RIGHT. Same sign-flip rule as Unit 21.

That gives you a ratio, like "1 pencil = 3 erasers." If a SECOND clue also mixes those two items with a total weight, swap in the ratio you just found, combine, and divide to get a real weight — then multiply back for the other item if needed.

⚠️ Always finish Step 1 (find the ratio) completely before touching Clue 2. Don't mix the two clues together too early.

Worked Example 1 — Just the Ratio

Q: On a balance scale, the left pan holds 2 pencils and 1 eraser. The right pan holds 1 pencil and 4 erasers. The scale balances. How many erasers weigh the same as 1 pencil?
StepEquationWhat we did
Step 12p + 1e = 1p + 4eWrite it as an equation, just like Unit 21 (p = pencil, e = eraser).
Step 22p−1p = 4e−1eMove all the p's to the LEFT and all the e's to the RIGHT — subtract the smaller group from both sides (same sign-flip rule as Unit 21).
Answer1p = 3e1 pencil = 3 erasers.

Worked Example 2 — The Full Chain

Q: On a balance scale, the left pan holds 3 cups and 1 saucer. The right pan holds 1 cup and 5 saucers. The scale balances. Separately, 1 cup + 1 saucer = 24 grams. How much does 1 saucer weigh?
StepEquationWhat we did
Step 13c + 1s = 1c + 5sWrite Clue 1 as an equation (c = cup, s = saucer).
Step 23c−1c = 5s−1sSort: c's LEFT, s's RIGHT — subtract the smaller group from both sides.
Step 32c = 4s → 1c = 2sSimplify. Ratio found: 1 cup = 2 saucers.
Step 41c + 1s = 24gNow bring in Clue 2 — the total weight equation.
Step 52s + 1s = 24gSubstitute: swap "1 cup" for "2 saucers" (from Step 3).
Step 63s = 24gCombine like terms.
Answer1s = 24 ÷ 3 = 8g1 saucer = 8 grams.

🗺️ The Plan

1Write Clue 1 as an equation (pick a letter for each item).
2Sort: move one letter's terms to the LEFT, the other letter's terms to the RIGHT — subtract the smaller group from both sides (Unit 21 rule).
3Simplify to get a clean ratio: 1 [item] = n [other item].
4Is there a second clue with a total weight? If not, you're done — the ratio IS the answer.
5If yes: write Clue 2 as an equation, then SUBSTITUTE the ratio from Step 3 in place of one item.
6Combine like terms, divide to find one item's weight.
7Multiply back using the Step 3 ratio if the question asks for the OTHER item's weight.

⚠️ Finish sorting Clue 1 completely (Step 3) BEFORE touching Clue 2. Don't substitute partway through sorting.

Sort Clue 1 into a ratio (Unit 21's method), then substitute that ratio into Clue 2, combine, divide, multiply back.

Same equation-sorting skill you already know — just used twice, back to back.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You finish sorting Clue 1 completely before touching Clue 2
  • You substitute the FULL ratio, not just part of it
  • You multiply back for the other item if that's what's asked
