🎯 A New Plan — Easy Words

Last week's drill used confusing words. That was my mistake, not yours. New plan: two easy words — the TAKEN piece and the LEFT-OVER piece — and we start with easy numbers so you can see what's happening.

Why this is worth learning

Pouring some juice from a bottle, cutting a piece off a rope, using some flour for a cake — every time, there are two pieces: the bit you take and the bit that is left over. Knowing both, quickly, is useful in the kitchen, in the workshop and in every maths paper.

Two colours, the same in every lesson from now on:

🟦 taken piece⬜ left-over piece
Draw the bar. Shade the taken piece. Then fill two boxes.

📏 Route A — The Bar

A bottle has 12 L of juice. Ben drinks 1/3 of the juice.

The bar is the bottle: 12 L. Cut it into 3 equal pieces (that is the 3 in 1/3). Each piece is 12 ÷ 3 = 4 L. Shade 1 piece.

🟦 shaded = taken piece    ⬜ white = left-over piece

Taken piece = 4 L
Left-over piece = 8 L
The juice he drinks is a piece of the bottle. Cut the bar, shade the piece.

💧 The Other Kind — The Story TELLS You

A bottle has 12 L of juice. Ben drinks 1/3 L.

1/3 L is just a small splash — the story told us exactly how much was taken.

the taken piece is only a tiny sliver of the bottle

Taken piece = 1/3 L
Left-over piece = 11 2/3 L
Taken piece = 1/3 L (the story told us)
Left-over piece = 12 − 1/3 = 11 2/3 L
1/3 OF the juice
= 4 L — a big piece
1/3 L
= a small splash
Does the story TELL you how much was taken? Then that's the taken piece. If it says '1/3 OF the juice', work it out first.

🔢 Route B — The Same Bottle, As Numbers

Same bottle, written as numbers.
Taken = 1/3 × 12 = 4 L (1/3 of the whole)
Left over = 12 − 4 = 8 L (whole take away the taken piece)
or: 2/3 is left over → 2/3 × 12 = 8 L

⭐ Your Turn

Practice — Fill Both Boxes

Draw the bar on your paper first.

P1.  A tank has 20 L of water. Mia uses 3/4 of the water.
The story says 3/4 OF the water — work out the taken piece first (a piece of the bar)
Taken = 3/4 × 20 = 15 L
Left over = 20 − 15 = 5 L (whole take away the taken piece)
P2.  A rope is 25 m long. Leo cuts off 2/5 of the rope.
The story says 2/5 OF the rope — work out the taken piece first (a piece of the bar)
Taken = 2/5 × 25 = 10 m
Left over = 25 − 10 = 15 m (whole take away the taken piece)
P3.  A bag has 9 kg of rice. Sam cooks 2/3 kg of the rice.
The story TELLS us how much was taken: 2/3 kg — so that is the taken piece
Left over = 9 − 2/3 = 8 1/3 kg (whole take away the taken piece)
P4.  A bag has 24 kg of flour. Jo uses 5/8 of the flour.
The story says 5/8 OF the flour — work out the taken piece first (a piece of the bar)
Taken = 5/8 × 24 = 15 kg
Left over = 24 − 15 = 9 kg (whole take away the taken piece)

Finish-the-question check: did you answer the EXACT question asked, is your fraction in simplest form, and did you write the UNIT?
