🎯 A New Plan — Easy Words

Last week's drill used confusing words. That was my mistake, not yours. New plan: two easy words — the TAKEN piece and the LEFT-OVER piece — and we start with easy numbers so you can see what's happening.

Why this is worth learning

Pouring some juice from a bottle, cutting a piece off a rope, using some flour for a cake — every time, there are two pieces: the bit you take and the bit that is left over. Knowing both, quickly, is useful in the kitchen, in the workshop and in every maths paper.

Two colours, the same in every lesson from now on:

🟦 taken piece⬜ left-over piece
Draw the bar. Shade the taken piece. Then fill two boxes.

📏 Route A — The Bar

A bottle has 12 L of juice. Ben drinks 1/3 of the juice.

The bar is the bottle: 12 L. Cut it into 3 equal pieces (that is the 3 in 1/3). Shade 1 piece. Tap Next line to see each move.

🟦 shaded = taken piece    ⬜ white = left-over piece

One piece = 12 ÷ 3cut the 12 L bar into 3 equal pieces
= 412 ÷ 3 = 4, so one piece is 4 L
Taken = 4 × 1the taken piece is 1 of those pieces (the top number)
= 4 L4 × 1 = 4
Left over = 12 − 4the whole take away the taken piece
= 8 L12 − 4 = 8
Taken piece = 4 L
Left-over piece = 8 L

🔢 Route B — The Same Bottle, As Numbers

Same bottle, written as a times sum. Watch: same answer.
Taken = 1/3 × 12"1/3 of the juice" means 1/3 × the whole
= 1/3 × 12/1a whole number is a fraction over 1: 12 = 12/1
= 12/3tops: 1 × 12 = 12; bottoms: 3 × 1 = 3
= 4 L12 ÷ 3 = 4 — the same 4 L the bar gave us
Left over = 12 − 4the whole take away the taken piece
= 8 L12 − 4 = 8
Bar or times sum — they always agree. In Lesson 2 the times sum does the heavy lifting.

💧 The Other Kind — The Story TELLS You

A bottle has 12 L of juice. Ben drinks 1/3 L.

1/3 L is just a small splash — the story told us exactly how much was taken.

Taken = 1/3 Lthe story TOLD us the taken piece: a number with a unit right after it
Left over = 12 − 1/3the whole take away the taken piece
= 36/3 − 1/3make the bottoms the same: 12 wholes = 36 thirds
= 35/336 thirds take away 1 third = 35 thirds
= 11 2/3 L35 thirds = 11 wholes and 2 thirds over

🗺 The First-Step Map

The bar shows you what the story is. The map tells you what to write first. Tap Next step and watch the path light up.

Walk 1: the bar story (Ben drinks 1/3 of the juice). Walk 2: the told story (Ben drinks 1/3 L).

START Find the WHOLE (what was there at the start). Draw the bar. Write the whole on top.
▼
Q1 Does the story TELL me the taken piece?Look right after the fraction: is there a unit (L, kg, m)?
▼
YES — "drinks 1/3 L" → Taken = 1/3 L. Jump to Q3.
Example: A bottle has 12 L. Ben drinks 1/3 L. The taken piece is 1/3 L.
NO — "drinks 1/3 of the juice" → work it out. Go to Q2.
▼
Q2 Is the whole a whole number, or a fraction bottle?
▼
WHOLE NUMBER (12 L) → One piece = whole ÷ bottom number. Taken = one piece × top number.
Example: 1/3 of 12 L: one piece 12 ÷ 3, taken 4 × 1.
FRACTION BOTTLE (2 4/5 L) → Taken = fraction × whole, then one line each: change the whole to an improper fraction · tops × tops, bottoms × bottoms · simplify · back to a mixed number.
Example: 1/4 of 2 4/5 L: 1/4 × 14/5, then 14/20, then 7/10 L.
▼
Q3 Which box does the question ask for?
▼
"How much does he drink / use / cut off?" → the TAKEN box. Done.
"How much is left?" → Left over = whole − taken, then one line each: improper fractions · same bottoms · take away · simplify · mixed number.
▼
BONUS Does the story keep going: "then … of what was left"? The left-over piece becomes the NEW whole. Go back to START.
