🎯 Why This Matters

Real life uses this all the time

Seating friends around a round birthday table, threading beads onto a bracelet, hanging lanterns around a circular tent, arranging charms on a circular keychain — every one of these is a circular arrangement, and often some of the items look IDENTICAL (two same-color beads, three matching flags). Getting the ORDER of steps right is what separates a correct answer from a wrong one.

Why THIS lesson, right now

On the last big review, the repeats formula and the circle formula were both solid ON THEIR OWN — but when a question combined BOTH ideas, the steps got done in the wrong order, and one specific case (a letter or item repeating exactly 4 times) still trips things up. This lesson locks in the correct ORDER and finishes the job on repeats of 4.

Two fixes today: (1) circle step ALWAYS first, repeats step SECOND. (2) repeats of 4 → divide by 4! = 24, not by 4.

🗺️ Pocket Map — One Rule To Steer By

Before solving ANY arrangement question, ask these in order:

1Is it arranged in a CIRCLE (round table, bracelet, keyring, pole)? If yes → start with (n−1)!, NOT n!.
2Are any items IDENTICAL (same color, same letter, same twin)? If yes → divide by r! for each repeated group, AFTER the circle step.
3Is it in a STRAIGHT LINE with a spacing rule (fence posts, trees, lamp posts)? That's a different move — covered in the next lesson, "Fencepost Problems."

⚠️ Today's lesson trains Moves 1 and 2, done in the RIGHT ORDER, together. Move 3 gets its own lesson right after this one.

Quick Recap — Two Moves You Already Know

Move 1 alone — circle, no repeats. 7 friends sit around a round table. How many different circular seatings?

Rule: (n−1)!
n = 7 friends. Circular → (n−1)! = 6!
6! = 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 720
720 different circular seatings

Move 2 alone — repeats, no circle. 6 beads are placed in a straight row. 3 of the beads are identical red. How many different-looking rows?

Rule: n! ÷ r!
n = 6 beads. 3 are identical (r = 3). Straight row → start with n! (no minus-1 here — that's only for circles).
6! ÷ 3! = 720 ÷ 6 = 120
120 different-looking rows
Both moves are solid. Today's job is combining them in the RIGHT ORDER.

Worked Example — Circle + Repeats, Step By Step

8 charms are threaded onto a circular bracelet. 2 of the charms are identical gold stars. How many different-looking bracelets can be made?

Rule, in order: (n−1)! FIRST, then ÷ r! for each repeat
Step 1 — count total items: n = 8 charms
Step 2 — is it a circle? Yes → start with (n−1)! = 7!
Step 3 — work out 7!: 7! = 7 × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 5040
Step 4 — are any items identical? Yes — 2 gold stars are identical → r = 2
Step 5 — divide by r!: 5040 ÷ 2! = 5040 ÷ 2 = 2520
2520 different-looking bracelets
Circle step BEFORE repeats step. Every single time.

🔍 Finishing The Job — When 4 Items Repeat

You already know these two cold:

2!
2 × 1 = 2
3!
3 × 2 × 1 = 6

Here's the one that keeps slipping — build it the SAME way:

4!
4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24

⚠️ When something repeats 4 times, you divide by 4! = 24 — NOT by 4. This is the exact same pattern as 2! and 3!, just one step further. Never divide by the plain count.

Try it: 9 tiles are placed in a straight row. 4 of the tiles are identical red. The other 5 tiles are all different colors. How many different-looking rows?

n = 9 tiles. Straight row (no circle) → start with n! = 9!
9! = 362,880
4 tiles repeat → r = 4. Divide by 4! = 24 (NOT by 4).
362,880 ÷ 24 = 15,120
15,120 different-looking rows
⭐ Capstone — Both Fixes Together

8 Charms, Circular Bracelet, 4 Identical

8 charms are threaded onto a circular bracelet. 4 of the charms are identical silver stars. The other 4 charms are all different. How many different-looking bracelets can be made?

(n−1)! FIRST, then ÷ 4! = 24
Step 1 — count total items: n = 8 charms
Step 2 — circle? Yes. Start with (n−1)! = 7!
7! = 5040
Step 3 — repeats? Yes, 4 identical → r = 4. Divide by 4! = 24.
5040 ÷ 24 = 210
210 different-looking bracelets
Same two fixes, used together: circle first, then divide by the FACTORIAL of every repeat count.

🗺️ The 5-Step Checklist

Use this EXACT order on every circle/repeats question, including in the test:

1Count the total items → that's n.
2Is it arranged in a CIRCLE? If yes, start with (n−1)! — NOT n!.
3Find every item/letter that repeats, and how many times each one repeats.
4Divide by r! for EACH repeated group — remember 4! = 24, not 4.
5Re-read the question: does "circular" or "round" appear? If yes, double check you used (n−1)! and not n! before you write your final answer.

⚠️ Step 2 happens BEFORE Step 4, always. The circle adjustment and the repeats adjustment are two separate moves, done in that order.

📖 Today's Two Fixes

1) Circle step ALWAYS goes first: (n−1)!, not n!. 2) Any repeat of 4 divides by 4! = 24, never by 4.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You check "is this a circle?" BEFORE doing anything else
  • You never divide by the plain repeat count (like 4) instead of its factorial (4! = 24)
  • You can explain out loud WHY the circle step comes before the repeats step
