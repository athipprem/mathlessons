🎯 Why — One Trick, Not Five Rules

Before anything else:

Last test was too hard, and that was my fault, not yours — too many rules at once. Here's what your paper showed me: every time the story was about a SET of things — like the tickets — you got it. So this lesson has ONE trick: turn any amount into a set of pieces. Then it's a question you already know how to do.

Turn the amount into a SET of equal pieces.

⚡ Route B — The Shortcut

Look: the 10 pieces you counted is the TOP of 10/3. That's why the shortcut works.
3/5 × 3 1/3
= 3/5 × 10/3 (3 1/3 = 10 thirds)
cancel the 3s, and 10 ÷ 5 = 2: 1/1 × 2/1
= 2 m

📝 The Frame, Filled In

A jug holds 1 3/5 L of juice. Tia pours 3/4 of it into a glass. How much juice is in the glass?

Here is what goes in the frame:

The WHOLE is:1 3/5 L
Cut it into pieces:8 pieces of 1/5 L
Working:8 ÷ 4 = 2, × 3 = 6 pieces
Answer (with unit):6 fifths = 1 1/5 L

⚖ One Contrast — "1/3 L" Or "1/3 OF It"?

Two cards, one word apart. Do both.

A pot has 1 1/5 L of soup. Dan eats 1/3 OF the soup. How much does he eat?
A pot has 1 1/5 L of soup. Dan eats 1/3 L. How much is left?
OF → 6 pieces of 1/5 L → 6 ÷ 3 × 1 = 2 pieces = 2/5 L
1/3 L → no pieces needed, it is an amount: 1 1/5 − 1/3 = 13/15 L
OF → count the pieces. A fraction with a unit after it (1/3 L) → it's an amount.

📖 Today's Big Idea

To find a fraction OF an amount, cut the amount into equal pieces and count them. Then it is a set — and you already know sets.
