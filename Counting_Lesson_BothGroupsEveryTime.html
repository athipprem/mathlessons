⚡ The One Rule

You already know circular arrangements cold. This is a 2-minute add-on for ONE specific situation: when there are TWO (or more) different identical groups at the table.

If there's more than ONE repeat group — divide by EACH group's factorial, one after another. Never just one.

That's it. Same circular method you already know (subtract 1, then factorial). The only new step: if you count 2 groups, you divide TWICE.

👀 Quick Example

10 people sit around a circular table. Among them, 4 are wearing identical black caps, and 3 are wearing identical white caps (the rest are all different). How many different arrangements are possible?

Step 1 — circular arrangements, like always: (10−1)! = 9! = 362,880
Step 2 — how many repeat groups? Count them: 2 groups (black, white).
Step 3 — divide by the FIRST group's factorial: 362,880 ÷ 4! = 362,880 ÷ 24 = 15,120
Step 4 — divide by the SECOND group's factorial too: 15,120 ÷ 3! = 15,120 ÷ 6 = 2,520
2,520 different arrangements

⚠️ The One Mistake This Fixes

✗ Stopping after ONE group

362,880 ÷ 4! = 15,120 — then stopping. This forgets the white-cap group entirely. 15,120 is too big and wrong.

✓ Dividing by BOTH groups

362,880 ÷ 4! ÷ 3! = 2,520 — correct. Every repeat group gets its own division, no skipping.

Before you write your final answer, always ask: "How many groups did I count? Did I divide that many times?"
