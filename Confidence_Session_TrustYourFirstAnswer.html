🔑 The ONE rule for exam day

You're allowed to check your answer. Checking is smart. But there's a right way and a wrong way to check.

Only change your answer if you can point to ONE exact number that's wrong.

Right reason to change an answer: "Wait — I wrote 4×4, but it should have been 4×3. Let me fix that number."

Wrong reason to change an answer: "Hmm, that doesn't feel right." / "That seems too easy." / "I'm not sure, let me just try something else."

A feeling is not a reason. A wrong number is a reason.

If your working is there and the arithmetic is correct, the answer is correct — even if it feels surprising. Trust the numbers on the page, not the worry in your head.
