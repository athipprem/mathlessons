🎯 You Know The Steps — Let's Make The Numbers Easy

What actually happened last time

On the last test, the circle-first-then-repeats ORDER was right almost everywhere it mattered. The questions that went blank all shared one thing: they needed a BIG factorial like 8! (that's 40,320) computed by hand, and there wasn't an easy way to get there. Multiplying 8×7×6×5×4×3×2×1 all in one go, from scratch, is genuinely hard for anyone — it's not a sign you don't understand circles or repeats.

The fix

There's a shortcut you've actually used before: you never have to multiply 8 numbers in one go. You just need the PREVIOUS factorial, and one more multiplication. This lesson makes that shortcut automatic.

Never restart from scratch. Build UP from the last factorial you already know.

🪜 The Staircase — One Small Step At A Time

Tap each step. Each one only needs ONE multiplication, using the step right before it.

⚠️ Look at what you just did: to get from 6! to 9!, you never multiplied more than TWO numbers at a time. You just kept using your last answer.

Worked Example — Using The Staircase Inside A Real Question

9 lanterns are hung around a circular arch. 2 of the lanterns are identical purple. How many different-looking arrangements can be made?

(n−1)! FIRST, then ÷ r! — build the factorial UP as you go
Step 1 — count total items, check for a circle: n = 9, circular → need (9−1)! = 8!
Step 2 — build UP to 8!, writing every step down:
5! = 120 (already know this)
6! = 6 × 120 = 720
7! = 7 × 720 = 5,040
8! = 8 × 5,040 = 40,320
Step 3 — repeats? Yes, 2 identical → divide by 2!: 40,320 ÷ 2 = 20,160
20,160 different-looking arrangements
Four small multiplications, written down one at a time — never one giant one.

🎯 The Other Fix — Trust Your Own Working

Here's something else worth fixing: one question last time had PERFECT working shown — a fully correct long division, right down to the last line — but the number written on the answer line didn't match it. The working said one thing; the hand wrote something else.

⚠️ After you finish your working, STOP. Look at the very last number you wrote. Copy THAT number onto the answer line — don't try to remember it or re-do it in your head.

Last working line = answer line. Copy, don't recall.

Quick Recap — The Order Rule (You've Got This Part)

This part was already working well last time — just a fast refresh:

1Is it a CIRCLE? Start with (n−1)! — not n!.
2Are any items IDENTICAL? Divide by r! for each repeated group, AFTER the circle step.
3Building the factorial? Use the STAIRCASE — one step up from the last value you know.

🗺️ The Full Checklist

1Count n. Check: circle or straight line?
2Circle → you need (n−1)!. Straight → you need n!.
3Building a big factorial? Climb the staircase: write down each step (5!, 6!, 7!…) using the one before it. Never restart from 1.
4Repeats? Divide by r! for each repeated group.
5Look at your LAST working line. Copy that exact number onto the answer line.

⚠️ If a number feels too big to hold in your head, that's exactly when the staircase helps most — write every step down, one at a time.

n=8, circular → (8−1)! = 7! (staircase: 5!=120, 6!=720, 7!=7×720=5,040)
4 repeat → 5,040 ÷ 24 = 210
210
P4.  9 charms are threaded onto a circular keychain. 3 of the charms are identical navy. How many different-looking keychains?
n=9, circular → (9−1)! = 8! (staircase: 5!=120, 6!=720, 7!=5,040, 8!=8×5,040=40,320)
3 repeat → 40,320 ÷ 6 = 6,720
6,720
📖 Today's Two Fixes

1) Build factorials UP the staircase, one small step at a time — never restart from scratch. 2) Copy your LAST working line onto the answer line — don't recall it from memory.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You never try to multiply more than 2 numbers together in one step
  • You write down 5!, 6!, 7!, 8! as you climb, even if the question only asks for the last one
  • Your answer line always matches your own last working line, exactly

The circle-and-repeats rule was never the problem. Now the numbers won't be either. Ready for the retest!
