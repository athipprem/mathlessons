⏰ What is LCM Scheduling?

In Unit 12, you found LCM values. Now we use LCM to answer real-world timing questions:

The core question: "When do they meet again?"

• Two buses leave every 15 and 20 minutes — when do they depart together again?

• A red light flashes every 8 seconds, blue every 12 seconds — when do they flash together?

• Three bells ring at different intervals — when do all three ring at once?

The Answer is Always: LCM

Find the Least Common Multiple of all the intervals.
That's the number of seconds/minutes/days until they meet again.

💡 LCM = "Least Common Multiple". Use prime factorisation to find it accurately.

🔧 LCM by Prime Factorisation — Quick Reminder

The fastest, most reliable method for LCM:

Step 1: Prime factorise each number

Step 2: Take the HIGHEST power of each prime that appears

Step 3: Multiply them together

Example — LCM of 12 and 18
12 = 2² × 3¹ 18 = 2¹ × 3² LCM = 2² × 3² = 4 × 9 = 36

Take 2² (highest power of 2) and 3² (highest power of 3). Multiply: 4 × 9 = 36.

Example — LCM of 6, 10, 15
6 = 2¹ × 3¹ 10 = 2¹ × 5¹ 15 = 3¹ × 5¹ LCM = 2¹ × 3¹ × 5¹ = 30

All primes: 2, 3, 5 — each appears once. LCM = 2 × 3 × 5 = 30.

🚨 For LCM: use the HIGHEST power. For HCF: use the LOWEST power of COMMON primes only.

🚌 Two-Event Scheduling

Worked Example — Two trains

Train A departs every 14 minutes and Train B every 21 minutes. They both depart at 9:00 AM. When is the next time they depart together?

1
Factorise: 14 = 2 × 7    21 = 3 × 7
2
LCM = 2 × 3 × 7 = 42 minutes
3
9:00 AM + 42 min = 9:42 AM

🚦 Three-Event Scheduling

For three or more events — still the same method. Just find LCM of all the intervals.

Worked Example — Three buses

Three buses leave at 7:00 AM. Bus A every 6 min, Bus B every 10 min, Bus C every 15 min. When do all three leave together again?

1
6 = 2 × 3    10 = 2 × 5    15 = 3 × 5
2
LCM = 2 × 3 × 5 = 30 minutes
3
7:00 AM + 30 min = 7:30 AM
💡 Three variables? No problem. Find LCM(A,B) first, then LCM(result, C). Or just write all prime factorisations and take highest powers of each.

📊 "How Many Times" Problems

Sometimes you're asked: "How many times do they meet in the next X hours/days?"

Number of meetings = Total time ÷ LCM  (take the whole number part)
Example

Two events happen every 15 minutes and 20 minutes. Starting at noon, how many times do they happen together in the next 2 hours?

1
LCM(15, 20) = ?   15=3×5, 20=2²×5 → LCM=2²×3×5=60 min
2
2 hours = 120 minutes. Meetings = 120 ÷ 60 = 2 times (at 1:00 PM and 2:00 PM)

🎯 Four Numbers & Harder Setups

Worked Example — Four bells

Four bells ring at intervals of 4, 6, 8, and 12 minutes. They all ring together at noon. When do they next all ring together?

4 = 2² 6 = 2 × 3 8 = 2³ 12 = 2² × 3 LCM = 2³ × 3 = 24 minutes → 12:24 PM
💡 For four numbers, just write all factorisations in a column and take the highest power of each prime. Quick and clean.

📋 LCM Scheduling — Summary

The 3-Step Process

1. Identify all intervals (seconds, minutes, days)
2. Find LCM using prime factorisation
3. Add LCM to the start time / day

"Next together"
Find LCM → Add to start time
"How many times"
Total period ÷ LCM (whole number)
"Nth time after"
Start + LCM × n
