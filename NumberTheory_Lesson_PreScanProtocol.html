⚠️ 4 Units — Same Mistake

Why Does the Trigger Keep Failing?

In Units 5, 6, 7, and 8, Q1–Q3 were left completely blank. All of them were prime factorisation questions. You know how to do factor trees — we've drilled them many times. So what's going wrong?

The problem: When you sit down and see Q1 — "Factorise 56" — your brain jumps straight to "what's the answer?" It doesn't say "this is prime factorisation". The trigger word ("factorise") hasn't been spotted yet, so the method never loads.

This is a cold-start problem. The method is in your head — but it doesn't activate unless you consciously recognise the keyword first.

❌ What happens now

See Q1 → blank mind → skip → blank

✅ What we need

Spot keyword FIRST → trigger loads → solve

The solution is a pre-scan — a 60-second habit you do before writing a single answer.

🔍 The Protocol

Pre-Scan: 5 Steps Before You Answer Q1

Every time you receive a test paper, do this before writing any answer:

1
Pick up your pencil. Don't start writing yet.
2
Read through ALL questions — just the question text, not solving.
3
Circle every trigger word you see:
prime   factor   factorise   factors of   how many factors
4
Count how many you circled → write that number in the margin.
5
NOW start answering from Q1.
Why it works: Reading all questions first tells your brain what methods are needed before it needs them. By the time you reach Q1, your brain already knows a factor tree is coming — so it fires immediately.

The scan takes about 60 seconds. It will save you 3+ blank questions every test.

The One Habit That Changes Everything

You don't need to get faster or smarter to fix the blank problem. You just need one 60-second habit at the start of every test.

🔍 SCAN FIRST. Answer second.
From U9 onwards — every test, every time:
1. Scan all questions. Circle trigger keywords.
2. Count the circles. Write the number in margin.
3. Then start Q1.

The trigger gap has lasted 4 units not because you can't factorise, but because the signal to start never fired. The scan fires it.
