🎯 Why — Two Fractions, Two Wholes

This is the harder half of the same idea.

In a story with two fractions, each fraction acts on a DIFFERENT whole. The whole trick is asking: OF WHAT?

Every fraction needs its own "of what?" before you can multiply.

📏 Route A — Redraw The Bar For Each New Whole

A rope is 7 1/2 m long. Jo uses 2/5 of the rope for a swing. She uses 1/4 of the swing rope for knots. How much rope is in the knots?

Bar 1 — the whole rope, 7 1/2 m in 5 equal parts. 2 shaded = the swing rope = 2/5 × 7 1/2 = 3 m.
Bar 2, redrawn — the SWING ROPE (3 m) is the new whole, cut into 4. 1 shaded = the knots = 1/4 × 3 = 3/4 m.

🔗 Route B — The Arrow Chain

Each new bar is a new whole. Written down, it's a chain of OF-sentences.
rope 7 1/2 m → swing = 2/5 of 7 1/2 = 3 m → knots = 1/4 of 3 m = 3/4 m
Before every fraction, ask: OF WHAT? Write the answer above the arrow.

👥 "Of The Rest" — The Whole Is The REST

In a class, 2/5 of the children sing in the choir. Of the rest, 1/3 play football.

(a) What fraction of the class play football?

1 of the 3 "rest" parts plays football = 1 part out of 5 total.
rest = 1 − 2/5 = 3/5
football = 1/3 × 3/5 = 1/5 of the class

(b) 9 children play football. How many children are in the class?

1/5 of the class = 9
class = 9 × 5 = 45
"Of the rest" — the rest is the new whole. Find the rest FIRST.

🔄 "As Many As" Means Times

Tuesday: 3/5 AS MANY pages AS Monday
→
Tuesday = 3/5 OF Monday's pages

Ella read 5/12 of a book on Monday. On Tuesday she read 3/5 as many pages as on Monday.

(a) What fraction of the book did she read on Tuesday?

3/5 × 5/12 = 1/4

(b) She read 24 pages on Tuesday. How many pages are in the book?

1/4 of the book = 24
book = 24 × 4 = 96

Check: Monday = 5/12 × 96 = 40 pages. Tuesday = 3/5 × 40 = 24 pages. ✓

⭐ Your Turn

Practice — Of What?

P1.  A cake weighs 2 2/3 kg. 3/4 of the cake is sponge. 3/8 of the sponge is iced. How much of the cake is iced sponge?
sponge = 3/4 × 2 2/3 = 3/4 × 8/3 = 2 kg
iced = 3/8 × 2
= 3/4 kg
P2.  In a box, 1/5 of the pens are red. 1/4 of the rest are blue. (a) What fraction of all the pens are blue? (b) 6 pens are blue. How many pens are in the box?
rest = 1 − 1/5 = 4/5
blue = 1/4 × 4/5 = 1/5 of all the pens
1/5 of the box = 6 → box = 6 × 5
= 30
P3.  Max ran 3 3/4 km. Sam ran 4/5 as far as Max. How far did Sam run?
"as far as" = ×: 4/5 × 3 3/4 = 4/5 × 15/4
= 3 km
P4.  Raj had 2 1/2 L of juice. He drank 2/5 of it. Then he poured out 1/4 L. How much juice is left?
drank (of it, ×): 2/5 × 2 1/2 = 1 L
after drinking: 2 1/2 − 1 = 1 1/2 L
poured out 1/4 L is an AMOUNT — take away: 1 1/2 − 1/4
= 1 1/4 L
📖 Today's Big Idea

Every fraction has its own "of what?" — find the whole it acts on before you multiply. "Of the rest" means find the rest first. "As many as" means times.

Finish-the-question check: before you write your final answer — did you answer the EXACT question asked, is your fraction in simplest form, and did you write the UNIT?
