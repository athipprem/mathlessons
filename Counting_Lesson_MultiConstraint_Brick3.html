🎯 Why Learn This?

Same skill, brand-new picture

Competitions love asking about points and triangles: "here are some dots — how many triangles can you make by joining any 3 of them?" It looks totally different from boys/girls or coloured counters, but it uses the EXACT same Total Minus Bad skill you already own.

This is the last brick. You'll also learn something new: working BACKWARDS — when you're TOLD the answer and have to find a missing number instead.

🔁 Choose-3, New Picture — Points Make Triangles

Any 3 points that are NOT all on one straight line can be joined to make exactly ONE triangle. So "how many triangles?" is really just asking "how many groups of 3 points?" — your Choose-3 rule again.

6 points are drawn, no 3 of them in a straight line. How many different triangles can be made by joining any 3?

1
6 × 5 × 4 ÷ 6
6 is the total number of points (n=6). 5,4 count down (n−1, n−2) — exactly your Choose-3 rule. Divide by 6 to remove repeat-orders, since triangle "A,B,C" is the same triangle as "C,B,A".
6×5=30, 30×4=120, 120÷6=20 triangles
Triangles from n points (none collinear) = Choose 3 from n = n×(n−1)×(n−2)÷6

💡 The New Idea — Straight Lines Are NOT Triangles

8 points are drawn. 4 of them happen to lie on one straight line (red, dashed). How many triangles can be made from any 3 of the 8 points?

Why some groups of 3 DON'T count

If you pick 3 points that are ALL on that same red line, they don't make a triangle — they just make a straight line. Those groups are "BAD" — you must count them and subtract.

Step 1 — ALL (every possible group of 3, ignoring the straight line for now)
1
8 × 7 × 6 ÷ 6
8 is all the points (n=8). 7,6 count down. Divide by 6, same reason as always.
8×7=56, 56×6=336, 336÷6=56 (this is ALL)
Step 2 — BAD (groups of 3 that are all on the red line)
2
4 × 3 × 2 ÷ 6
4 is how many points sit on that one straight line (n=4 — we switch pools to just the collinear points). 3,2 count down. Divide by 6.
4×3=12, 12×2=24, 24÷6=4 straight-line "bad" groups
Step 3 — Subtract
✓
56 − 4
ALL groups of 3 minus the straight-line groups leaves only the groups that actually form a triangle.
56 − 4 = 52 triangles
Points on a straight line = a BAD case (Choose 3 from JUST that line), exactly like Choose 3 from JUST the boys, or JUST one colour.

🔗 Two Straight Lines At Once — Your Brick 2 Skill Returns

9 points in total: 4 lie on one straight line (blue), a different 3 lie on another straight line (green), and 2 points aren't on either line. How many triangles can be made?

This is exactly Brick 2's "more than one bad case" idea, just with straight lines instead of colours.

Step 1 — ALL
1
9 × 8 × 7 ÷ 6
9 is all the points (n=9). 8,7 count down. Divide by 6.
9×8=72, 72×7=504, 504÷6=84 (this is ALL)
Step 2 — BAD, one line at a time
a
4 × 3 × 2 ÷ 6
Blue line: 4 points sit on it (n=4). 3,2 count down. Divide by 6.
4×3=12, 12×2=24, 24÷6=4 bad groups on the blue line
b
3 × 2 × 1 ÷ 6
Green line: 3 points sit on it (n=3, so there's only ONE way to pick all 3). 2,1 count down. Divide by 6.
3×2=6, 6×1=6, 6÷6=1 bad group on the green line
Step 3 — ADD the bad lines together, THEN subtract
✓
(4 + 1) then 84 − 5
Add the two bad lines: 4+1=5. The 2 free points never appear in a bad group, so they don't add anything to BAD — they only ever help make real triangles.
84 − 5 = 79 triangles

↺ Working Backwards — Find the Missing Number

So far you've always been TOLD how many points are on the line, and asked to find the triangle count. Now flip it: you're TOLD the triangle count, and must find how many points were on the line.

9 points are drawn. Some of them, call it k, lie on one straight line. There are 74 possible triangles. How many points (k) are on that line?

Step 1 — Find ALL first (this doesn't depend on k)
1
9 × 8 × 7 ÷ 6
Same as always — ALL only needs the total point count (9), not k.
9×8=72, 72×7=504, 504÷6=84 (this is ALL)
Step 2 — Since GOOD = ALL − BAD, work out BAD by REARRANGING
2
84 − 74
You know ALL (84) and you know GOOD (74, given in the question). If GOOD = ALL − BAD, then BAD = ALL − GOOD — just move the numbers around.
84 − 74 = 10 (this is BAD)
Step 3 — Match BAD to a Choose-3 value using a reference table

BAD is always Choose-3 from k points. So find which k gives Choose-3 = 10:

k (points on the line)34567
Choose 3 from k14102035
✓
BAD = 10  matches  k = 5
10 only appears under k=5 in the table. So the straight line must have had 5 points on it.
k = 5 points were on the line

💡 The 3-step reverse recipe: (1) find ALL from the total points, (2) find BAD by doing ALL − GOOD, (3) look up which k gives that Choose-3 value using the small reference table (3→1, 4→4, 5→10, 6→20, 7→35, 8→56).

⭐ Your Turn

Practice — 4 Questions

🚦 Check: is this a forward question (find the triangles) or a backward question (find k)? Use the right recipe.

P1.  7 points are drawn. 3 of them lie on one straight line. How many triangles can be made from any 3 of the 7 points?

ALL: choose 3 from 7 → 7×6×5=210, 210÷6=35.
BAD (3 collinear): C(3,3)=1.
GOOD: 35−1=34 triangles.
A) 35 is ALL, B) 1 is BAD — both half-finished.

P2.  10 points are drawn. 4 lie on one straight line, and a different 3 lie on another straight line. How many triangles can be made?

ALL: choose 3 from 10 → 10×9×8=720, 720÷6=120.
BAD: line 1 C(4,3)=4, line 2 C(3,3)=1 → 4+1=5.
GOOD: 120−5=115 triangles.

P3.  8 points are drawn, and NO 3 of them lie on the same straight line. How many triangles can be made?

No 3 points are collinear, so there is NOTHING to subtract — BAD=0.
ALL: 8×7×6=336, 336÷6=56. GOOD=ALL=56.
C) 50 wrongly subtracts something anyway — don't invent a bad case that isn't there.

P4.  8 points are drawn. Some of them, k, lie on one straight line. There are 52 possible triangles. How many points (k) are on that line?

ALL: choose 3 from 8 → 8×7×6=336, 336÷6=56.
BAD = ALL−GOOD = 56−52=4.
Table: Choose 3 from k=4 gives 4. k=4.

📖 Brick 3's Big Idea — and All of Total Minus Bad

Points on a straight line are just another "bad case" — Choose 3 from that line, same as Choose 3 from boys, or Choose 3 from one colour. To work BACKWARDS: find ALL, do ALL−GOOD to get BAD, then match BAD to a k using the reference table.

GOOD = ALL − BAD   |   BAD = ALL − GOOD (working backwards)

🚦 Finish-Line Check — a 2-part question may ask you to find k FIRST (backwards), then use that k to find a NEW triangle count (forwards). Do them in order, one at a time.

🏆 You've completed all 3 bricks when…

  • You know a group smaller than 3 gives a bad case of 0, not a skipped term
  • You can apply Total Minus Bad to a brand-new picture (points/triangles), not just people or colours
  • You can work backwards: given GOOD, find BAD, then find the missing group size
