🎯 You Already Know This — Let's Get It Precise

Why this lesson?

On the last test, your WORKING for the repeats questions was actually correct almost every time — the n! ÷ (r! × r! × ...) formula was applied properly. The problem wasn't the maths. It was two small precision slips: once, you counted how many DIFFERENT letters a word has instead of how many ARRANGEMENTS it has. And a few times, the right answer ended up written under the wrong question number.

This lesson fixes BOTH: the "which number am I counting?" trap, and the "check before you write" habit.

Take the word "DEEP." There are TWO totally different questions you could ask about it — and they have TWO totally different answers.

Quick Refresh — One Repeated Letter

How many arrangements of the letters of "NOON" are there?

Step 1 — count total letters: N, O, O, N → n = 4
Step 2 — find repeats: N appears 2 times. O appears 2 times.
Step 3 — start with n!: 4! = 24
Step 4 — divide by r! for EACH repeated letter: 24 ÷ 2! ÷ 2! = 24 ÷ 2 ÷ 2 = 6
6 arrangements — NOT "2 different letters" (that's N and O — the wrong question!)

Quick Refresh — A Letter Repeated 3 Times

How many arrangements of the letters of "SASSY" are there?

Step 1 — count total letters: S, A, S, S, Y → n = 5
Step 2 — find repeats: S appears 3 times. (A and Y each appear once — they don't matter.)
Step 3 — start with n!: 5! = 120
Step 4 — divide by r! for the repeated letter: 120 ÷ 3! = 120 ÷ 6 = 20
20 arrangements
Formula, every time: n! ÷ (r₁! × r₂! × ...) — one r! for EACH letter that repeats, no matter how many times it repeats.

🗺️ The 7-Step Checklist

Use this EXACT order on every repeats question, including in the test:

1Read the WORD for THIS question number. Say the question number out loud in your head.
2Count the total letters → that's n.
3Find every letter that repeats, and how many times each one repeats.
4Compute n! ÷ (r₁! × r₂! × ...).
5Look at the question number again — the SAME one from Step 1.
6Write your answer on THAT question's line — not the next one, not the one you just finished.
7Glance back: does the word next to your answer match the word you actually solved? If not, fix it before moving on.

⚠️ Step 1 and Step 5 are the same question number ON PURPOSE — that's the check that stops answers from sliding into the wrong box.

📖 Two Fixes — in one line

Always answer "how many arrangements," never "how many different letters." And always double-check the question number BEFORE you write your answer.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You never confuse "how many different letters" with "how many arrangements"
  • You say the question number before AND after solving each question
  • Your written answer always matches the word right next to it

You already have the formula down cold — this is just about being sure every single time. Ready for the retest!
