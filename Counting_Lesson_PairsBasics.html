🤝 The Only Other Move

Why learn this?

"How many handshakes at a party?" "How many ways to pick 2 friends?" These are everywhere in puzzles and contests. They feel like multiplying — but there's a tiny twist.

In Lesson 1 you learned: build / arrange → multiply. Today is the only other move:

When you just CHOOSE A PAIR →
multiply, then ÷ 2

See It First — 4 People Shake Hands

Four friends — A, B, C, D — each shake hands once with each other. Let's list every handshake:

A–BA–CA–D B–CB–DC–D

That's 6 handshakes.

Now why isn't it just 4 × 3 = 12? Because A shaking B is the SAME handshake as B shaking A. If we multiply, we count every handshake twice. So we divide by 2:

4 × 3 = 12 ← this counts each handshake twice
12 ÷ 2 = 6 ← A–B and B–A are the same, so halve
= 6 handshakes
🔑 The idea

Choosing a PAIR (where swapping the two changes nothing) → multiply, then ÷ 2.

Same Move, More Examples

Handshakes, picking 2 friends, games where each pair plays once — all "choose a pair", all ÷ 2.

5 people shake hands: 5 × 4 = 20, ÷ 2 = 10
choose 2 friends from 6: 6 × 5 = 30, ÷ 2 = 15

🔍 The Whole Topic — Just One Question

Before you count, ask yourself one thing:

✖️ Building / arranging?
(order matters)

1st & 2nd, a row, outfits, codes → MULTIPLY (Lesson 1).

🤝 Just a pair / a group?
(order doesn't matter)

handshakes, pick 2 friends → multiply, ÷ 2.

💡 Quick test: "if I swap the two, is it the same thing?"  Same → halve (pair).  Different (1st vs 2nd) → don't halve, just multiply.

Your Turn

All pairs today: multiply, then ÷ 2.

Q1.   6 people at a party each shake hands once with everyone else. How many handshakes?
6 × 5 = 30, ÷ 2 = 15
Q2.   From 5 friends, how many ways can you choose a pair of 2 to share a desk?
5 × 4 = 20, ÷ 2 = 10
Q3.   10 people each shake hands once with every other person. How many handshakes?
10 × 9 = 90, ÷ 2 = 45
Q4.   4 teams each play every other team once. How many games are played?
each game = a pair of teams: 4 × 3 = 12, ÷ 2 = 6
📖 The Whole of Counting — Two Moves

BUILD / ARRANGE

order matters → MULTIPLY

CHOOSE A PAIR

order doesn't → multiply, ÷ 2

Ask: does the order matter? That one question tells you which move.

🏆 You've got it when...

  • You ask "does the order matter?" before counting
  • Handshakes / pick-2 → you multiply then ÷ 2
  • 1st & 2nd / a row → you just multiply (no halving)
