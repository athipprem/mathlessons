❓ "Do I take away the fraction, or work out a piece first?"

Before you read anything below — ask yourself:

"Does the story TELL me how much was taken — a number of L, kg or m? Or does it say 'of the …'?"

Say your answer out loud. Then scroll down and check.

🧱 Go And Get 12 Lego Bricks

12 bricks, all the same size.

  1. Each brick is 1/4 kg of clay, so all 12 bricks = 3 kg of clay (4 bricks make 1 kg, and 12 ÷ 4 = 3).
  2. "Use 1/3 OF the clay": make 3 equal piles and take one → 4 bricks = 1 kg taken, 2 kg left over.
  3. "Use 1/3 kg": that is not even 2 bricks. Same "1/3", very different taken pieces.

No bricks handy? Look at the picture:

The Bar For Both, Side By Side

1/3 OF the clay
taken piece = 1 kg · left-over piece = 2 kg
1/3 kg
taken piece = 1/3 kg (told) · left-over piece = 2 2/3 kg
"1/3 OF it" is a piece of the clay you work out. "1/3 kg" is a taken piece the story already told you.

Now You Try

1.  A tub has 1 3/5 kg of butter. 1/4 of the butter is used. How much is left over?
2.  A tub has 1 3/5 kg of butter. 1/4 kg is used. How much is left over?

You've got it if you can say which word made the taken pieces different.
