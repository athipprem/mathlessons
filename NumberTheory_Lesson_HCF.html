📘 Number Theory

🔢 What is HCF?

HCF stands for Highest Common Factor — also called GCD (Greatest Common Divisor).

1
Factors of a number are all the numbers that divide it exactly with no remainder. Example: factors of 12 = {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12}
2
Common factors = factors that appear in BOTH lists.
3
Highest common factor = the biggest one.
Example — HCF(12, 8)

Factors of 12: 1243612

Factors of 8: 1248

Common factors: 124 → HCF = 4

💡 When is HCF useful? When you need to split things into equal groups with NO leftovers — like tiles, bags, or teams.

📋 Method 1: List All Factors

Good for smaller numbers (under 50). Write out all factors of each number, find the common ones.

Find HCF(18, 24)
1
Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18
2
Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
3
Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6 → HCF = 6
💡 Trick: Start from the largest factor of the smaller number and check if it divides the bigger number. The first one that does is your HCF.
Fast check for HCF(18,24)

Largest factor of 18 (excluding itself) = 9. Does 9 divide 24? 24÷9 = 2 remainder 6. ✗
Next: 6. Does 6 divide 24? 24÷6 = 4 exactly. ✓ → HCF = 6

Quick Check

Find HCF(12, 18).

Factors of 12: 1,2,3,4,6,12. Factors of 18: 1,2,3,6,9,18. Common: 1,2,3,6. HCF = 6

🌳 Method 2: Prime Factorisation

Better for larger numbers. Use factor trees, then multiply the common prime factors (taking the lower power).

HCF = product of common prime factors, each to the lower power
Find HCF(24, 36)
1
24 = 2³ × 3
36 = 2² × 3²
2
Common primes: 2 and 3
Lower powers: 2² and 3¹
3
HCF = 2² × 3 = 4 × 3 = 12
💡 Remember: For HCF → take the lower power. For LCM → take the higher power. Opposite rules!

🧩 HCF in Word Problems

The key phrase to spot: "split into equal groups / tiles / pieces with nothing left over" → that's HCF.

Worked Example

A school has 48 students and 36 chairs to arrange into equal groups — same number of students and chairs per group, with none left over. What is the maximum number of groups?

1
Recognise: "equal groups, nothing leftover" → HCF
2
48 = 2⁴ × 3    36 = 2² × 3²
HCF = 2² × 3 = 12
3
Maximum groups = 12 (4 students + 3 chairs per group)
💡 Always check: Total ÷ HCF must be a whole number for both quantities.

⚖️ HCF vs LCM — Know the Difference

HCF — Highest Common Factor

Keywords: split into equal groups, largest tile, share equally, no leftovers

Take the lower power of common primes

Answer is ≤ both numbers

LCM — Lowest Common Multiple

Keywords: meet again, next time together, repeating event

Take the higher power of all primes

Answer is ≥ both numbers

Quick Checks

HCF(20, 30) = ?

20 = 2²×5, 30 = 2×3×5. Common: 2¹×5¹ = 10. ✓

LCM(6, 10) = ?

6 = 2×3, 10 = 2×5. LCM = 2×3×5 = 30. ✓

💪 Practice — HCF Applied

For each question: identify the type (HCF or LCM), then calculate.

Q1

A baker has 40 blueberries and 60 strawberries. He makes identical fruit cups using all the berries, with no leftovers. What is the maximum number of cups?

HCF(40, 60): 40=2³×5, 60=2²×3×5. HCF=2²×5=20. (2 blueberries + 3 strawberries per cup) ✓
Q2

A traffic light flashes every 8 seconds. Another flashes every 12 seconds. They both flashed together just now. In how many seconds will they flash together again?

LCM(8, 12): 8=2³, 12=2²×3. LCM=2³×3=24 seconds. ✓
