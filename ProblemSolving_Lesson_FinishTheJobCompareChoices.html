🎯 Why This Matters

What actually happened last time

On the last test, one question asked you to find a shop's total bill AND say whether it still made a profit. The working showed the FIRST part done perfectly — the sale price of one item, calculated exactly right. But the question also asked for the total for several items, and whether there was still a profit. Neither of those got answered. The maths that was shown was correct; the job just wasn't finished.

Why this matters in real life

If a shop worker calculates a sale price perfectly but forgets to multiply by how many the customer is buying, the till is wrong and the shop loses money. If you compare prices but only check ONE option instead of a few, you might pay more than you needed to. Both of today's skills are about not stopping too early.

Getting the method right is only half the job. Finishing it — and checking you answered everything asked — is the other half.

🏁 The Finish Line Check

Before you write your final answer, stop and do this check:

STEP 1Re-read the question ONE more time, after you've finished calculating.
STEP 2Count how many things it actually asked for. (A total? A yes/no? A "how much more"? Sometimes it's TWO things in one question.)
STEP 3Check each one off. Did you multiply by the quantity if it asked for more than one? Did you answer the yes/no or comparison part, not just the number part?
STEP 4Only then write your final answer — for ALL parts asked.

This is the same "read the exact ask" habit from way back (M3, U35) — just applied to questions with MORE THAN ONE part to answer, and to answers that need one extra step (like multiplying by a quantity) after the main calculation.

1Does the question ask for a total for MORE THAN ONE item? → multiply your per-item answer by the quantity.
2Does the question ask "does X still happen" (like "still make a profit")? → that needs its own separate answer, not just a number.
3Does the question ask "which is cheaper AND by how much"? → that's two answers: the name/choice, AND the number.

Worked Example — Using The Finish Line Check

A toy shop buys action figures for ฿90 each. It marks each one up by 50% to set the selling price, then offers a 20% discount off that marked price during a sale. If a customer buys 4 action figures during the sale, how much do they pay in total, and does the shop still make a profit per figure?

First: what is this question actually asking for? TWO things.
ASK 1The TOTAL cost for 4 action figures
ASK 2Whether the shop still makes a profit per figure
Step 1 — marked price: 90 × 1.5 = 135
Step 2 — sale price (one figure): 135 × 0.8 = 108
Step 3 — ASK 1: multiply by 4 (don't stop at one figure!): 108 × 4 = 432
Step 4 — ASK 2: compare sale price to cost price: 108 − 90 = 18 → YES, still a profit
฿432 total; yes, ฿18 profit per figure

⚠️ Notice: BOTH asks got answered. If you'd stopped after Step 2, you'd have the right method but a wrong, incomplete answer — exactly what happened last time.

🔍 Compare Before You Choose

Some table questions ask for the CHEAPEST way to buy something using a combination of pack sizes. The trap: picking the very FIRST combination that works, without checking if a different combination is even cheaper.

Try at least 2–3 different combinations. Write down each total. THEN pick the smallest.
1Work out the price-per-item for each pack size (price ÷ quantity). The BEST rate is usually your biggest pack.
2Try the combination using mostly the best-rate pack first.
3Try at least one more combination (e.g. using a smaller pack size) as a comparison.
4Compare your totals. The smallest one is your answer — write down ALL the combinations you tried, not just the winner.

Worked Example — Comparing Combinations

Balloon packs: 6-pack ฿36, 12-pack ฿66, 24-pack ฿120. A party planner needs exactly 48 balloons, spending as little as possible. What is the LOWEST total cost?

PackPricePrice per balloon
6-pack฿36฿6.00
12-pack฿66฿5.50
24-pack฿120฿5.00 ← best rate
Combination 1 — mostly 24-packs: 2 × 24-pack = 48 balloons → 2 × 120 = 240
Combination 2 — compare with 12-packs: 4 × 12-pack = 48 balloons → 4 × 66 = 264
Combination 3 — compare with 6-packs: 8 × 6-pack = 48 balloons → 8 × 36 = 288
Compare all three: 240 < 264 < 288
Cheapest: ฿240 (using two 24-packs)

⚠️ The best-rate pack (24-pack) divided EVENLY into 48, so it won. If it hadn't divided evenly, you might need to MIX pack sizes — always check.

🗺️ The Full Checklist

1Build your Clue Board first — every given fact on its own line, and write down the GOAL(s). Count how many things are being asked.
2Work through your steps, writing each one down — especially on the harder, multi-part questions where it matters most.
3If it asks for a total for more than one item: multiply by the quantity. Don't stop at "one item's price."
4If it asks "does X still happen" or "which is cheaper": answer that part directly, in words if needed, not just a number.
5If it's a "cheapest combination" question: try at least 2–3 combinations and compare, don't stop at the first one that works.
6Finish Line Check: re-read the question one last time before writing your final answer. Did you answer EVERYTHING it asked?

⚠️ A perfectly correct calculation that only answers HALF the question is still an incomplete answer. Always do the Finish Line Check.
