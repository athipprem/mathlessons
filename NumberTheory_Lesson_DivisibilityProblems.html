🧩 Now Use the Rules Backwards

You know the rules. Tests rarely just ask "is it divisible?" — they hide a missing digit, or ask for the largest/smallest number that fits, or test two divisors at once. Same rules, used as a tool.

🔍 What is the question really asking?

❓ MISSING DIGIT — "□ makes it divisible by ___"

Use the rule as an equation. For 3 or 9: the digit sum (with the blank) must hit a multiple. For 2/5/10: only the last digit matters.

🔼 LARGEST / SMALLEST — "biggest number divisible by ___"

Start near the limit and step by the divisor until a number passes the rule.

🔗 TWO DIVISORS AT ONCE — "divisible by both ___ and ___"

It must pass BOTH rules (e.g. by 4 and 6 → by 12).

Missing Digit — Digit-Sum Rules (3, 9)

Add the digits you know, then ask: what does the blank need to be to make the total a multiple of 3 (or 9)?

Example: what digit makes 3 □ 9 divisible by 3?

3
?
9
known digits: 3 + 9 = 12 (already ÷ 3)
blank must keep the total ÷ 3 → blank ∈ {0, 3, 6, 9}← smallest is 0

💡 Asked for the smallest? Take the lowest digit that works. Largest? Take the highest. For 3 there are usually several answers; for 9 there is often just one.

Missing Digit — End Rules (2, 5, 10, 4)

For these, only the end matters — ignore the front of the number.

Example: 4,1□ is divisible by 4. What can the blank be?

last two digits = 1□ → 10,11,12…19
which are ÷ 4? 12 and 16 → blank = 2 or 6

Example: 2,8□ is divisible by 5. What can the blank be?

÷5 → ends in 0 or 5 → blank = 0 or 5

Largest / Smallest & Two Divisors

Example: largest 3-digit number divisible by 9.

biggest 3-digit is 999. 9+9+9 = 27 → ÷9 ✓← 999 itself works

Example: largest 3-digit number divisible by both 4 and 6.

by 4 AND 6 → by 12
999 ÷ 12 = 83 remainder 3 → 12 × 83 = 996← step back to the multiple

💡 "Divisible by both A and B" usually means divisible by A×B when A and B share no factors (like 4 and 6 → 12, or 3 and 5 → 15). Then just find multiples of that.

Practice

Use the rule as a tool. Digit sum for 3/9, the end for 2/5/10/4, both rules for "by both".

Q1.   What is the smallest digit for the blank so that 2□7 is divisible by 3?
2+□+7 = 9+□. Already ÷3 → smallest blank = 0
Q2.   What digit makes 61□ divisible by 9?
6+1+□ = 7+□ → needs 9 → blank = 2
Q3.   What is the largest number below 432 that is divisible by 5?
step down from 432: 430 ends in 0 → ÷5 ✓ (435 is above 432)
Q4.   Is 7,002 divisible by 6?
÷2? ends in 2 ✓. ÷3? 7+0+0+2=9 ✓. Both → Yes
📖 Problem Types, One Card
Missing digit (3/9):add known digits, choose blank to reach a multiple. Missing digit (2/5/10/4):only the end matters. Largest / smallest:start near the limit, step by the divisor. By both A and B:pass both rules → use A×B (e.g. 4&6 → 12). Smallest vs largest:read which one they asked for.

🏆 You are ready when...

  • You turn a missing-digit question straight into a digit-sum check
  • You combine 4 & 6 into 12 without hesitating
  • You re-read for "smallest" vs "largest" before answering
