🎯 Why This Lesson — Your Last Test Told Us Something

Here's what your last test showed. When the story said "is blue" or "cooks", you multiplied — and got them right. When it said "drinks", "cuts off" or "pours out", you took the fraction away instead. And you wrote "X or +" — you knew you were choosing. This lesson is about one choice, and how to make it every time.

Drinks, uses, cuts off, pours out, eats, gives away — these words do NOT mean "take the fraction away". They tell you which part we are finding.
You can already multiply. This is about deciding BEFORE you calculate.

📏 Route A — The Bar, On Your Own Question

Kai drinks 1/3 of 9/10 L of milk. That was Question 1 on your last test.

The bar is the milk: 9/10 L. Cut it into 3 equal parts. Shade 1 part.

9/10 L cut into 3 equal parts: each part is 9/10 ÷ 3 = 3/10 L (9 tenths shared into 3 gives 3 tenths each)
He drinks 1 part = 3/10 L

Your test line was:

9/10 − 1/3 = 17/30

That takes away one-third of a LITRE, not one-third of the MILK.

"1/3 OF the milk" is a piece of the bar. We have to find how big that piece is.

👆 Route B — Two Taps, Then The Numbers

Same thing, as two questions you answer before any numbers.
Worked example.  A bag has 1 4/5 kg of flour. Jo uses 1/3 OF the flour. How much does she use?
① Is the fraction OF something, or an AMOUNT?
② Does the question want the PART, or what is LEFT?
1 4/5 = 9/5 (1 whole is 5/5, plus 4/5)
The fraction OF something = fraction × the whole: 1/3 × 9/5
cancel the 3 on the bottom with the 9 on top: 9 ÷ 3 = 3 (so 1/3 × 9/5 = 1/1 × 3/5)
= 3/5 kg

➖ LEFT = Whole Take Away The Part

Example 1.  Same bag: 1 4/5 kg of flour. Jo uses 1/3 OF the flour. How much flour is LEFT?
① Is the fraction OF something, or an AMOUNT?
② Does the question want the PART, or what is LEFT?
Part first: 3/5 kg (from the last screen)
1 4/5 − 3/5 = 9/5 − 3/5 = 6/5 (whole take away the part, same fifths)
= 1 1/5 kg
Or: if 1/3 is used, 2/3 is left → 2/3 × 9/5 = 18/15 = 1 1/5 kg.
Example 2 (the contrast).  Now a different bag: 1 4/5 kg of flour. Jo uses 1/3 kg. How much flour is LEFT?
① Is the fraction OF something, or an AMOUNT?
② Does the question want the PART, or what is LEFT?
"1/3 kg" has a unit, so it is already an amount. Nothing to find first.
1 4/5 − 1/3 = 9/5 − 1/3 = 27/15 − 5/15 (fifths and thirds both become fifteenths)
= 22/15 = 1 7/15 kg
OF something → find the part first. LEFT → whole take away the part. A fraction with a unit → it's already an amount.

Practice — Tap First, Then Work

P1.  Max has 1 1/5 L of juice. He drinks 1/2 of the juice. How much does he drink?
① Is the fraction OF something, or an AMOUNT?
② Does the question want the PART, or what is LEFT?
Tap both boxes above to open the answer box.
① OF the juice   ② the PART
1 1/5 = 6/5 (1 whole is 5/5, plus 1/5)
1/2 × 6/5 = 6/10 (top times top, bottom times bottom)
6/10 = 3/5 (both ÷ 2)
He drinks 3/5 L
P2.  Max has 1 1/5 L of juice. He drinks 1/2 L. How much is left?
① Is the fraction OF something, or an AMOUNT?
② Does the question want the PART, or what is LEFT?
Tap both boxes above to open the answer box.
① an AMOUNT (1/2 L)   ② LEFT
1 1/5 = 6/5 = 12/10 (same size pieces: tenths)
1/2 = 5/10
12/10 − 5/10 = 7/10
7/10 L is left
P3.  A rope is 3 1/3 m long. 2/5 of the rope is cut off. How much rope is left?
① Is the fraction OF something, or an AMOUNT?
② Does the question want the PART, or what is LEFT?
Tap both boxes above to open the answer box.
① OF the rope   ② LEFT: part first, then take it away
3 1/3 = 10/3 (3 wholes = 9/3, plus 1/3)
Part: 2/5 × 10/3 = 20/15 = 1 1/3 m (cut off)
Left: 3 1/3 − 1 1/3 = 2
2 m is left
P4.  A pizza weighs 1 1/2 kg. 2/3 of the pizza is eaten. How much pizza is left?
① Is the fraction OF something, or an AMOUNT?
② Does the question want the PART, or what is LEFT?
Tap both boxes above to open the answer box.
① OF the pizza   ② LEFT
1 1/2 = 3/2
Part: 2/3 × 3/2 = 6/6 = 1 kg (eaten)
Left: 1 1/2 − 1 = 1/2
1/2 kg is left
📖 Today's Big Idea

Decide first. A fraction OF something → find the part. LEFT → whole take away the part. A fraction with a unit → already an amount.

Finish-the-question check: did you answer the EXACT question asked, is your fraction in simplest form, and did you write the UNIT?
