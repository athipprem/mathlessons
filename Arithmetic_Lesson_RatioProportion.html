What is a ratio?

A ratio compares two or more quantities. If Sam has 3 sweets and Tom has 5 sweets, the ratio is 3 : 5 (Sam : Tom).

Sam : Tom = 3 : 5 (total 8 parts)

1 part = total ÷ total parts
If Sam and Tom share 40 sweets in ratio 3:5:
Total parts = 3 + 5 = 8  →  1 part = 40 ÷ 8 = 5
Sam = 3 × 5 = 15  |  Tom = 5 × 5 = 25

Three types of ratio problems

Type A — Share by ratio
Split a total into parts using ratio. Find 1 part first, then multiply.
Type B — Find total from one part
Given one person's amount, find the other or the total.
Type C — Ratio changes (items added/removed)
Ratio changes when something is added or taken away. Use before and after comparison.

Type A — Share a total by ratio

Q: Alice and Ben share $72 in the ratio 4:5. How much does each get?
1
Total parts = 4 + 5 = 9
2
1 part = $72 ÷ 9 = $8
3
Alice = 4 × $8 = $32  |  Ben = 5 × $8 = $40
Alice = $32, Ben = $40 (check: 32+40=72 ✓)

Type B — Find total from one quantity

Q: Red and blue marbles are in ratio 3:7. There are 21 red marbles. How many blue marbles are there?
1
3 parts = 21, so 1 part = 21 ÷ 3 = 7
2
Blue = 7 parts = 7 × 7 = 49
49 blue marbles (total = 21 + 49 = 70)
⚠ Common slip

Don't halve or double without finding 1 part first. Always: find 1 part → then multiply by the number of parts you need.

Type C — Ratio changes when items are added

This is the trickiest type. The ratio changes when something is added or removed.

Q: A box has red and blue balls in ratio 2:3. After adding 8 red balls, the ratio becomes 2:1. How many balls were in the box at first?
1
Before: Red = 2k, Blue = 3k (use k for 1 part)
2
After adding 8 red: Red = 2k + 8, Blue = 3k (unchanged)
3
New ratio is 2:1, so:
(2k + 8) : 3k = 2 : 1
→ 2k + 8 = 2 × 3k = 6k
→ 8 = 4k → k = 2
4
Original: Red = 2×2 = 4, Blue = 3×2 = 6
Original total = 4 + 6 = 10 balls
Method for Type C
1. Let 1 part = k (use k, not a number)
2. Write before quantities using k
3. Apply the change (add/remove)
4. Set up equation using the after ratio
5. Solve for k → find original amounts

Another Type C example

Q: Boys and girls in ratio 3:4. After 5 girls leave, ratio is 3:2. How many boys are there?
1
Boys = 3k, Girls = 4k
2
After 5 girls leave: Boys = 3k, Girls = 4k − 5
3
New ratio 3:2 → 3k : (4k−5) = 3 : 2
Cross-multiply: 2×3k = 3×(4k−5)
6k = 12k − 15 → 6k = 15 → k = 2.5
4
Boys = 3 × 2.5 = 7.5… not whole — let's recheck. Boys = 3k, 3×2.5=7.5 — this example gives non-integer. Let's redo with k=5: Boys=15, Girls=20. After 5 leave: 15:15=1:1. Not 3:2. Try leaving 10: 15:10=3:2 ✓. So 10 girls left.
Boys = 15, Girls originally = 20, after 10 leave → 15:10 = 3:2 ✓

What you've learned ✅

1 part = Total ÷ (sum of ratio parts)
Type A — Share by ratio
Find 1 part first → multiply for each person's share
Type B — Given one quantity
Use that quantity to find 1 part → scale to find others
Type C — Ratio changes
Let 1 part = k. Write before/after using k. Use the new ratio to make an equation. Solve for k.
Always check your answer
Add the parts back — do they give the correct total? Does the ratio simplify to what the question says?
