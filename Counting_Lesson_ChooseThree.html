🎯 Why Learn This?

You already know how to choose 2 — now go one bigger

Any 3 dots on a page make a triangle. Picking 3 toppings for a pizza, choosing 3 students for a team, taking 3 books on a trip — these all ask the same question: "how many different GROUPS of 3 can I make, when the order I pick them in doesn't matter?" You already solved this exact puzzle for groups of 2 (pairs, handshakes). Today you stretch that same idea one step further.

Choosing 3 is just like choosing 2 — one extra person, one extra "un-ordering" step.

🤝 Recap — Choosing 2 (You've Got This)

You already mastered this: to choose 2 people from a group of n, you multiply n × (n−1) to count every ORDERED pair, then divide by 2 because every pair got counted twice (Alex-then-Bo AND Bo-then-Alex are the SAME pair).

Example: choose 2 people from 4 (Alex, Bo, Cara, Dan)
Ordered pairs: 4 × 3 = 12
Each pair counted twice → ÷ 2
12 ÷ 2 = 6 different pairs
Choose 2 from n  =  n × (n−1) ÷ 2

➕ The One New Idea — One More Person, One More "Un-Order" Step

What changes when you choose 3 instead of 2

Choosing 3 people has the exact same "counted more than once" problem — just bigger. If you pick 3 people, there are 3! = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6 different orders you could have picked them in (you already know this from arrangements!), and all 6 orders are really the SAME group of 3. So: multiply out the ordered count, then divide by 6.

Choose 3 from n  =  n × (n−1) × (n−2)  ÷  3! (which is 6)

⚠️ Notice the pattern: choosing 2 people → divide by 2! (=2). Choosing 3 people → divide by 3! (=6). However many you choose, multiply that many numbers counting down from n, then divide by that many factorial.

🧭 The ONE Rule

1How many are you choosing? Call it r (here, r = 3).
2Multiply r numbers counting DOWN from n: n × (n−1) × (n−2) …
3Divide by r! (for r=3, that's 3! = 6).
4That's your answer — the number of different GROUPS, order doesn't matter.
Choose r from n  =  (n × (n−1) × … for r numbers) ÷ r!

Worked Example — Choose 3 From 8 Points On A Circle

8 points are marked on a circle (no 3 in a straight line). Any 3 of them can be joined to make a triangle. How many different triangles are possible?

Step 1 — how many are we choosing?
r = 3
Step 2 — multiply 3 numbers counting down from 8:
8 × 7 × 6 = 336
Step 3 — divide by 3! = 6:
336 ÷ 6 = 56 different triangles

⚠️ "Choose 3 points" and "make a triangle" mean the same thing here — any 3 points (not in a line) make exactly one triangle, and the order you pick the 3 points in doesn't change the triangle.

P1.  A DJ has 11 songs and wants to choose 3 to play first. How many different groups of 3 songs are possible?
11 × 10 × 9 = 990 ordered ways.
990 ÷ 6 = 165 different groups

13 × 12 × 11 = 1716 ordered ways.
1716 ÷ 6 = 286 different triangles
📖 Today's Big Idea

Choosing 3 works exactly like choosing 2 — multiply that many numbers counting down from n, then divide by that many factorial (3! = 6). Same idea, one bigger step.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You can multiply the right number of counting-down terms for how many you're choosing
  • You remember to divide by r! (3! = 6 for choosing 3), not just 2
  • You recognise "any 3 points make a triangle" as a choose-3 question
  • You can explain WHY you divide — because every group got counted in every possible order
