🎯 Why Learn This?

You already know how to choose 2 — today you go one bigger

Picking 3 toppings for a pizza. Choosing 3 friends for a team. Taking 3 books on a trip. Joining any 3 dots to make a triangle. All of these ask the same question:

“How many different GROUPS of 3 can I make, when the order I pick them in doesn’t matter?”

Your superpower today:
count thousands of teams without writing a single list.

💡 There is also a second, sneaky half to today’s lesson: learning when you must NOT divide. That one trips up more people than the counting itself.

🗺️ Your Counting Pocket Map

Every “how many ways?” question starts with ONE question you ask yourself:

Does the ORDER matter?
1ORDER MATTERS (1st, 2nd, 3rd · gold, silver, bronze · a row of seats)
→ Move LINE-UP: just multiply. Never divide.
2ORDER DOESN’T MATTER, picking 2 (pairs, handshakes, one line between 2 dots)
→ Move PAIR: multiply 2 numbers, then ÷ 2.
3ORDER DOESN’T MATTER, picking 3 (teams, pizzas, triangles)
→ Move TEAM: multiply 3 numbers, then ÷ 6. TODAY

⚠️ Keep this map in your head. Almost every counting mistake is choosing the wrong move — not doing the arithmetic wrong.

🤝 Recap — Move PAIR (You’ve Got This)

Question: Alex, Bo, Cara and Dan are in a club. Two of them will be chosen to be partners. How many different pairs are possible?

Step 1 — count the ORDERED ways (pretend order matters for a moment):
1st person: 4 choices
2nd person: 3 choices left
4 × 3 = 12 ordered ways
Step 2 — but Alex-then-Bo and Bo-then-Alex are the SAME pair. Every pair was counted 2 times:
12 ÷ 2
= 6 different pairs
Move PAIR  =  multiply 2 numbers counting down  ÷  2

⚠️ The ÷ 2 is there for ONE reason only: each pair got counted twice. Remember that reason — you are about to need it again with a different number.

➕ The One New Idea

Now the club must choose 3 people instead of 2. Everything works the same way — only the divide number changes. Here is why.

Ask: if I pick 3 people, how many different ORDERS could I have picked them in?
1st slot: 3 choices
2nd slot: 2 choices left
3rd slot: 1 choice left
3 × 2 × 1 = 6 orders
So every team of 3 gets counted 6 times → divide by 6
Choosing 2 → each group counted 2 times → ÷ 2
Choosing 3 → each group counted 6 times → ÷ 6

⚠️ You have met that 6 before: it is 3! = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6, the factorial you mastered in Unit 33. Nothing new — just the number of ways to shuffle 3 things.

🛑 The Sneaky Half — When You Must NOT Divide

You now know two dividing numbers (÷2 and ÷6). That means the real skill is knowing which one to use — or whether to divide at all.

👥 GROUP — order doesn’t matter

Everyone chosen gets the same thing.

“Choose 3 for the team.”
“Pick 3 toppings.”
“Join 3 dots into a triangle.”

→ multiply, then ÷ 6

🏅 LINE-UP — order matters

Everyone chosen gets a different job or place.

“Gold, silver, bronze.”
“1st, 2nd, 3rd.”
“President, Secretary, Treasurer.”

→ multiply only. NEVER divide.

The test: swap two of the chosen people around.
Did anything change? → LINE-UP (don’t divide)
Nothing changed? → GROUP (divide)

🧭 Move TEAM — The Whole Rule On One Page

1Check the map first: is it a GROUP (order doesn’t matter)? If it is a LINE-UP, stop — just multiply.
2Multiply 3 numbers, counting DOWN from how many you can pick from.
From 8: 8 × 7 × 6. From 12: 12 × 11 × 10.
3Divide by 6 (because each team of 3 got counted 6 times).
4Write the answer as a NUMBER of groups — not a list.
Choose 3 from n  =  n × (n−1) × (n−2)  ÷  6

💡 Handy shortcut for the arithmetic: divide before you multiply everything out when you can. For 9 × 8 × 7 ÷ 6, notice 9 ÷ 3 = 3 and 8 ÷ 2 = 4, so it becomes 3 × 4 × 7 = 84. Same answer, much smaller numbers.

Worked Example — Triangles From 8 Points

8 points are marked on a circle. No 3 of them lie on a straight line. Any 3 points can be joined to make a triangle. How many different triangles are possible?

Different triangles seen: 1
Step 1 — GROUP or LINE-UP? Swap two of the 3 corner points around. Is it a different triangle?
No — same triangle. So it is a GROUP. Use Move TEAM.
Step 2 — multiply 3 numbers counting down from 8:
8 × 7 = 56
56 × 6 = 336
Step 3 — divide by 6, because each triangle was counted 6 times:
336 ÷ 6
= 56 different triangles

⚠️ “Choose 3 points” and “make a triangle” are the same job. Every set of 3 points (as long as they are not in a straight line) makes exactly one triangle.

📋 Build The Count Yourself

A shelf has 9 different books. You will choose 3 of them to take on a trip. How many different groups of 3 books are possible?

Work each step out in your head or on paper first, then tap to check.

GROUP. Swapping two books around gives the same 3 books in the bag — nothing changed. So we will divide.
9 × 8 = 72, then 72 × 7 = 504
504 ÷ 6 = 84
The question asked HOW MANY groups — so the answer is 84 different groups of 3 books.
⭐ Your Turn

Practice — 4 Questions

🚦 Before every answer: check the Pocket Map first (GROUP or LINE-UP?), then check you answered the EXACT question asked.

P1.  A DJ has 11 songs. She will choose 3 of them to play at a party (all 3 just get played, in no special order). How many different groups of 3 songs can she choose?
GROUP or LINE-UP? All 3 songs get the same treatment → GROUP.
11 × 10 = 110
110 × 9 = 990
990 ÷ 6
= 165 different groups of 3 songs
P2.  13 points are marked on a circle, and no 3 of them lie on a straight line. How many different triangles can be made by joining any 3 of the points?
Swap two corners → same triangle → GROUP.
13 × 12 = 156
156 × 11 = 1716
1716 ÷ 6
= 286 different triangles
P3.  A pizza shop offers 7 different toppings. You may put 3 different toppings on your pizza. How many different 3-topping pizzas can be made?
Swap two toppings around on the pizza → it is the same pizza → GROUP.
7 × 6 = 42
42 × 5 = 210
210 ÷ 6
= 35 different pizzas
P4.  🏅 Careful — read this one twice. 7 runners are in a race. Medals are given for 1st place (gold), 2nd place (silver) and 3rd place (bronze). How many different ways can the 3 medals be handed out?
Swap the gold and silver runners around. Is that the same result? NO — a different person is champion. So this is a LINE-UP.
7 × 6 = 42
42 × 5 = 210
= 210 ways — and we do NOT divide by 6
Compare with P3: exactly the same 7 × 6 × 5, but P3 divided and P4 did not. The words decided it, not the numbers.
📖 Today’s Big Idea

Ask “does order matter?” FIRST. If it doesn’t and you are picking 3 — multiply 3 numbers counting down, then divide by 6, because each team got counted 6 times. If order DOES matter, just multiply and never divide.

GROUP of 3 → n × (n−1) × (n−2) ÷ 6   |   LINE-UP of 3 → n × (n−1) × (n−2)

🚦 Finish-Line Check — before you write your answer down: did you answer the EXACT question asked? A count of groups is a NUMBER, not a list. And did you check GROUP vs LINE-UP before dividing?

🏆 You’ve got it when…

  • You ask “does order matter?” before you touch any numbers
  • You can explain WHY you divide by 6 — each team gets counted in all 6 of its orders
  • You spot “any 3 points make a triangle” as a choose-3 question
  • You do NOT divide when the 3 chosen people get different jobs or places
  • You can do 9 × 8 × 7 ÷ 6 without a calculator
