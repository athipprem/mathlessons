🎯 The Same Skill — With One Extra Bit Of Care

Why this top-up?

You already know the "someone joins or leaves" average skill from your last lesson — find the BEFORE total, find the AFTER total, then subtract. On the test, that method worked perfectly on the easier questions. On the harder ones — where you have to hold TWO totals in your head at once — two small things went wrong: sometimes the AFTER total got mixed up while you were working, and sometimes the final subtraction itself had a slip even though both totals were right.

What today fixes

Two small habits, not a new method: (1) WRITE both totals down with clear labels — "BEFORE total" and "AFTER total" — before you subtract anything, so you never lose track of which number is which. (2) CHECK your subtraction by adding the answer back — if it takes you back to the BEFORE total, you know it's right.

Same rule as before: BEFORE total, AFTER total, then subtract. Today just makes it impossible to get lost along the way.

🗺️ Quick Recap — The Rule You Already Know

1BEFORE total = before average × before count.
2AFTER total = after average × after count.
3Subtract the smaller total from the bigger total — that's your answer.
4NEW today: write Steps 1 and 2 as clearly LABELLED lines before you subtract anything. TODAY
5NEW today: after Step 3, add your answer back to the smaller total — it should rebuild the bigger total. TODAY

⚠️ Nothing new to learn here — just two habits that make the exact same method impossible to lose track of.

Fix 1 — Label BEFORE And AFTER Before You Subtract

Write "BEFORE total = ..." and "AFTER total = ..." as their own lines FIRST.

4 conference staff packed an average of 18 gift bags each. A 5th staff member joined the shift, and the new average across all 5 staff was 23 gift bags. How many gift bags did the 5th staff member pack?

Step 1 — write the BEFORE total as its own labelled line:
BEFORE total (4 staff) = 18 × 4 = 72
Step 2 — write the AFTER total as its own labelled line:
AFTER total (5 staff) = 23 × 5 = 115
Step 3 — NOW subtract (someone JOINED, so AFTER − BEFORE):
115 − 72 = 43
The 5th staff member packed 43 gift bags

⚠️ Writing "BEFORE total (4 staff) = 72" and "AFTER total (5 staff) = 115" as two separate labelled lines means you can NEVER accidentally subtract the wrong pair of numbers, even if you get interrupted or your working gets messy.

📋 Label Both Totals Yourself

3 painters finished an average of 16 canvases each this month. A 4th painter joined the studio, and the new average across all 4 painters was 21 canvases. How many canvases had the 4th painter already finished? Tap each button in order.

BEFORE total (3 painters) = 16 × 3 = 48
AFTER total (4 painters) = 21 × 4 = 84
84 − 48 = 36 canvases

Write both totals as their own labelled lines before you subtract.

P1.  4 potters shaped an average of 30 bowls each. A 5th potter joined the workshop, and the new average across all 5 potters was 35 bowls. How many bowls had the 5th potter shaped?
BEFORE total (4 potters) = 30 × 4 = 120
AFTER total (5 potters) = 35 × 5 = 175
175 − 120 = 55 bowls

BEFORE total (5 guides) = 22 × 5 = 110
AFTER total (6 guides) = 27 × 6 = 162
162 − 110 = 52 tourists

Fix 2 — Check Your Subtraction By Adding Back

Answer + smaller total = bigger total? If yes, your subtraction is correct.

6 hikers carried an average of 35 kg in their backpacks. One hiker turned back early, and the average weight carried by the remaining 5 hikers rose to 37 kg. How many kg had the hiker who turned back been carrying?

Step 1 — BEFORE total (6 hikers):
35 × 6 = 210
Step 2 — AFTER total (5 hikers):
37 × 5 = 185
Step 3 — subtract (someone LEFT, so BEFORE − AFTER):
210 − 185 = 25
The hiker who turned back was carrying 25 kg
Step 4 — CHECK IT: add your answer back to the AFTER total:
185 + 25 = 210 — matches the BEFORE total! ✓ Correct.

⚠️ This check catches subtraction slips instantly. If adding back does NOT rebuild the BEFORE total, you know to redo the subtraction before moving on — you don't have to wait until the test is marked to find out.

📋 Check Your Own Subtraction

5 recycling bins held an average of 42 kg of paper each. One bin was emptied and removed from the count, and the average among the remaining 4 bins rose to 45 kg. How many kg had been in the bin that was removed? Tap each button in order.

BEFORE total (5 bins) = 42 × 5 = 210
AFTER total (4 bins) = 45 × 4 = 180
210 − 180 = 30 kg
180 + 30 = 210 — matches the BEFORE total! ✓ Correct.
⭐ Your Turn — Fix 2: Check It

Practice — Checking Your Subtraction

Find both totals, subtract, then check by adding your answer back.

P1.  6 suitcases weighed an average of 48 kg each at the airport check-in. One suitcase was checked in first and set aside, and the average weight of the remaining 5 suitcases rose to 50 kg. How many kg did the suitcase set aside weigh?
BEFORE total (6 suitcases) = 48 × 6 = 288
AFTER total (5 suitcases) = 50 × 5 = 250
288 − 250 = 38 kg
Check: 250 + 38 = 288 ✓
P2.  7 fruit crates held an average of 33 oranges each. One crate was sold, and the average among the remaining 6 crates rose to 35 oranges. How many oranges had been in the crate that was sold?
BEFORE total (7 crates) = 33 × 7 = 231
AFTER total (6 crates) = 35 × 6 = 210
231 − 210 = 21 oranges
Check: 210 + 21 = 231 ✓

Both Fixes Together — A Harder One

Label BOTH totals first. Subtract. Then check by adding back.

7 volunteers collected an average of 53 cans each for a food drive. The volunteer who collected the MOST left early, and the average among the remaining 6 volunteers dropped to 49 cans. How many cans had the volunteer who left collected?

Step 1 — BEFORE total (7 volunteers), labelled:
BEFORE total = 53 × 7 = 371
Step 2 — AFTER total (6 volunteers), labelled:
AFTER total = 49 × 6 = 294
Step 3 — the average DROPPED, which makes sense here: the volunteer who left had the MOST cans (above the average), so removing them pulls the average down. Subtract (BEFORE − AFTER):
371 − 294 = 77
The volunteer who left collected 77 cans
Step 4 — check it:
294 + 77 = 371 — matches the BEFORE total! ✓ Correct. (And 77 is well above the original average of 53 — matches "collected the MOST".)

⚠️ Notice this one's average DROPPED, not rose — because the person who left was ABOVE average, not below. Whichever direction the average moves, the same 4 steps still work: label BEFORE, label AFTER, subtract, check.

Label both totals, subtract, then check your answer by adding back.

AFTER total (5 members) = 31 × 5 = 155

BEFORE total (8 tanks) = 55 × 8 = 440
AFTER total (7 tanks) = 57 × 7 = 399

🗺️ The Full Checklist

1Write "BEFORE total (___ people/things) = ___" as its own labelled line.
2Write "AFTER total (___ people/things) = ___" as its own labelled line.
3Subtract the smaller total from the bigger total.
4Check it: add your answer back to the smaller total — it should rebuild the bigger total.
5If the check doesn't match, redo Step 3 before writing your final answer.

⚠️ This is the exact same method from your last lesson — these two habits just make it impossible to lose track of which total is which, or to hand in an unchecked subtraction.

📖 Today's Big Idea

Label BEFORE and AFTER before you subtract. Check your answer by adding it back. Same method, zero room to get lost.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You write "BEFORE total = ..." and "AFTER total = ..." as two separate labelled lines, every time
  • You subtract the smaller total from the bigger total, whichever direction the average moved
  • You always check your answer by adding it back to the smaller total
  • If the check doesn't rebuild the bigger total, you redo the subtraction before finishing

You already know the method — these two habits just make sure it holds up under harder numbers.
