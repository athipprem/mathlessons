🎯 Why This Matters

Where you actually use this

Every time you compare prices at a shop, check a bus or train timetable, or work out how much something costs after a discount, you're doing exactly this kind of problem. On a competition paper, these are usually the LONGEST questions — lots of words, lots of numbers, several facts to juggle at once.

Why these questions trip people up

It's almost never that the maths itself is hard. It's that there are 2, 3, sometimes 4 separate facts packed into a paragraph, and trying to hold them all in your head at once while also calculating is where mistakes creep in — or where you freeze and skip the question entirely.

The fix isn't harder maths. It's writing every fact down BEFORE you try to combine them.

📋 The Clue Board Method

Before you calculate anything, build a Clue Board: one line per fact, written in your own simple words or symbols.

CLUE 1Write down the first fact, exactly as given.
CLUE 2Write down the second fact on its own new line — don't combine yet.
CLUE 3Keep going until every fact in the question has its own line.
GOALWrite down exactly what the question is asking you to find.

Only once every clue is written down do you start combining them. This is the same idea as the "balance scale" equations from Unit 21 — you already know how to turn a sentence into a written line. The new part is doing it for EVERY fact before touching any of them.

1Read the whole question once, all the way through.
2Is it a TABLE question (prices, times, sizes listed out)? → go to Table Reading.
3Is it a DISCOUNT or PROFIT question (marked price, sale, cost, sold for)? → go to Discount & Profit.
4Otherwise → it's a Multi-Clue question. Build the Clue Board first.
Skill 1 of 3

Multi-Clue Word Problems

Maya is 5 years older than her cousin Alex. Together, their ages add up to 27. How old is Alex?

Clue Board FIRST, then combine
CLUE 1Maya = Alex + 5
CLUE 2Maya + Alex = 27
GOALFind Alex's age
Step 1 — substitute Clue 1 into Clue 2: (Alex + 5) + Alex = 27
Step 2 — combine the Alex terms: 2 × Alex + 5 = 27
Step 3 — subtract 5 from both sides: 2 × Alex = 22
Step 4 — divide both sides by 2: Alex = 11
Alex is 11 years old (Maya is 16)

⚠️ Notice: this is the EXACT same "variables on both sides" method from Unit 21 — nothing new here except writing the clues down first instead of trying to remember them.

Table Reading — Find the Right Row, Compare, Combine

Zoe wants to buy 1 muffin and 1 croissant from this bakery. How much does she SAVE by buying the Bundle instead of buying them separately?

ItemPrice
Muffin฿35
Croissant฿45
Bundle (1 muffin + 1 croissant)฿70
CLUE 1Buying separately: muffin ฿35 + croissant ฿45
CLUE 2Buying the bundle: ฿70
GOALHow much LESS does the bundle cost?
Step 1 — work out the separate total: 35 + 45 = 80
Step 2 — compare to the bundle price: 80 − 70 = 10
Zoe saves ฿10 by buying the bundle

⚠️ Table questions almost always need TWO totals worked out before you can answer — read every row you need first, THEN compute.

Discount & Profit — The Three Formulas

Sale Price = Marked Price − Discount
Discount = Marked Price × Discount%
Profit = Selling Price − Cost Price
Selling Price (with markup) = Cost Price + (Cost Price × Markup%)

A bicycle is marked at ฿2,400. During a sale, it is discounted by 15%. The shop bought the bicycle for ฿1,800. After selling it at the sale price, what is the shop's profit?

CLUE 1Marked price = ฿2,400
CLUE 2Discount = 15% off the marked price
CLUE 3Cost price (what the shop paid) = ฿1,800
GOALProfit = Sale Price − Cost Price
Step 1 — find the discount amount: 2,400 × 15% = 2,400 × 0.15 = 360
Step 2 — find the sale price: 2,400 − 360 = 2,040
Step 3 — find the profit: 2,040 − 1,800 = 240
The shop's profit is ฿240

⚠️ The two most common mix-ups: (1) forgetting the discount is a PERCENT OF the marked price, not a flat number to subtract straight away, and (2) mixing up marked price, sale price, and cost price — they're three DIFFERENT numbers. Label each one on your Clue Board.

🗺️ The Full Checklist

1Read the whole question once, all the way through, before writing anything.
2Sort it: Multi-Clue? Table? Discount/Profit? (It can also mix two of these.)
3Build the Clue Board — every given fact on its own line, in your own words or symbols.
4Write down exactly what the GOAL is — what number is the question actually asking for?
5Table question? Find every row/column you need before calculating anything.
6Discount/Profit question? Label marked price, sale price, cost price, and selling price separately — never assume two of them are the same number.
7Only now start combining the clues, one step at a time.

⚠️ If a question feels like "too much to hold in your head," that's exactly the signal to slow down and build the Clue Board — it's not a sign you can't do the maths.

📖 Today's Big Idea

Build the Clue Board first: every given fact on its own line, and the exact GOAL written down, before you combine anything. Multi-Clue, Table Reading, and Discount & Profit are all the same skill wearing three different outfits.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You write down every fact BEFORE trying to calculate anything, every single time
  • You can tell marked price, sale price, cost price, and selling price apart without mixing them up
  • For a table question, you check every row/column you might need before you start adding or comparing

This is the exact skill that decides the longest, wordiest questions on any competition paper. Slow, careful, written-down beats fast and guessed every time.
