🔍 You Only Need the END

Why learn this?

Contests love asking "what is the LAST digit of 23 × 47?" or even of a huge multiplication. Doing the whole sum is slow — but you don't have to. The last digit of the answer comes only from the last digits of the numbers.

To get the LAST digit of a product,
multiply only the LAST digits.

See It — 14 × 28

The full answer is 392. Its last digit is 2. Now watch — we get that 2 from just the last digits, 4 and 8:

last digits: 4 and 8
4 × 8 = 32
last digit of 32 is 2 ← that's our answer
last digit of 14 × 28 = 2

Another: 23 × 51

3 × 1 = 3 → last digit = 3
🔑 The idea

Ignore everything except the last digits. Multiply them, and take the last digit of THAT.

Multiplying the Same Number Again and Again

What about 7 × 7 × 7 × 7 × 7 × 7 (that's 7 six times)? Keep only the last digit each time, and a repeating pattern appears:

7 → last digit 7
7 × 7 = 49 → 9
… × 7 = (9×7=63) → 3
… × 7 = (3×7=21) → 1
… × 7 = (1×7=7) → 7 ← it starts over!

So the last digits go in a cycle of 4:

7
9
3
1
7
9

So what is the last digit of 7 multiplied 6 times?

count along the pattern: 1st=7, 2nd=9, 3rd=3, 4th=1, 5th=7, 6th=9
last digit = 9

💡 The trick: write the last digits until they repeat, then just count to the position you want.

Your Turn

Use only the last digits. For repeated multiplying, write the pattern and count.

Q1.   What is the last digit of 24 × 36?

Q2.   What is the last digit of 17 × 17?
7 × 7 = 49 → last digit 9
Q3.   What is the last digit of 5 × 5 × 5?
5 stays 5: 5×5=25 (5), ×5=125 (5)
last digit 5
Q4.   What is the last digit of 2 × 2 × 2 × 2?
2 → 4 → 8 → (8×2=16) 6
4th one = 6
📖 The Last Digit Trick

Last digit of a product = multiply only the last digits, take the last digit of that.

Multiplying the same number again and again? Write the last digits until they repeat, then count to the spot you need.

🏆 You've got it when...

  • You find the last digit without doing the whole multiplication
  • For repeated multiplying, you spot the repeating pattern and count
