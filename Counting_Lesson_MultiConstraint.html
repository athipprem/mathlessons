Brick 1 of 3

🎯 Why Learn This?

Real counting questions almost always have a catch

“Choose a team of 3 with at least 1 girl.” That extra rule makes the question much harder to count directly — unless you know one clever trick.

This is Brick 1 of a 3-part mini-lesson. Today you learn the trick for when there is ONE way to break the rule. We go slow, one idea at a time, and every number you see gets its own reason.

🔁 First, Let’s Remind Ourselves — Choose 3 From n

In Lesson 1 you learned how to choose 3 things from a group, when the order you pick them in doesn’t matter. Let’s redo it slowly with a fresh example, and this time say out loud WHY each number is there.

Example: choose 3 people from a group of 5 (Alex, Bo, Cara, Dan, Emma).

1
5 × 4 × 3
5 is the total group size (n = 5). If order mattered, there'd be 5 choices for who you pick 1st, then 4 people left for who you pick 2nd, then 3 people left for who you pick 3rd. That's why you multiply three counting-down numbers.
5 × 4 = 20, then 20 × 3 = 60 — that's 60 different ORDERED lists of 3 people.
2
60 ÷ 6
But a GROUP doesn't care about order — "Alex, Bo, Cara" is the same group as "Cara, Alex, Bo". Every group of 3 got counted 6 times in that list of 60, because there are 3×2×1=6 different orders you could arrange any 3 people in. Dividing by 6 removes those repeats.
60 ÷ 6 = 10 different groups
Choose 3 from n  =  n × (n−1) × (n−2)  ÷  6

💡 One New Idea — Count the Part You DON'T Want

Here is the ONLY new idea in this whole brick. Forget counting groups for a second — think about something much simpler first.

A class of 20

Prem's class has 20 students. Today, 3 of them are away sick. How many students are actually in class right now?

You would NOT count all the present kids one by one. You'd just do:

✓
20 − 3 = 17
You counted the WHOLE class (20), counted the part you DON'T want — the absent 3 — and subtracted. That's it. That's the whole idea.
GOOD  =  ALL  −  BAD
(the part you want)  (everything)  (the part that breaks the rule)

Counting GROUPS works exactly the same way. The only thing that changes is HOW you count "ALL" and "BAD" — you'll use your Choose-3 rule from the last page for both of them.

Worked Example — At Least 1 Girl

A class has 4 boys and 3 girls (7 students in total). A group of 3 students is chosen to present a project. How many different groups contain AT LEAST 1 girl?

Step 1 — Count ALL (ignore the "at least 1 girl" rule completely)
1
7 × 6 × 5
7 is the TOTAL number of students (n = 7, all 4 boys + 3 girls together). 6 is one-less-than-7 (n−1) — after the 1st pick, one fewer choice remains. 5 is two-less-than-7 (n−2) — after 2 picks, two fewer choices remain. Same reasoning as the last page.
7 × 6 = 42, then 42 × 5 = 210
2
210 ÷ 6
Divide by 6 to remove the repeat-orders, exactly like last page — every group of 3 students got counted 6 times.
210 ÷ 6 = 35 groups altogether (this is ALL)
Step 2 — Figure out what BAD means

"At least 1 girl" is broken only ONE way: having ZERO girls — meaning all 3 chosen students are boys. So BAD = choose 3 from JUST the 4 boys.

1
4 × 3 × 2
4 is now the boys-only total (n = 4 — we switched pools, we're only choosing from boys now). 3 is one-less-than-4 (n−1). 2 is two-less-than-4 (n−2). Same rule, smaller n.
4 × 3 = 12, then 12 × 2 = 24
2
24 ÷ 6
Divide by 6 again, same reason as always — remove the repeat-orders.
24 ÷ 6 = 4 bad groups (all-boy)
Step 3 — Subtract
✓
35 − 4
ALL (35) minus BAD (4) leaves only the GOOD groups — the ones with at least 1 girl. Exactly like the class-of-20 example.
35 − 4 = 31 groups with at least 1 girl

⚠️ The classic mistake: thinking the opposite of "at least 1 girl" is "all girls." It is NOT. Always ask: what is the ONE situation that breaks the rule? Here, it's having no girls at all.

🔍 Watch Out — Not Every Question Needs This!

Total Minus Bad is only for questions with an EXTRA rule attached. If there's no extra rule, just use plain Choose-3 from Lesson 1 — don't subtract anything.

Question A — has an extra rule

"Choose 3 students from 7 (4 boys, 3 girls). How many groups have AT LEAST 1 girl?"

→ USE Total Minus Bad: 35 − 4 = 31

Question B — no extra rule

"Choose 3 students from 7 (4 boys, 3 girls) to present a project. How many different groups are possible?"

→ Just plain Choose-3: 7×6×5÷6 = 35. Done — no subtracting!

⚠️ The test: does the question add a rule about who MUST be included ("at least 1…") or who must be EXCLUDED ("not all the same…", "excluding…")? If yes → Total Minus Bad. If it just asks "how many groups are possible" with no extra condition → plain Choose-3, no subtracting.

⭐ Your Turn

Practice — 4 Questions

🚦 For each question, first decide: does it need Total Minus Bad, or plain Choose-3? Then work it out.

P1.  A club has 5 boys and 4 girls (9 members in total). A team of 3 is chosen. How many different teams have AT LEAST 1 girl?

ALL: choose 3 from all 9 → 9×8×7=504, 504÷6=84.
BAD (0 girls = all boys, from the 5 boys): 5×4×3=60, 60÷6=10.
GOOD: 84−10=74 teams with at least 1 girl.
A) 84 is ALL, B) 10 is BAD — both are half-finished.

P2.  A group has 6 boys and 2 girls (8 in total). A group of 3 is chosen. How many different groups have AT LEAST 1 girl?

ALL: choose 3 from all 8 → 8×7×6=336, 336÷6=56.
BAD (0 girls = all boys, from the 6 boys): 6×5×4=120, 120÷6=20.
GOOD: 56−20=36 groups with at least 1 girl.

P3.  The SAME group as P2 (6 boys and 2 girls, 8 in total) is choosing 3 students to represent the school — no other rule this time. How many different groups of 3 are possible?

No extra rule here — just plain Choose-3 from all 8: 8×7×6=336, 336÷6=56. No subtracting needed! (A) 36 wrongly subtracts anyway.

P4.  A committee of 3 is chosen from 10 people (7 men and 3 women). How many different committees have AT LEAST 1 woman?

ALL: choose 3 from all 10 → 10×9×8=720, 720÷6=120.
BAD (0 women = all men, from the 7 men): 7×6×5=210, 210÷6=35.
GOOD: 120−35=85 committees with at least 1 woman.

📖 Brick 1's Big Idea

When a question adds "at least 1…", find ALL groups, find the ONE way to be BAD (zero of that type), and subtract. If there's no extra rule, don't subtract at all — just use plain Choose-3.

GOOD = ALL − BAD

🚦 Finish-Line Check — the ALL number and the BAD number are NOT the answer. The answer is what's left AFTER subtracting.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You can find ALL using your Choose-3 rule, and say why each number is there
  • You know "at least 1 girl" is broken by ZERO girls — not "all girls"
  • You can tell when a question does NOT need subtracting at all
  • You subtract at the end, and hand in the GOOD number
