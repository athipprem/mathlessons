🎯 Why Learn This?

Real counting questions almost always have a catch

"Choose a team of 3 with at least 1 girl." "Pick 3 marbles that are NOT all the same colour." "Choose 3 points that don't lie on a straight line." Real competition questions rarely ask for a plain, unrestricted group — they add ONE extra condition that makes direct counting painful. Today you learn the trick that turns these into easy problems: instead of counting the hard way (the groups you WANT), count the EASY way (the total, minus the groups you DON'T want).

GOOD groups = ALL possible groups − BAD groups

🤝 You Already Know This Pattern

In your last unit (Probability), you learned: P(NOT happening) = 1 − P(happening). Instead of working out the hard outcome directly, you found the easy opposite and subtracted. Today's idea is the exact same trick, just for COUNTING instead of probability:

What you already know (Probability):
P(NOT rain) = 1 − P(rain)
Today's new version (Counting):
Count(GOOD) = Count(ALL) − Count(BAD)

⚠️ The only difference: probability divides to get a fraction between 0 and 1. Counting just subtracts to get a whole number of groups. Same "find the opposite" thinking, different final step.

🔍 Spotting The Signal

Watch for these key words

Use "Total minus Bad" whenever a question includes a phrase like: "at least 1…", "not all the same…", "excluding…", or "but NOT…". These all describe a GOOD group as "everything except some annoying, hard-to-directly-count exception."

ALL groups
−
BAD groups
(the exception)
=
GOOD groups
(the answer)

⚠️ The trick only saves time when "BAD" is EASIER to count directly than "GOOD" is. "At least 1 girl" is hard to count directly (could be 1, 2, or 3 girls) — but "0 girls" (all boys) is one easy calculation. That's the signal to flip to Total Minus Bad.

Worked Example — At Least 1 Girl

A class has 4 boys and 3 girls (7 students total). A group of 3 is chosen to present a project. How many different groups have AT LEAST 1 girl?

Step 1 — Total groups of 3 from all 7 students:
7 × 6 × 5 ÷ 6 = 35
Step 2 — "BAD" here means 0 girls, i.e. all 3 are boys. Count groups of 3 boys from the 4 boys:
4 × 3 × 2 ÷ 6 = 4
Step 3 — GOOD = ALL − BAD:
35 − 4 = 31 groups with at least 1 girl

⚠️ Notice: "at least 1 girl" is the OPPOSITE of "0 girls," not the opposite of "all girls." Always ask: what is the ONE simple case that breaks the "at least 1" rule? Here, that's exactly 0 girls.

Worked Example — Excluding A Bad Case

9 points are marked on a circle. Exactly 3 of these 9 points happen to lie on a straight line (so those 3 do NOT form a triangle). How many different triangles can be formed using any 3 of the 9 points?

Step 1 — Total ways to choose any 3 of the 9 points:
9 × 8 × 7 ÷ 6 = 84
Step 2 — "BAD" here is the ONE group that fails to make a triangle: the 3 collinear points chosen together. Only 1 such group exists (it's the same 3 specific points every time):
1 bad group
Step 3 — GOOD = ALL − BAD:
84 − 1 = 83 different triangles

⚠️ This is the SAME points-on-a-circle idea from Lesson 1 — but now with one exception carved out. Same total-counting method, one extra subtraction step for the exception.

🧭 The Total Minus Bad Roadmap

1Spot the signal words: "at least 1," "not all the same," "excluding," "but not."
2Work out Count(ALL) — choose r from the whole pool, ignoring the extra rule completely.
3Identify exactly what "BAD" means — the ONE simple, easy-to-count exception (usually the opposite extreme, like "0 of the special type").
4Work out Count(BAD) using the same choose method, on just the leftover/excluded pool.
5Subtract: GOOD = ALL − BAD. That's your answer.

📋 Build The Count Yourself

A bag has 5 red counters and 4 blue counters (9 total). You draw 3 counters. How many different groups of 3 have AT LEAST 1 blue counter? Tap each button in order.

9 × 8 × 7 ÷ 6 = 84
BAD = 0 blue counters = all 3 are red.
5 × 4 × 3 ÷ 6 = 10
84 − 10 = 74 groups with at least 1 blue counter.
⭐ Your Turn

Practice — Total Minus Bad

P1.  A fruit basket has 8 red apples and 3 green apples (11 total). You pick 3 fruits. How many different groups have AT LEAST 1 green apple?
Total: 11 × 10 × 9 ÷ 6 = 165
BAD (all red): 8 × 7 × 6 ÷ 6 = 56
165 − 56 = 109
P2.  A jar has 2 black, 3 white, and 4 grey marbles (9 total). You draw 3 marbles. How many different groups are NOT all the same colour?
Total: 9 × 8 × 7 ÷ 6 = 84
BAD (all same colour) — can't pick 3 black (only 2 exist, 0 ways); all white: 3×2×1÷6=1; all grey: 4×3×2÷6=4
BAD total: 0 + 1 + 4 = 5
84 − 5 = 79
📖 Today's Big Idea

When a counting question says "at least 1," "not all the same," or "excluding" — don't count the hard case directly. Count ALL, count the easy BAD exception, and subtract.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You can spot the signal words that mean "use Total Minus Bad"
  • You correctly identify what "BAD" means for each question (usually the opposite extreme)
  • You use the choose-r method from Lesson 1 for BOTH the total and the bad count
  • You remember to add up MULTIPLE bad cases when there's more than one (like 3 same-colour groups)
