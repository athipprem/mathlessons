U8 Q13 Gap Fix

Part 1: Fraction Direction — "Give Away" Problems

In U8 Q13, Tom gave away ⅓ of his stickers and had 24 left. The answer was 36, but the common mistake was writing 72 — which comes from multiplying by 3 instead of the right operation.

❌ Common Mistake

"Gave ⅓ away" → think of ⅓ → do × 3
24 × 3 = 72 ✗

✅ Correct Thinking

Gave ⅓ away → kept ⅔ → 24 = ⅔ × N
N = 24 ÷ ⅔ = 36 ✓

The key shift: When someone GIVES AWAY a fraction, ask: "What fraction did they KEEP?"

Gave away ⅓ → kept ⅔
Gave away ¼ → kept ¾
Gave away ⅖ → kept ⅗

Then work backwards: if the amount left = kept fraction × original,
then original = amount left ÷ kept fraction

Fraction Direction

Worked Examples + Practice

📘 Worked Example

Q: Tom gave away ⅓ of his stickers and had 24 left. How many did he start with?

Gave away ⅓ → kept ⅔
24 = ⅔ × original
Original = 24 ÷ ⅔ = 24 × 3/2 = 36
He started with 36 stickers ✓

Now try these. First, write what fraction they kept, then find the original.

Q1. Maya gave away ¼ of her sweets and had 30 left. How many did she start with?

Kept ¾ → 30 = ¾ × N → N = 30 ÷ ¾ = 30 × 4/3 = 40

Q2. A shop sold ⅖ of its books. 18 books remain. How many books did the shop have originally?

Sold ⅖ → kept ⅗ → 18 = ⅗ × N → N = 18 ÷ ⅗ = 18 × 5/3 = 30

Q3. ⭐ Ben spent ⅓ of his money, then spent another $8 on lunch. He had $16 left. How much did he start with?

After lunch: 16+8=24. Kept ⅔ → 24 = ⅔ × N → N = 24 × 3/2 = 36

Part 2: Both-Ends Permutations

In U8 Q7: 8 students in a row, two specific ones (Maya and Leo) must be at the two ends. How many arrangements?

The mistake: treating it like a single-end fix and counting 5! = 120, or forgetting that the two end people can also swap positions.

Method — Both ends fixed:
Step 1: Arrange the 2 end people → 2! = 2 ways (Maya left + Leo right, OR Leo left + Maya right)
Step 2: Arrange the remaining (n−2) middle people → (n−2)!
Step 3: Multiply → Total = 2 × (n−2)!

For 8 students: 2 × 6! = 2 × 720 = 1440

Both-Ends Practice

Practice: Both-Ends Permutations

Q4. 6 people stand in a row. Anna and Ben must be at the two ends. How many arrangements?

2 × (6−2)! = 2 × 4! = 2 × 24 = 48

Q5. 7 children stand in a row for a photo. The two tallest must stand at both ends. How many ways can all 7 be arranged?

2 × (7−2)! = 2 × 5! = 2 × 120 = 240

Q6. ⭐ 5 students in a row. Student A must be at the LEFT end. Student B must be at the RIGHT end. (They cannot swap.) How many arrangements?

1 way for ends (A fixed left, B fixed right) × 3! middle = 6
Note: When the question says each person is fixed at a SPECIFIC end (not "either end"), use 1 × (n−2)! not 2 × (n−2)!. If they can be at EITHER end, use 2 × (n−2)!.
🏆 Done!

Two Gap Fixes — Locked In

Fraction Direction:
"Gave away X" → ask "What fraction was KEPT?" → kept = (1 − X)
Amount left = kept × original → original = amount left ÷ kept fraction
Both-Ends Permutations:
Two specific people at EITHER end → 2 × (n−2)!
Each person at a SPECIFIC end (no swap) → 1 × (n−2)!

These two error types are common in ISA and competition problems. Now you have the framework — the question is just deciding which version applies.
