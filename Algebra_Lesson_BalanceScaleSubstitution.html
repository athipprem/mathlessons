🔍 One Clue Isn't Always Enough

Why learn this?

In Lesson 1, cancelling turned ONE mixed scale into a clean ratio like "1 apple = 2 bananas." But some puzzles give you a ratio clue AND a separate clue about a TOTAL weight — and the total weight clue still has BOTH items mixed in it, with nothing to cancel. The fix: use the first clue to REPLACE one item with the other everywhere it appears, so the second clue turns into a same-item problem you already know how to solve.

Two clues, one link between them — swap one item for the other, then solve.

The Big Idea — Substitution

If Clue 1 tells you "1 apple = 2 bananas," then ANYWHERE you see "1 apple" in Clue 2, you can cross it out and write "2 bananas" instead — the weight doesn't change, only how you describe it.

Substitute using Clue 1 → combine everything into ONE item → divide the total weight by the count → multiply back if you need the OTHER item's weight.

⚠️ Substitute EVERY copy of the item, not just one. If Clue 2 has "2 apples," that becomes 2 × (Clue 1's banana amount), not just 1 copy.

Worked Example 1

Clue 1: 1 apple = 2 bananas. Clue 2: 1 apple + 3 bananas = 20 grams. How much does 1 apple weigh?

Step 1 — substitute Clue 1 into Clue 2. Replace "1 apple" with "2 bananas":
1 apple + 3 bananas → 2 bananas + 3 bananas
Step 2 — combine the bananas into one count:
2 bananas + 3 bananas = 5 bananas
Step 3 — the 5 bananas weigh 20 grams together. Divide to find ONE banana:
20 ÷ 5 = 4 grams  →  1 banana = 4 grams
Step 4 — use Clue 1 to find 1 apple (multiply back):
1 apple = 2 bananas = 2 × 4 = 8 grams
1 apple = 8 grams

Worked Example 1 — Reveal Step by Step

Tap each button in order. Watch Clue 2 transform into a solvable one-item equation.

Clue 1: 1 apple = 2 bananas
Clue 2: 1 apple + 3 bananas = 20 grams
✅ 1 apple + 3 bananas → 2 bananas + 3 bananas
✅ 2 bananas + 3 bananas = 5 bananas = 20 grams
✅ 20 ÷ 5 = 4 grams → 1 banana = 4 grams
✅ 1 apple = 2 × 4 = 8 grams

Worked Example 2 — When Clue 1 Isn't Given Directly

On a balance scale, the left pan holds 2 oranges + 1 grape. The right pan holds 1 orange + 4 grapes. The scale balances. Also: 2 oranges + 1 grape = 21 grams. How much does 1 orange weigh?

Step 1 — Clue 1 isn't given directly this time. Use Lesson 1's cancel trick on the balance first:
Cancel 1 orange from both sides: Left → 1 orange+1 grape. Right → 4 grapes.
Cancel 1 grape from both sides: Left → 1 orange. Right → 3 grapes.
Clue 1 found: 1 orange = 3 grapes
Step 2 — substitute into the weight clue. Replace "2 oranges" with "2 × 3 grapes":
2 oranges + 1 grape → 6 grapes + 1 grape
Step 3 — combine:
6 grapes + 1 grape = 7 grapes = 21 grams
Step 4 — divide to find 1 grape:
21 ÷ 7 = 3 grams  →  1 grape = 3 grams
Step 5 — multiply back using Clue 1 to find 1 orange:
1 orange = 3 grapes = 3 × 3 = 9 grams
1 orange = 9 grams

Worked Example 2 — Reveal Step by Step

This one has 5 steps — cancel first to FIND Clue 1, then substitute like before. Tap each button in order.

Balance: 2 oranges+1 grape  =  1 orange+4 grapes
Weight clue: 2 oranges + 1 grape = 21 grams
✅ Cancel 1 orange, then 1 grape → Clue 1: 1 orange = 3 grapes
✅ 2 oranges + 1 grape → (2×3 grapes) + 1 grape = 6 grapes + 1 grape
✅ 6 grapes + 1 grape = 7 grapes = 21 grams
✅ 21 ÷ 7 = 3 grams → 1 grape = 3 grams
✅ 1 orange = 3 grapes = 3 × 3 = 9 grams

🗺️ The Plan

1If Clue 1 (the ratio) isn't given directly, find it first using Lesson 1's cancel trick on a raw balance picture.
2Look at Clue 2. Find every place the FIRST item appears in it.
3SUBSTITUTE: replace every copy of that item with its equivalent from Clue 1.
4Combine everything into ONE running count of the second item.
5Divide the total weight by that count → weight of ONE of the second item.
6Multiply back using Clue 1's ratio → weight of ONE of the first item.

⚠️ Substitute the FULL amount every time the item appears — if it appears twice, that's 2 × the swap, not 1 ×.

📖 Substitution Chains — in one line

Find Clue 1 (cancel if needed) → substitute it into Clue 2 → combine → divide → multiply back.

Two clues are just one extra swap away from a problem you already know how to solve.

🏆 You've got it when…

  • You can spot which item to substitute, and swap EVERY copy of it
  • You divide the total by the combined count to get ONE item's weight
  • You remember to multiply back if the question asks about the OTHER item

You now have both balance-scale tools: cancelling and substitution. Ready for the test!
