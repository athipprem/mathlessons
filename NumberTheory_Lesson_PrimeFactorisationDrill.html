The Method

Factor Tree — the 3-step habit

Every time you factorise, do exactly these three steps. Same order, every time.

1
Draw the number at the top. Circle it.
2
Split into two factors (one should be prime if possible). Branch down. Circle the primes — they're done.
3
Keep splitting until every branch ends in a prime. Then write the answer as a product: 2³ × 3 etc.
Prime numbers to know cold: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 — these cannot be split. When you reach one, circle it and stop.
Worked Example

Factorise 36

Watch the tree grow step by step:

36 = 2² × 3²

Collect the primes: two 2s → 2²  |  two 3s → 3²  |  Multiply: 2² × 3²

Practice 1 — Warm Up

Factorise 12

Draw the tree on paper first. Then type the answer as a product of primes.

Hint: Start by asking — is 12 divisible by 2? Yes. So split: 12 = 2 × 6. Now split 6...
12 = 2² × 3

Tree: 12 → 2 × 6 → 2 × (2 × 3). Primes at the bottom: 2, 2, 3. Collect: 2² × 3 ✓

Practice 2 — Medium

Factorise 48

Draw the tree. Keep splitting until every branch is prime.

Tip: 48 = 2 × 24. Keep splitting the 24...

48 = 2⁴ × 3

48 = 2 × 24 = 2 × 2 × 12 = 2 × 2 × 4 × 3 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 = 2⁴ × 3

Building the Reflex

These words = draw a factor tree immediately

The reason Q6 and Q7 were blank is that the question didn't say "use LCM" or "use prime factorisation" — it just showed a number and asked a direct question. Your brain didn't fire the right tool.

From now on, whenever you see any of these words or patterns, your hand starts drawing a tree before you even think:

🔑 Auto-trigger words
"prime factors" "factorise" "express as a product of primes" "prime factorisation of" "write [number] as..." a number ≥ 12 with no other instruction
The reflex habit:
See trigger word → Write the number at the top of your page → Draw two branches → Start splitting.

Don't wait to "think about it." Start the tree first, think while drawing.

Challenge — Competition Style

Express 72 as a product of prime factors.

No hints. Draw your tree on paper, then type the answer in the form 2a × 3b.

72 = 2³ × 3²

72 → 2 × 36 → 2 × 4 × 9 → 2 × 2² × 3² = 2³ × 3²

Or: 72 → 8 × 9 → 2³ × 3² — same result either way.
