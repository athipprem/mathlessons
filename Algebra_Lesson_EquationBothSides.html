n on BOTH sides — a new problem 🤔

In Lesson 1, all the n's were on the left. Now they appear on both sides:

6n + 3 = 2n + 35

There are n's on the left AND on the right. You can't use the 3-step method directly because you have two separate n groups.

The solution: sort the equation first. Move all the blue n's to the LEFT and all the red numbers to the RIGHT. Then it becomes a normal equation you can solve.

← n's go here
6n − 2n = 4n
=
numbers go here →
35 − 3 = 32

So 4n = 32 → n = 8. Check: 6×8+3 = 51 = 2×8+35 = 51 ✓

The sign flip rule 🔄

When a term crosses the = sign, its sign flips: +2n becomes −2n, and −4 becomes +4.

Why? Because you're subtracting it from both sides. Subtracting removes it from one side and "adds the opposite" to the other.

Crossing the = sign: sign FLIPS
6n + 3 = 2n + 35
Subtract 2n from both sides → 2n crosses and becomes −2n:
6n − 2n + 3 = 35
Subtract 3 from both sides → 3 crosses and becomes −3:
4n = 35 − 3 = 32
n = 8
The 4-Step Method
Step 1: Expand any brackets
Step 2: Move all n-terms to the LEFT (subtract the smaller n group)
Step 3: Move all numbers to the RIGHT (add/subtract to cross the =)
Step 4: Divide both sides to find n
Always check: substitute n back in — both sides must be equal
✅ Decision Guide — ask yourself this for every term:
Is it an n-term? → It belongs on the LEFT. If it's on the right, subtract it from both sides.
Is it a number? → It belongs on the RIGHT. If it's on the left, subtract it from both sides.
When you subtract a term and it crosses the = sign, its sign flips:
+3n on the right → subtract 3n → it becomes −3n on the left → n-terms combine
+8 on the left → subtract 8 → it becomes −8 on the right → numbers combine
